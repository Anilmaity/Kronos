So, here we are with our first example, and before we begin, it is important that you understand that you can only do this with correlated assets. So, for example, here is the S&amp;P 500, here is the NASDAQ. These are correlated assets, and they should have the same structure, but sometimes they do not. So, here you can see, if we reference the same high right here, what do we notice? One asset has taken that out, while the other hasn't. So, we can go ahead and consider that an SMT, or a divergence between the assets, as NASDAQ has made a lower high, while the S&amp;P has made a higher high. So, by using that, we can determine which is stronger and which is weaker. The NASDAQ is weaker, as it is making the lower high. The S&amp;P is stronger, as it is making the higher high. So, that is how you use SMT to determine relative strength or weakness.
