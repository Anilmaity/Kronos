These are unscripted New York-open streams. The teaching is reactive: he narrates a live
bias, then defends it against chat. That makes them the richest source in the corpus for
**exceptions, invalidations and "when I do NOT trade"** — which is where the notes below
concentrate. All four streams follow the same shape: pre-open asset sweep → one (and only
one) framed idea → 9:30 open → 10:00 a.m. decision point → wind-down by ~10:30.

## New York Open Live Q&A  (aqnY1yZvfYs, https://youtu.be/aqnY1yZvfYs, 5262s)

### What is actually taught

The core of this stream is **how to hold a single directional idea and refuse everything
else**. He comes in bullish on the indices off a daily candle that is an *inside day* — a
case where most people would say bias is undefined. His answer: on an inside day you stop
asking whether price leaves the prior range and instead read the **internal formation of
the developing candle** (opened, traded lower first, closed back up = bullish formation),
plus the lower-timeframe state inside that daily candle (no bearish CISD present). That
gives a bias without needing a range break.

He then picks the vehicle by **relative strength**: YM strongest, NQ weakest, ES the
middle asset. He only frames longs on the *strongest* asset, refuses the lagging one
outright, and tells anyone who struggles with the strength read to just default to ES,
because the middle asset "does what actually is happening" while the outer two decouple.
He also demonstrates that strength **flips intraday**, and that the flip typically occurs
at a higher low — and that when it flips he still will not switch into the newly-weak
asset, because he would rather trade the one that was strong from the start.

Second major theme: **relevant highs and lows require "separation."** Two highs a few
points apart collapse into one level; two highs with meaningful vertical distance are two
independent targets. He is explicit that he has *no mechanical rule* for how much
separation counts — repeated twice under direct questioning.

Third theme: **time decay of opportunity**. The further into the New York session, the
less he wants to trade, because a daily candle that spent the morning ranging has less
time left to trend. He states his personal window is roughly 8:00–10:30 a.m., and when the
10:00 a.m. candle came and the daily formation had gone open-low-high-low (i.e. not a
clean power-of-three shape), he declared the whole day uninteresting even though a
mechanically valid short setup was printing.

Fourth: **the short-term-target-already-taken invalidation**. A textbook-valid continuation
whose target has already been reached is not tradeable without extra confirmation, because
a new phase of price can begin there. He pulls up his own "Why your continuations fail"
lesson live to make the point.

The back half is a long open-mic video wish-list, which is useful as a map of what he
considers unsolved: previous-day-high-and-low-both-taken-before-New-York, when *not* to
trade, trailing stops/pyramiding, invalidation of daily bias, and "why I don't use
killzones anymore."

### Q&A extractions

**Q:** How can you have a bias when today is an inside day?
**A:** Because the bias is not "does it leave the range." He reads the developing candle's
own formation — it closed weak on Monday so he does not expect a range break, but the
individual candle formation (open, trade lower, close up) is bullish, so he frames higher.

**Q:** When should I renew / re-mark the C1 candle?
**A:** You never actually focus on C1. C1 is arbitrary — it is simply whatever precedes
C2. Only C2 matters.

**Q:** What if C2 does not make a CISD?
**A:** Then you require a V-shape instead. And even with a V-shape you still need a
continuation afterwards — a V-shape alone is not an entry.

**Q:** Would you have taken the 15-minute short on NQ that just played out?
**A:** It was a valid fractal-model pattern, but it did not align with his bias, so no. "I
can only frame to the upside today. If I'm wrong, I'm wrong."

**Q:** Why weren't you interested in NQ on the long side?
**A:** Because NQ was the weakest asset. He does not trade lagging assets. Same idea
executed on YM went straight to target in two minutes; on NQ it chopped.

**Q:** How do you know how much separation between two highs/lows is enough for both to
matter?
**A:** There is no mechanical rule and no percentage — it changes across timeframes. You
have to look at the chart and give it screen time. (Asked twice; same answer both times.)

**Q:** An SMT formed on ES/NQ at the London high and it failed — why?
**A:** Because you are hunting SMT and then inventing a reason to trade. You must enter the
day with a higher-timeframe bias first; SMT is a confirmation, not a trigger. He was bullish,
so a failing bearish SMT was simply irrelevant to him.

**Q:** Wouldn't you want to see a higher low on the 4-hour before trusting trend direction?
**A:** Not at the open — the open supplies the volatility to just go. Waiting four hours
for a higher low means entering too late. After the open has run, *then* a formed 4-hour
structure can set up the 10:00 a.m. move.

**Q:** Do you use the midnight open / settlement?
**A:** No midnight open. Settlement is turned off; back-adjustment is on.

**Q:** Do you mainly trade 9:30–10:00?
**A:** Roughly 8:00 a.m. to 10:30 a.m., depending on when he is up.

**Q:** Do you scalp before FOMC news?
**A:** No — he does not sit and scalp ahead of the Fed rate announcement.

**Q:** Will you make an OTE + fractal model video?
**A:** No, and he explains why: his whole model rests on price *not* retracing deeply
during expansion. If price reaches OTE he is no longer looking for an entry in that
direction.

**Q:** Why don't you trade consolidations?
**A:** You can, but trading a consolidation means fading the breakout back across the
range. He prefers continuations and expansion. Also: do not trade *after* the expansion —
you want to be trading the expansion itself.

**Q:** If you don't have a weekly bias, how do you run weekly profiles?
**A:** Weekly profile is not weekly bias. He has only a daily bias; the weekly profile is
using what the previous day did to anticipate the next day, chained across the week (e.g.
Tuesday reversal → Wednesday continuation). His model already encodes this as C2 → C3 → C4,
and it deliberately does not trade C5.

**Q:** What if candle 3 does not close above/below the open of candle 2?
**A:** Then it is nothing — no setup.

**Q:** Where do I start learning?
**A:** ttrades.com education centre → Foundation playlist → then the Fractal Model
playlist, in that order.

**Q:** Can everyone be profitable?
**A:** No. Trading is zero-sum in the sense that for you to win on a position someone must
have taken the other side.

**Q:** How do you approach the day when both previous-day high *and* low are taken before
New York?
**A:** He does not answer — he flags it as a genuinely good question and adds it to his
future-video list. (Open problem.)

**Q:** Turtle soup video?
**A:** Sure, but "we're going to call it just a liquidity sweep. It's literally just that."

**Q:** How to trade model 1 of CRT?
**A:** He rejects the framing — says CRT is his own models renamed, and that he does not
use CRT terminology.

### Concepts introduced or clarified (his terminology)

- **C1 is arbitrary** — only C2 is analysed.
- **V-shape requirement** — the substitute confirmation when C2 produced no CISD.
- **Separation** — the (admittedly non-mechanical) vertical distance that makes two swing
  levels count as two targets rather than one.
- **Relative strength triad** — YM / ES / NQ, ES structurally in the middle; chart layout
  literally arranged ES-centre with YM and NQ either side.
- **T spot** — the level on the reversal candle that must hold (used loosely here for the
  0.5-of-wick / opposing-candle midpoint zone).
- **Short-term targets taken** — the named condition in his "Why your continuations fail"
  lesson requiring additional confirmation.
- **Failure swings** — minor lows he does not trust to trade away from or place stops behind.

### Rules / conditions stated

- Only one direction is framed per day; a valid pattern in the opposite direction is not
  taken.
- Trade the strongest asset for longs / weakest for shorts; never the lagging asset.
- If relative strength is unclear, default to ES (the middle asset).
- Two swing levels are separate targets only if there is visible separation between them;
  otherwise treat as one.
- A continuation setup whose short-term target has already been hit requires additional
  confirmation or is skipped.
- C2 with no CISD → require a V-shape, plus a subsequent continuation.
- C3 that fails to close beyond the open of C2 → no setup.
- The model trades C2 → C3 → C4; it does not trade C5.
- Trading window ≈ 08:00–10:30 ET; interest declines monotonically through the session.
- Do not enter after the expansion has already happened.
- Settlement off, back-adjust on.

### Quotes

> "you never actually focus on C1. You only focus on C2"

> "You don't want to trade after the expansion."

### Open questions / ambiguities

- "Separation" is explicitly non-mechanical — the single largest gap for automation.
- The inside-day bias read mixes a daily-candle-formation judgement with an unstated
  lower-timeframe CISD check; the exact LTF timeframe is never named.
- The "V-shape" substitute for CISD is asserted but never geometrically defined.
- He says the daily formation "open, low, high, low" is not clean, but does not define the
  threshold at which a shape stops being a valid power-of-three.

## New York Open Live Q&A  (Qv6Ux_Z8VrA, https://youtu.be/Qv6Ux_Z8VrA, 6981s)

### What is actually taught

The longest and most doctrinally dense of the four. The central lesson is the
**continuation-only doctrine**: he states plainly that he is always and only seeking a
continuation, that reduced to its simplest form his model is "waiting for a lower high if
bearish and a higher low if bullish," and that reversal entries are where he lost money
early on — trading a reversal with a stop on the extreme gave him no confirmation that
price would actually go.

He introduces (unusually, as an aside) two named private-ish concepts. First, a warning
case: when **C2 is simultaneously the reversal candle and the expansion candle**, the two
roles blend and the following candle sets up at lower probability — often producing a new
phase of price rather than a clean C3 expansion. Second, **IC-CISD (intra-candle change in
state of delivery)**, which he says is what forms the wick of a higher-timeframe candle;
he notes it needs a point of interest, and that if there is no FVG or high/low available
the CISD itself can serve as the level. He compares it to a "unicorn"-style formation but
cleaner.

He teaches the **bias/confirmation trade-off** explicitly and draws it: as conviction in
the higher-timeframe bias rises, the amount of lower-timeframe confirmation required
falls, and vice versa. On this day his bias was weak, so he demanded a specific closure
below a level, never got it, and therefore took nothing — while the idea itself turned out
directionally right. He frames that as the point: "the idea is still valid technically,
but the lower timeframe price action didn't allow me to get on side."

**Retracement depth as an invalidator** is the other big idea. In expansion, retracements
are shallow. A deep retracement — specifically one that reaches the OTE of the impulse leg
— is not a retracement at all; it is a V-shape reversal, and it flips the read. He calls
out that many traders wait for exactly that OTE retracement and that he does not use it.

He also gives a clean **relative-strength procedure**: three inputs only — SMT, candle
closures, and structure — walked through live on his own website's lesson page. And a
**prop-account sizing rule**: divide the drawdown by the number of losing trades you are
willing to survive; that quotient is your per-trade risk.

Notable execution details: he takes the entry **on the closure through the level and does
not wait for a retest** of the resulting order block; he will size *down* when entering
near the open with a wide-ish structure, deliberately expecting to be stopped; he does not
care which timeframe the entry comes from (H1, M30, M15, M3 all acceptable) provided there
is a daily framework and a higher-timeframe wick to trade away from; and for overnight
work he associates Asia with H1 entries and London with M15, while stressing the timeframe
itself does not matter.

There is also a substantial psychology block: on hostile chat ("they are struggling and
trying to feel better"), on why he does not display wealth, and on the fact that asking
"where are my targets" after entering proves you are trading someone else's idea.

### Q&A extractions

**Q:** Why aren't you shorting right now if your bias is bearish?
**A:** Because there is no valid framework at this moment: the 4-hour is deep into its
range (not near the open), the hourly is already at candle 6, and the 30-minute offers no
swing to trade away from. You cannot force it.

**Q:** Doesn't trading a continuation mean entering later / worse price?
**A:** Yes, but with a higher win rate.

**Q:** Your model's stop is too big compared to other models.
**A:** If you think the stop in his model is huge, you likely do not understand the model.
(No further concession.)

**Q:** I keep missing entries because price never retests the order block after CISD.
**A:** He does not look for a retest after a closure. He takes the entry when price closes
through.

**Q:** How do you determine so quickly which index is weakest?
**A:** Three things, in order: SMT (which asset failed to take the level), then candle
closures on lower timeframes, then structure. Nothing else.

**Q:** DXY and euro have SMT — should I trade it?
**A:** You can. He personally keeps all his currency analysis on the dollar and works
outward from there.

**Q:** How do you execute fast enough in fast price action?
**A:** If entries stress you, the root cause is that your timeframe is too low. Move up —
M1/M3 → M5 → M15 → H1. Identify the problem, then the solution.

**Q:** Is entry timing important?
**A:** Timing matters *within* the higher-timeframe candle, not by clock. You must wait
until the candle's wick has formed; entering before the wick forms puts you in drawdown.
Once the wick is in, where exactly you enter matters much less.

**Q:** When you trade overnight, H1/daily or M15/H4?
**A:** Either. As a rough association: Asia tends to be H1 entries, London M15 — but it
does not matter as long as there is a higher-timeframe wick to trade away from.

**Q:** I got a 25K flex account — how should I plan risk?
**A:** Take the drawdown figure (e.g. $1,500), decide how many losing trades you are willing
to take before the account is gone (e.g. 10), divide. That is your per-trade risk ($150).

**Q:** If there is a C2 closure on the hourly on indices, should I expect C3 continuation?
**A:** That is generally ideal *if* the C2 closure is good and it aligns with your
higher-timeframe bias.

**Q:** H4 and H1 both show C2 closures — why are you still bearish?
**A:** Because the goal of a C2 closure is *price opens, makes a low, and reverses*. On the
4-hour price opened, traded up, then continued — that is not a reversal candle, so he does
not count it. He does not flip-flop bias off a shape alone.

**Q:** Why isn't the V-shape low on NQ valid?
**A:** Because it did not occur off anything relevant. Without a relevant level to reverse
from, the move is "fake" to him and gives no upside framework.

**Q:** Do you take countertrend / retracement trades against your bias?
**A:** Not really. The only countertrend trading he does is trading the *invalidation* of
his bias (flagged as an upcoming video).

**Q:** Do you use order flow / TPO / footprint?
**A:** No — none of it.

**Q:** How often do you trade / how many trades a week?
**A:** It varies week to week; some weeks none. He notes he has barely traded since
October/November because the index price action has been range-bound with no trend.

**Q:** Is trading clean right now?
**A:** No. He shows months of ranging and says there is zero trend; there are setups
inside it, but he has largely sat out.

**Q:** How do you square streaming a bias with not giving signals?
**A:** He names the level and the shape he wants, but never announces entries. If you have
to ask him where your targets are after entering, you are not trading your own plan.

**Q:** What is the difference between YouTube and your paid course?
**A:** The information is close to identical; the course sells structure (A-to-Z ordering,
a flowchart of the model) plus Discord access. "Everything in life is time or money."

### Concepts introduced or clarified (his terminology)

- **Continuation-only doctrine** — always seeking a continuation, never a reversal entry.
- **Blended reversal+expansion C2** — when C2 is also the expansion candle, the next candle
  is lower-probability and often starts a new phase of price.
- **IC-CISD (intra-candle change in state of delivery)** — the mechanism that forms the
  higher-timeframe candle's wick; needs a POI, or the CISD level itself if none exists.
- **Bias ↔ confirmation trade-off** — stronger bias requires less confirmation.
- **Deep retracement = V-shape reversal** — reaching OTE of the leg invalidates the
  expansion read.
- **Framework to trade away from** — his precondition for any entry: a formed
  higher-timeframe wick / swing.
- **Consolidation sweep ambiguity** — after a range forms, a sweep of the range low may
  simply be liquidity collection to run the other way; a closure through it is not
  automatically continuation.

### Rules / conditions stated

- Enter only after the higher-timeframe candle's wick has formed; before that you are in
  drawdown by construction.
- Enter around the *opening price* of the higher-timeframe candle, not deep into its body.
- Do not wait for a retest after the closure through the level — the closure is the entry.
- A C2 closure only counts if the candle actually opened, made its extreme, and reversed.
- A reversal must occur at a *relevant level* (previous day high/low, daily level) or it
  does not create a framework.
- Retracement into the OTE of the impulse leg invalidates the continuation read.
- If bias is weak, demand the specific confirming closure; if it does not come, do not
  trade — even if the idea proves right.
- Per-trade risk = account drawdown ÷ number of losses you accept before blowing it.
- If entries feel rushed or stops keep getting swept, move up a timeframe.

### Quotes

> "waiting for a lower high if bearish and a higher low if bullish"

> "The more my bias increases, right? The less confirmation I need."

### Open questions / ambiguities

- "Relevant level" is enumerated by example (previous day high/low, daily swing lows,
  equal lows within a day's range) but never defined as a closed set.
- IC-CISD is described only in passing; no detection rule is given in this stream.
- "Deep retracement" is anchored to OTE of the leg in one breath and to "lower half of the
  gap" in another — two different measurements for the same invalidation.
- The blended reversal+expansion C2 warning has no stated threshold for how much expansion
  makes C2 "also" an expansion candle.

## New York Open Live Q&A  (yud7TpE2AMs, https://youtu.be/yud7TpE2AMs, 4635s)

### What is actually taught

He wakes late, misses the open, and turns that into the lesson: **what to do when the
move has already happened.** His answer is a two-step decision: (1) do nothing, or (2)
check whether any drawn-on-liquidity objective is still open — and if one is, take the
setup with *lowered expectations*, on a lower timeframe, accepting it is now a
lower-probability trade. He works this live: gold and oil had already expanded so both are
discarded; only YM still had an untouched previous-day low, so YM is the entire day.

The strongest new material is **expansion-sequence quality**. You want expansion followed
by retracement or consolidation. Expansion met with expansion met with expansion is a
warning: the sequence is degenerating into consolidation, and the setup quality drops. He
uses this to explain why a mechanically valid continuation looked bad to him.

He gives the clearest statement in the unit of the **protected swing** and its lifecycle:
once a closure/continuation prints, the swing behind it is protected, the stop moves
there, and it should not be touched until the target is reached. Then the genuine edge
case, which he says he rarely discusses publicly: **after the target is hit, price
frequently runs out the first protected swing, and that run-out is where he finds the next
entry.** He shows it happening live on YM.

He also names **framework transfer** — importing the framework from NQ/ES onto another
asset and treating a candle as C2 even when it is not structurally C2 on that chart — used
to explain a 7:00 a.m. expansion candle on YM.

Other substantive material: an eyeball method for **average daily range** (scan recent
expansion candles: ~700 points on YM; then ask whether the previous-day level sits inside
that range); the note that FOMC-type days extend the achievable range; a repeat of the
**entry-location rule** (only at the reversal point and at the continuation, never after
several short-term lows have already been taken); and a clean statement of **what a
protected swing lets you do with a stop.**

Notably he also handles a live failure: his anticipated continuation ran the low and came
back into the range, and he says plainly that a 1-minute reversal does not delete the
higher-timeframe idea — you decide whether you still want the idea, you do not
automatically abandon it.

There is a longer digression on his **actual toolkit**, delivered as a list: highs and
lows, fair value gaps, CISD, with SMT as the confluence. He explicitly disclaims rejection
blocks, breaker blocks, OTE/fib, silver bullet, breakaway gaps, order flow and
fundamentals.

### Q&A extractions

**Q:** How do you calculate average daily range?
**A:** No formula. Look at the daily chart, eyeball recent expansion candles (700, 700,
800…), take that as the expected range, and then check whether your intended target sits
inside it. High-volatility events (FOMC) allow more.

**Q:** What do you do if the daily move already happened before the open?
**A:** Two options. One: nothing, avoid it. Two: check for open draw on liquidity — if a
previous-day high/low is still untouched, you can take a lower-probability continuation
toward it on a lower timeframe, with reduced expectations.

**Q:** Your indicator sometimes prints bullish and bearish T-spots on top of each other —
shouldn't it be one or the other?
**A:** It happens when a single candle swept both sides of the range and produced a change
in state of delivery on both sides. Both patterns are genuinely there — which is exactly
why you must not pattern-trade. You need the higher-timeframe idea to pick one.

**Q:** How do you confirm a higher-of-day or lower-of-day?
**A:** Either a change in state of delivery, or a candle closure. With SMT off the previous
day high plus a directional daily read, he takes the high as confirmed and expects at
minimum a run to the previous day low before any reversal.

**Q:** How do you identify which asset gained strength?
**A:** Three inputs again — SMT at the level, separation (which asset is closest to the
target is the weakest for shorts), and structure. On this day: SMT on previous day high,
YM closest to the low, therefore YM weakest, therefore YM is the vehicle.

**Q:** Why did YM look weakest today when yesterday it was strongest?
**A:** Because it is not fixed. Strength is assessed fresh each day, *after* the
directional bias is formed, not before.

**Q:** Why didn't you wait for a wick on the 4-hour before trusting the move lower?
**A:** Because the retracement had already completed before 10:00 a.m. — price did not need
to build another retracement inside the new 4-hour candle in order to expand.

**Q:** My bias is right but my stop keeps getting swept before price runs to target.
**A:** The cause is almost always entering on M1/M3 without the higher-timeframe opening
context. Fix: take entries on M5 or M15 instead.

**Q:** Why did you choose that particular level to expect the reaction from?
**A:** Because the prior candle closed below the level, which says the high should remain;
if the high remains, 50% of it should be respected; 50% of the bodies lined up with a
volume imbalance, so he used the volume imbalance. And the stop had to go above the swing
because everything below it was failure swings.

**Q:** Do you need your indicator to trade the model?
**A:** No. He marked it manually for about a year — the indicator only saves time.

**Q:** How many trades per week?
**A:** Low frequency; one trade a day maximum.

**Q:** Do you use rejection blocks / breakaway gaps / silver bullet / OTE / fib?
**A:** No to all of them. His chart shows highs and lows, fair value gaps and CISD, with
SMT as confluence, and nothing else.

**Q:** What is a "stop raid"?
**A:** Deflates the term — it is just price taking out a high to go lower or a low to go
higher. Nothing fancy.

**Q:** Do you trade German indices / crypto / single stocks?
**A:** No to German indices. Bitcoin is tradeable but he sticks to futures and indices
because that is what he knows. Single stocks give better one-sided moves but less
information to act on; he swings options on a higher timeframe instead.

**Q:** Is there a peculiarity per asset class?
**A:** Yes — indices move differently from oil; each instrument has its own signature you
have to learn.

**Q:** Why was there no SMT on the 10:00 a.m. candle?
**A:** There was an SMT *prior* to 10:00 a.m., and that is sufficient — you do not
necessarily need SMT on the 10:00 a.m. candle itself.

### Concepts introduced or clarified (his terminology)

- **Expansion met with expansion met with expansion** — the degeneration signature; healthy
  sequences interleave retracement or consolidation.
- **Protected swing run-out after target** — the post-target liquidity run that becomes his
  preferred re-entry.
- **Framework transfer** — importing another asset's C2/C3 framework onto the chart being
  traded.
- **Failure swings** — minor lows/highs that cannot support a stop; you must go up to the
  real swing.
- **Open draw on liquidity** — the surviving objective that makes a late, degraded setup
  still worth a lowered-expectation trade.
- **Framework transfer + 0.5 of the wick** — the recurring midpoint reference for where a
  reversal must hold.

### Rules / conditions stated

- If price already expanded pre-open: trade only if a drawn-on-liquidity objective remains
  open, and lower your expectations / size of ambition.
- A valid sequence is expansion → retracement/consolidation → expansion. Three consecutive
  expansions without an interleaved retracement degrades the setup.
- Once a continuation closure prints, the swing behind it is protected; move the stop
  there; the protected swing should not be touched before the target is reached.
- After the target is hit, expect the first protected swing to be run out; that run-out is
  a preferred entry location.
- Do not place stops behind failure swings — go up to the swing point that actually swept
  liquidity.
- Target must sit inside the eyeballed average daily range (≈700 points on YM at the time).
- Entry belongs at the reversal point or the first continuation, not after several
  short-term lows have already been taken.
- SMT prior to the decision candle counts; it need not print on the decision candle itself.
- A 1-minute reversal does not by itself invalidate a higher-timeframe idea.

### Quotes

> "You don't really want to see the expansion met with expansion"

> "I prefer not to trade the lagging or middle assets"

### Open questions / ambiguities

- "Lower your expectations" for the late/degraded setup is not quantified (no target or
  size adjustment given).
- 50% of the *wick* and 50% of the *bodies* are both used within minutes for the same
  decision; which applies when is not stated. (See also the contested EQ note below.)
- The post-target protected-swing run-out is described as frequent but no frequency,
  timeframe or invalidation is given — he says he deliberately avoids teaching it in real
  time.
- Framework transfer is named but the conditions under which it is legitimate are not
  specified.

## New York Open Live Q&A  (00iZXPAdR5A, https://youtu.be/00iZXPAdR5A, 3230s)

### What is actually taught

The most valuable single item in the whole unit: he states, unprompted and repeatedly,
**his A+ setup — the one he would trade if restricted to one setup forever**. It is a
nested triple continuation: a **daily candle 3** (i.e. the day after a daily C2 closure),
**London forms the reversal / the low of day**, and **New York continues** — executed on a
15-minute chart. He says the reason it is A+ is that you are trading a continuation inside
a larger continuation: the intraday continuation of a London reversal, itself a
continuation of the previous day's daily move, with a protected swing confirming the wick.
He points to a free blog article ("TTrades favourite fractal model") as the written version.

The second major item is the **point-of-interest exception**, which directly contradicts
the simplified rule he teaches elsewhere: asked whether an H4 C2 must be sitting in a point
of interest, he says **no for a continuation, yes 100% for a reversal** — and admits that
"for simplicity's sake I just say yes" in public material. He repeats it later: on a daily
continuation you can skip the POI because the daily profile itself supplies the extra
confirmation.

Third: the **entry-location principle** stated in its cleanest form. If you want to trade
expansion, you let the wick form and trade the body — and you can only trade the body if
you enter around the opening price of the higher-timeframe candle. Therefore entering after
the candle has already produced its average range is a chase. He applies it live to refuse
a long above the highs.

Fourth: **time-based exit.** Because the move was riding a 4-hour expansion candle, and
expansion candles close at or near their extreme, holding to the 4-hour close is a valid
exit. He watches the candle close on its high to demonstrate.

Fifth: a **mechanical-versus-discernment** lesson. Asked about an opposing daily fair value

mechanically a bearish tick-box, actually not bearish at all. His indicator, which encodes
the mechanics, refuses to print the bearish setup. He also contrasts a gold "setup" with
the YM setup to show that the same pattern in a poor framework is not the same trade.

Also here: session-cascade logic (Asia does not expand because nothing manipulated before

the cascade lands elsewhere); an explicit **data-settings** note (back-adjust ON to remove

### Concepts introduced or clarified (his terminology)

### Rules / conditions stated

- A+ setup: daily C3 + London reversal (low/high of day) + New York continuation, entered
  on M15, with a protected swing confirming the wick; 2R target near the equal highs.
- POI is required for a reversal C2, not required for a continuation C2.
- Enter around the opening price of the HTF candle; do not enter once the candle has made
  its average range.
- On a 4-hour expansion candle, holding to the candle close is a valid time-based exit.
- Do not trade C4 when both C2 and C3 already expanded.
- Back-adjust ON, settlement OFF.
- Step one before any entry is a higher-timeframe bias or reason; without it you are
  overtrading by definition.
- Price reaching the OTE of the impulse leg is a *short* signal, not a long entry.
- A mechanically valid close-back-through a level is not a signal if the approach was slow
  and shallow with no aggressive reaction.

### Open questions / ambiguities

- "Slow and shallow" versus "aggressive reaction" is the discretionary override on an

- The A+ setup names London as the reversal session but does not define London's boundaries

  high — precedence between the R multiple and the structural target is unstated.
