# Study notes — unit `sunday_sessions_live_q_a_03`

Playlist: **Sunday Sessions - Live Q&A** (batch 3 of 3). Channel: TTrades_edu.
Four long live streams, all read in full. Two are New York Open sessions (live,

## New York Open Live Q&A  (bMkRomKEunU, https://youtu.be/bMkRomKEunU, 5212s)

### What is actually taught

This is a "no-bias day" stream, and the teaching is mostly about how he behaves when
the higher timeframe gives him nothing. He opens by explaining why his tweeted target
for YM was a nearer level rather than the obvious further one: typical YM expansion
candles run roughly 700–800 points, price had already delivered ~500, so a 1,000-point
day was not a target he would size an idea around. Target selection is therefore
constrained by observed average daily range, not by the next drawn level.

He then rejects a TGIF read on the week. The structural preconditions were present
(Tuesday high of week, Wednesday and Thursday expansion) but the week never reached a
relevant level, and without that hit he will not trade the weekly candle back into its
range. This is the single most repeated gate in the stream: a level is only tradeable
if it is *relevant*, and relevance is defined by whether price actually reached a drawn
objective, not by whether a line exists there.

The bulk of the middle section is relative strength. He treats YM, ES and NQ as a
triad: YM and NQ sit at opposite ends of the strength spectrum and ES sits in the
middle. If YM is strong, NQ is weak, and vice versa. He shorts the weak one and longs
the strong one, and specifically avoids trading the *lagging* asset — he prefers the
leading or the middle one. He shows this live: YM ran to highs, NQ lagged into a gap,
ES filled the middle, and the two ends were briefly negatively correlated, which he
says is normal and not a problem.

Phases of price is used as the arbiter of whether a level will hold. A reversal has to
look like expansion up followed by expansion down — a V shape. What he actually saw
into the YM high was expansion then a shallow retracement, which he reads as
continuation, not reversal. He generalises: consolidation implies continuation.

Late in the stream gold finally delivers the setup he described pre-open. He walks the
entry mechanically: 10:00 a.m. brings a new 4-hour candle; SMT on the correlated metal;
the 4-hour must open, wick up into the 0.5 of the prior wick, and trade lower with a
small wick to support expansion; on the 5-minute price sweeps a high, closes below the
protected swing, retraces into a small fair value gap, closes below again — that close
is the entry, stop on the bodies, minimum 2R. Because he had no strong HTF bias he
takes most of the position off at 1.5–2R at the first failure swing rather than running
it to previous day low.

He also gives the psychological fix he considers most important: do not drop to the
lower timeframe until you have a higher timeframe reason to be there. Pattern trading —
seeing a clean LTF formation and acting on it without context — is what he blames for
most losses, including one he demonstrates live that immediately fails.

### Q&A extractions

**Q:** Does a candle-two closure have to form from a key level?
**A:** He prefers it to. The 4-hour and daily do not always need to, but he wants it there.

**Q:** The C2 didn't sweep C1 so it isn't a valid short, right?
**A:** No — it does. That formation is not a candle two, it is a candle three closure; there
is a separate C2 video and a separate C3 video for exactly this reason.

**Q:** The previous candle didn't close outside — how can it be bearish?
**A:** It can. On the monthly he calls the candle bearish even though it did not close below
its open, because it made a high and then delivered an aggressive move, and as long as the
EQ is respected it stays bearish to him. In the first version of his course this would have
been labelled a candle three closure using the EQ; he changed the wording because students
confused it.

**Q:** Where do you see SMT on gold?
**A:** The correlated asset had already run out the equivalent high while gold had not — but
he immediately flags that as *bad*, because an asset that does not take that high at all
usually means gold runs to the high instead.

**Q:** Do you enter on the breakout or the retest?
**A:** On the close through, because he does not know whether price will retest, and he will
not give up a whole trade to save a point or two. Exception: if the closure prints far from
the level, he will not enter on the break with a distant stop — he waits for a new continuation.

**Q:** Is a 50% win rate enough?
**A:** Not at 1R. Yes at 1.5R and yes at 2R.

**Q:** I have weeks of doing well then one week where I lose it all.
**A:** It is almost always a single tilt day, not a week. Cap it at one trade a day — that
bounds the week to five losses maximum, which two-and-a-half 2R winners cover.

**Q:** Do you use intermarket analysis (10-year, DXY vs indices, DXY vs gold)?
**A:** No. He says dollar and gold have not correlated usefully recently and he does not use
dollar against indices at all; it does not help him, so he does not do it.

**Q:** Which time do you mark session highs and lows?
**A:** He does not mark session highs and lows. He marks highs and lows that are relevant to
where he expects price to go; London high, for example, had no relevance that day.

**Q:** Why don't you teach PSP?
**A:** He can, but he does not use it — PSP helps for reversals and he almost exclusively
trades continuations.

**Q:** How much do politics / macroeconomics matter?
**A:** On a 5- or 15-minute chart, zero. On a monthly chart he would care more.

**Q:** Why did price reverse at that exact spot?
**A:** Sometimes there is no reason and he does not know. He explicitly refuses to invent one.

**Q:** How did you learn relative strength between pairs?
**A:** Screen time — in 2022–2023 he kept YM, NQ and ES on a side monitor at 15-minute and
1-minute and watched how they moved together.

### Concepts introduced or clarified (his terminology)

- **Relevant level** — the gate that decides whether a counter-range trade exists at all.
- **Average daily range / expansion candle size** — a ceiling on target selection.
- **Candle two closure vs candle three closure** — two distinct formations, deliberately
  taught as two separate videos; C3 does not require the C2 sweep-and-close-inside shape.
- **Protected swing** — the swept high/low that a closure protects; 50% of it must hold.
- **Failure swing** — the small swings left behind after a protected low; a preferred
  non-greedy target.
- **T-spot** (his indicator) — the shaded area where he is looking for a reaction; grey = looking
  for continuation lower, green = continuation higher. **X** = the model has failed.
- **Intra-candle change in state of delivery** — the CISD that forms inside a candle rather
  than at its close.
- **Lagging asset** — the weakest correlated asset; explicitly avoided as a vehicle.

### Rules / conditions stated

1. Do not trade the weekly candle back into its range (TGIF) unless a relevant level was hit,
   even if Tuesday-high / Wed-Thu-expansion structure is present.
2. Cap targets near the observed average expansion size (≈700–800 points on YM), not at the
   next distant level.
3. When trading a range, take trades at the *edges* going back into the range; do not trade
   the middle trying to exit the range.
4. If YM is strong then NQ is weak (and vice versa); ES is the middle. Short the weakest,
   long the strongest, and do not trade the lagging asset.
5. Reversal = expansion in, expansion out (V shape). Expansion in + shallow retracement or
   consolidation = continuation.
6. A 4-hour candle can only sweep an overhead high if it closes higher with a small wick; a
   large wick argues against expansion in that direction.
7. Entry sequence: sweep a high → close below → that is the point of interest → 50% must be
   respected → retrace into the fair value gap → close below the protected swing = entry.
8. Stop on the bodies; minimum 2R; take most off at 1.5–2R when there is no strong HTF bias.
9. Do not go to a lower timeframe until you have a higher timeframe reason to be there.
10. Do not enter on the breakout when the closure is far from the level — require a new continuation.
11. 50% win rate is only viable at ≥1.5R.
12. One trade per day as a hard tilt-control cap.

### Quotes

> "Consolidation means continuation, generally speaking"

> "I'd rather be late than early"

### Open questions / ambiguities

- "Relevant level" is never given a mechanical definition in this stream — it is decided by
  eye against drawn daily/weekly objectives.
- The monthly "bearish without closing below the open, as long as the EQ is respected" rule is
  stated but the EQ is not defined here (EQ of the prior candle's range? of the leg?).
- Target-sizing by average daily range is eyeballed, not computed; he is asked how to calculate
  it and the question is not answered (the open interrupts).
- "Stop on the bodies" — which bodies (the swing candle body, the series?) is not specified.

## New York Open Live Q&A  (wM1s7UivQ08, https://youtu.be/wM1s7UivQ08, 3556s)

### What is actually taught

This stream is the cleanest demonstration in the unit of a correct idea that he refuses to
trade, and the refusal is the lesson.

He comes in bullish. The prior daily candle closed aggressively higher, so as long as the
upper half of that candle holds he wants a push into the highs. NQ is strongest, so NQ is
the vehicle. But the 4-hour candle does not support expansion higher — it has a large wick —
and his timeframe-alignment rule wants daily, hourly and 4-hour all "green" together. With
the 4-hour against him, the mechanical read is that price trades back to the 4-hour opening
price, so he waits for 10:00 a.m. and a fresh 4-hour candle rather than chasing the open.

The framework he states and then holds to for the whole stream is a two-branch scenario tree
built on relative strength: YM is the weakest, ES the middle, NQ the strongest. Branch one —
YM trades into its low and reverses aggressively, printing SMT against NQ; then he looks for
NQ higher. Branch two — YM does not reverse there; then ES and eventually NQ follow it lower
and his bullish idea is dead. He sets an alert and does nothing until the low is reached.

He is explicit about *why* the sweep must come first: a reversal is only available after a
relevant swing has been run. Price drifting up before that is "drawing people in long" and he
treats intermediate highs as bait. He also gives the invalidation for the bullish branch: if
price reacts at a fair value gap and then closes below it, that is no longer a retracement
inside a reversal, it is continuation lower, and the bias is wrong.

The scenario then substantially plays out — YM sweeps the low, SMT prints, price reacts — and
he still does not trade. The reason is location: the reversal low formed at *equal lows*, with
no separation from the swept level, so there is nothing protected to trade away from. He needed
the low to form higher, above the sweep, to give separation. He repeats this several times under
chat pressure and does not soften it. He also declines a related gold long for the same class of
reason: price was already above the range high and away from the daily open, and he will only
enter near the daily open or mid-range *before* the high is taken.

Two further mechanical points. He gives a three-step method for identifying the weak and strong
asset — SMT, separation/structure, and candle closures (a big bearish closure on one asset
against a bullish closure on another marks the weak/strong pair). And he describes the
before-the-open positional entry: he needs a protected swing already formed before 9:30, all
timeframes green, a stop that covers the fair value gap plus its 50%, and a 2R target; if the
risk-reward does not work he simply waits until after the open.

### Q&A extractions

**Q:** Is this 9:00 a.m. hourly candle a C3?
**A:** Yes. Mechanically it is a candle two closure, and with the change in state of delivery
present it becomes a candle three higher.

**Q:** So we're basically waiting for the 4-hour C2?
**A:** Yes, but that does not mean you cannot take the earlier one — you are just trading
against the 4-hour candle, which is harder. Easy setups look cleaner.

**Q:** Explain breaker blocks in one sentence.
**A:** Low, then a high, then a lower low, then a higher high; take the first low to the high
and extend it across. He adds that he does not really use them.

**Q:** Do you trade outside kill zones?
**A:** He does not care about kill zones at all; he cares about which higher timeframe candle
he is trading inside.

**Q:** Do you use the first-presented fair value gap?
**A:** No. If you want something like that, use the 9:30 open instead.

**Q:** Are you bullish on indices for the whole week?
**A:** No — bias is rebuilt day by day. Committing to one direction for a week means missing
the setups that go the other way.

**Q:** How do I identify the weakest asset?
**A:** Three steps: SMT, separation/structure, and candle closures. A big bearish closure vs a
bullish closure on the correlated asset tells you which is weak.

**Q:** Can you trade the invalidation of your bias?
**A:** Yes, and a video on it is coming, but he will not flip on a day he expects to consolidate.

**Q:** When do you execute before the equity open?
**A:** Only with a specific scenario: a protected swing already formed, all timeframes green,
30-minute or higher framing, and a stop that gives the fair value gap room (cover the gap and
its 50%). Otherwise wait for after the open.

**Q:** YM took those lows — why do you keep saying "equal lows"?
**A:** He does not dispute the sweep; he disputes the *location*. He will not trade away from a
low that sits at equal lows with no separation, regardless of whether the sweep technically occurred.

**Q:** Do you consider strength switching?
**A:** Nothing mechanical. He noticed YM became stronger after the sweep but says he cannot
anticipate that, and he was still expecting NQ to be strongest.

**Q:** How long did the model take to build?
**A:** About 10 months to make, then roughly another year to refine it down to a flow chart
that is fully mechanical.

### Concepts introduced or clarified (his terminology)

- **Timeframe alignment / "all timeframes green"** — daily, 4-hour and hourly all supporting
  the same expansion direction.
- **Small wick supports expansion** — and its converse, a large wick means the candle is more
  likely to trade back to its opening price.
- **Separation** — the distance between the swept level and the reversal low; without it there
  is nothing to trade away from.
- **Two-branch scenario tree** — weak asset reverses → trade the strong one; weak asset fails →
  everything follows it.
- **Protected swing before open** — the precondition for a pre-9:30 positional entry.

### Rules / conditions stated

1. Require a sweep of a *relevant* swing before a reversal is available; do not act on drift.
2. Do not trade away from equal lows / a failure swing that has no separation from the swept level.
3. All timeframes green (daily + 4-hour + hourly) before an entry; if the 4-hour is against you,
   the mechanical expectation is a trade back to the 4-hour opening price.
4. A reversal out of a low must be aggressive (V shape); consolidation or retracement after
   expansion into a low means continuation lower.
5. If bullish and price reacts at a fair value gap then closes below it, the bias is wrong.
6. Rebuild bias daily; do not hold a directional view across a week.
7. Do not enter gold long above the range high and away from the daily open — the entry window
   is near the daily open or mid-range, before the high is taken.
8. Most entries fall between 8:00 a.m. and about 10:30; after 11:00 he loses interest.
9. Expect a smaller range the day after a very large (~1,000-point) range day.

### Quotes

> "I'm not trading away from those equal lows"

> "I don't really care about kill zones"

### Open questions / ambiguities

- "Separation" is never quantified — how far above the swept low the reversal low must form is
  judged visually.
- "All timeframes green" is read off his own indicator; the underlying rule for colouring a
  candle green is not restated here.
- The pre-open positional entry mentions "30-minute or higher" framing in passing but the exact
  timeframe requirement is not pinned down.
- He says he is bullish and still expects the move higher after refusing it — so the refusal is
  a trade-selection rule, not a bias invalidation, and the two are easy to conflate.

## TTrades Sunday Session - Live Q&A  (r7yW6ou1LDs, https://youtu.be/r7yW6ou1LDs, 2876s)

### What is actually taught

A weekend review structured around phases of price and the weekly profile, with the most
explicit methodological disagreements with mainstream ICT in the whole unit.

He starts with gold and explains how he decided the market was in consolidation rather than
retracement. After expansion, the next phase is one of three: retracement, reversal, or
consolidation. To confirm retracement-into-new-expansion he needs price to close over the series
of down-close candles. When that fails on successive days and price falls back inside the range,
he classifies it as consolidation and stops looking at the asset entirely until one side of the
range is taken. He is emphatic that he will let a range sit for months untouched.

On oil he restates the TGIF preconditions and why the week qualified: a low formed Monday/Tuesday,
expansion through Thursday, *and* the weekly objective was met. He contrasts this with the week he
did not take, where the objective was never reached. This is the same relevance gate as the other
NY stream, applied to the weekly candle.

The order block material is the most reusable. He answers whether an order block still works after
being mitigated: yes, as long as the drawn liquidity / objective he is trading toward has not yet
been taken. The failure mode he names is people longing a re-tapped block *after* price has already
run the objective. He also states the timeframe relationship — the order block timeframe is derived
from the higher timeframe level being used, so a daily level means an hourly order block. And he
prefers a market entry on the close of the confirming candle to a resting limit inside the block,
because the retest often never comes and the fill is not worth the missed trade.

He disagrees with ICT directly on CISD: ICT says the change in state of delivery does not need a
close beyond the opening price; he says he does not care, he does what works, and — the load-bearing
part — he never defines an opposing candle from a single candle, always from a series.

He rejects macros outright, calling a macro nothing more than a section of an open-high-low-close.
He does not trade Monday roughly 90–95% of the time, waiting instead for Monday to print a reversal
and taking the Tuesday continuation. He uses premium and discount inverted relative to common usage:
if he is bearish he does *not* want price retracing up into premium, because that shows a wick forming
and means the candle is no longer bearish. He does not trade after three days of expansion, because
that is where a new phase of price begins and you are chasing. Normal frequency is zero to four trades
a week, and he only manages a position after 2R.

He also gives the positional variant: to position in the wick of a higher timeframe daily candle when
bearish, sell above the daily open with the stop above, and treat a trade above 50% of the opposing
candle's range as invalidation.

### Q&A extractions

**Q:** Can I still use a change in state of delivery / order block that has already been mitigated?
**A:** Yes, as long as your drawn liquidity or objective has not been taken. Once price has already
run the objective and comes back into the block, longing it there is where people get stopped out.

**Q:** ICT says CISD doesn't need a close above the opening price.
**A:** He respectfully does not care — he does what works for him, and he defines opposing candles
from a series of candles, never one candle.

**Q:** Do we need CISD to confirm SMT?
**A:** Yes. The mistake is basing an entire trade on SMT alone and longing straight into continued
downside without waiting for the confirmation.

**Q:** How would you have caught a move that never retraced into the order block?
**A:** By entering at market on the close of the confirming candle rather than resting a limit in
the block — there is no reason to be greedy over a fill if the close does not destroy the R:R.

**Q:** What timeframe order block should I use?
**A:** Whatever is one step down from the higher timeframe level you are working from — a daily
level means an hourly order block.

**Q:** Do you trade macros?
**A:** No, and he does not think they are a real thing — a macro is just a section of an
open-high-low-close that people misread for lack of understanding.

**Q:** Do you do weekly outlooks (Monday does X, Tuesday does Y)?
**A:** No. The whole plan is invalid the moment Monday opens differently. He waits for each day to
print and decides then.

**Q:** Do you always skip Monday?
**A:** Roughly 90–95% of the time, yes.

**Q:** Do you use a 1-hour discount PD array?
**A:** He uses it the opposite way. If bearish he does not want price retracing into premium at all,
because that tells him a wick is forming and the candle is not bearish any more.

**Q:** Once the high or low of the week is made, does it get easier?
**A:** Yes. Trying to pick the low inside the week gets you stopped repeatedly; once the weekly low
is in, the following expansion days are far easier than the reversal day.

**Q:** How do you set SL/TP on a market entry?
**A:** Risk is known regardless of the exact fill; position size is derived from it either way.

**Q:** Do you go below the 5-minute?
**A:** Generally no — below the 5-minute he loses sight of the bigger picture.

**Q:** Does SMT determine direction?
**A:** No. SMT is a confirmation only; it does not set his bias.

**Q:** Do you watch YM?
**A:** He says he never watches YM at all and finds it very uncorrelated to ES and NQ. (This directly
contradicts bMkRomKEunU, where YM is central to his relative-strength triad.)

**Q:** How many trades a week?
**A:** Normally zero to four.

**Q:** When do you manage a position?
**A:** Only after 2R.

### Concepts introduced or clarified (his terminology)

- **Phases of price delivery** — after expansion, exactly one of retracement / reversal / consolidation.
- **Opposing candles (as a series)** — his replacement for a single-candle order block definition.
- **Drawn liquidity / objective** — the thing whose status decides whether a mitigated block is still live.
- **Positioning in the wick of the higher timeframe candle** — selling above the daily open with the
  stop above, invalidated above 50% of the opposing candle range.
- **High resistance vs low resistance liquidity** — set up on the *daily* chart, not intraday.

### Rules / conditions stated

1. After expansion, confirm retracement by a close over the series of down-close candles; failure plus
   a fall back into the range = consolidation.
2. In consolidation, take no interest until one side of the range is taken.
3. TGIF requires Monday/Tuesday low, expansion through Thursday, **and** a weekly objective hit.
4. A mitigated order block remains valid while the drawn objective is untaken; it is invalid once the
   objective has been reached.
5. Order block timeframe = one step below the higher timeframe level in use (daily → hourly).
6. Enter at market on the confirming close rather than resting a limit in the block.
7. Never define an opposing candle from a single candle; always use a series.
8. SMT alone is not a trade — require CISD confirmation.
9. Skip Monday ~90–95% of the time; take the Tuesday continuation after a Monday reversal.
10. If bearish, price must not retrace into premium; that indicates a wick and kills the bearish read.
11. Do not trade after three days of expansion.
12. Manage the position only after 2R.
13. Do not execute below the 5-minute chart.

### Quotes

> "I never select one candle I always will use a series of candles"

> "all a macro is is a certain section of an open high low close"

### Open questions / ambiguities

- "Drawn liquidity" is the pivot of the mitigation rule but is never mechanically defined — it is
  whatever objective he had marked at the time.
- "Series of candles" has no stated minimum count.
- He mentions using the 6 p.m. open for the daily candle but then says he does not care what happens
  at midnight price; the exact daily-candle open convention is left loose.
- The YM statement flatly contradicts his usage in bMkRomKEunU; neither is qualified.
- "20 to 30% of the range" appears as an objective in the other Sunday session but not here.

## TTrades Sunday Session - Live Q&A  (u8bnmaih_hA, https://youtu.be/u8bnmaih_hA, 3756s)

### What is actually taught

The most granular weekly review of the four, walked day by day across gold, oil, indices,
USDJPY, USDCAD and GBPUSD, with the running theme being what makes an *opposing candle* valid
and how invalidation rolls forward as expansion continues.

The gold section establishes the sequencing: expansion out of consolidation, then he refuses to
think about longs until a new phase of price delivery begins; a closure plus validated opposing
candles gives a retracement-to-expand-higher read and a midweek reversal. He then adds the weekly
overlay — with a Monday low and a week of upside, he will not try to buy on Friday, because Friday
normally caps the weekly range. That leaves Friday's trade back into the range as the only move
aligned with his strategy that week.

The oil section carries the strongest new material. First, he will not short below lows — once
price is beneath a swept low he waits for a swing point to form and trades back into the range,
where the range is taken from the last impulse leg, and he wants the discount of that range plus an
imbalance plus an overlapping order block. Second, and this is the sharpest edge case in the unit,
he explains why certain candles are *not* opposing candles: a candle that simply opens and goes
lower with no wick does not make a relevant high, and therefore gives him no validation. He wants
open-high-low-close shape. Third, invalidation is dynamic: while expanding, each newly formed
opposing candle's high becomes the level that must not be hit, and it rolls forward as new ones form.

The Friday TGIF on oil is worked fully: SMT with gasoline validated the opposing candles as an order
block, and the objective was set both at marked highs and at 20–30% of the weekly range (measured from
the weekly high down to the low), which put the target in the same area. As price closes over each
successive level, a new invalidation point is established behind it.

On indices he applies high vs low resistance liquidity directly off the daily chart — the side holding
failure swings is the side he wants to run first, so he has no interest in longs until those are taken.
The weekly candle closed weak and inside the prior week's candle, which frames early-week downside.

USDJPY produces the counter-example: he liked the Wednesday reversal and wanted longs in the upper half
of the range, but the overnight took out the highs first, which is trading into high resistance liquidity
inside the daily profile, and he wants the failure swings there instead. He also demonstrates the wick
rule — once price closes below the 0.5 of the opposing candles it stops being a strong trend.

He gives his fair value gap position clearly: he rarely enters on them, because a resting limit inside a
gap gets ripped through; instead he waits for price to react and *validate a new order block*, which gives
him a usable stop. Finally he demonstrates trailing by opposing candles on a New York reversal, and honestly
notes that this trail would have stopped him out before the target — "a little bit too tight" — while also
saving him from sitting through the chop.

### Q&A extractions

**Q:** How do you know the high of the week is formed?
**A:** You never know anything in the market — if you knew, you would not need risk management. But once
price closes below the reversal level he stops anticipating that high (or Tuesday's high) being taken.

**Q:** Do you use fair value gaps for entries?
**A:** Very rarely. A limit inside a gap gets ripped through. He waits for a reaction that validates a new
order block, then enters with a stop he can actually use — or at market.

**Q:** Do you use SMT?
**A:** Yes, but it is added confluence, not foundational. He will not skip a setup for lacking SMT because
the setup is based on his higher timeframe narrative.

**Q:** ICT says midnight open is not relevant after 11:00 a.m. — thoughts?
**A:** Not something he studies. He uses midnight open only as a support/resistance level; his emphasis is
on the actual daily candle open.

**Q:** Do you use internal and external liquidity?
**A:** He trades market maker models within his model but says he does not actually use internal and
external liquidity.

**Q:** Do you use macros?
**A:** No — it is all open-high-low-close on smaller timeframes.

**Q:** Would you trade from an area of relatively equal highs or lows?
**A:** Possible but not preferred; he prefers a sweep.

**Q:** How do you hold a trade more than a day?
**A:** It depends on the setup; when swinging he takes most of the position off.

**Q:** What is on your chart when you actually analyse?
**A:** The daily open and an equilibrium mark. That is all.

**Q:** Do you use double purge?
**A:** Not personally; it has appeared inside his model but he does not use it specifically.

**Q:** Which candles do you not count as opposing candles?
**A:** Ones that just open and go lower with no wick — they do not make a relevant high, so they give no
validation of the idea.

**Q:** How do you set a TGIF objective?
**A:** Marked highs, and 20–30% of the weekly range measured from the weekly high to the low.

### Concepts introduced or clarified (his terminology)

- **Opposing candle validity** — must have a wick and make a relevant high (open-high-low-close shape).
- **Rolling invalidation point** — each new opposing candle formed during expansion supplies the next
  level that must not be traded back to.
