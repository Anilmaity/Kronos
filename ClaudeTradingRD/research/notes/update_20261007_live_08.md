### What is actually taught

**1. Yesterday review — large-wick target order (the one genuinely new parameter).**
Yesterday's daily took out the range high leaving equal lows; he wanted the range lows, with
volatility expected at 9:30 (retrace up, retest the level at 9:30, "10 Q lower"). Asked why he
did not expect much range: wick size — "a larger wick ... a larger opposing run", so no
expansion. The targets on a large-wick day are stated as an **ordered ladder**: daily open,
then intraday (overnight) lows, then previous day low (or previous day high on the mirror).
"It's always in that order." All three were hit and the day was done. The library's
`large-wick-target-adjustment` already lists daily open and session lows as substitute
targets, but not as a strict sequence and not with PDL/PDH as the third rung.

**2. The asset closest to the relevant level is the tell.** For the week he wanted indices
lower off the weekly open. Of YM/ES/NQ, YM was closest to the relevant level (a previous low),
so YM's reaction there decides the others: a very shallow wick and reversal at that low means
the others will likely reverse too (possibly with SMT) and continue higher; YM trading right
through with no reaction means continuation lower for all. In the replay YM went straight
through — London expansion.

**3. After a large overnight (London) expansion into a relevant level, New York can only be
framed back into the range.** He does not like trading reversal days that fight the daily
range and "usually tends to avoid" New York on those days. Today: the daily profile is bearish
(open high first, PDL already run overnight), lower timeframes bullish — conflicting. He will
not short below the lows ("It's like a location thing"); the only frame is NQ higher as a
retracement, targets daily open then intraday/overnight highs, previous day high "a bit to go".
He explicitly would not take the 9:30 SMT off the open with the stop that implies ("quite
sketchy"). Lesson he repeats at the end: fading the daily profile is allowed only with lower
expectations — daily open, maybe intraday highs, not a trend day. When trades line up well
the daily candle supports them ("nice and green") and that is how trend days come.

**4. Relative strength vocabulary.** Leading = strongest, lagging = weakest, judged by just
looking at the candles (4H: NQ bright green, YM bright red, ES consolidating). Bullish → NQ
leads, YM lags, ES middle. For the bullish idea he wants the weakest (YM) to sweep its low and
reverse, so ES's low holds and the strongest continues. Live: YM did sweep the 4H low (direct
equal lows), giving SMT on the higher timeframe; he then wanted the 3-minute CISD — "close over
that level, which is just the opening price of the 4hour candle" — which never closed. YM then
failed to react a second time; he calls it chop, "a washed day", ES and NQ moving the same
~33 points when NQ "should be down like 100 or 200 points". No trade taken. Quiz to chat
confirms NQ leading. Demonstration: an entry at 9:30 on ES "haven't gone anywhere" versus NQ
having trended — why relative strength matters.

**7. Rules stated in Q&A.**
- Partially mitigated FVG: the unfilled part can still be treated as the gap, but simpler to
  just mark the low and treat it as resting liquidity.
- Liquidity "is a swing high or a swing low"; focus on important ones (PDH/PDL).
- HTF read: "obsess about the daily chart ... how the daily candle forms" — decide where the
  daily should go and why, understand the daily profile, let the wick form then trade away.
  Points to his intraday reversals video as the one to watch.
- He rarely uses 4H gaps.
- Trade window: roughly the first two hours of New York; from when he finishes his morning
  routine (sometimes 8, a little after) to about 11. Does not trade London (sleeps).
- When the New York open gives no idea, wait for 10:00 a.m.
- No SMT on micros — six charts makes no sense; minis only.
- News: does not care intraday ("I do not care what the news is"); does not trade during the
  Fed announcement; does not hold intraday trades through CPI/NFP; WILL hold through CPI/NFP
  when the trade was entered on the daily chart (has done so).
- R: does not really trade below 2R; a viewer's backtest of his "4 hour 15 minute model" over
  1.5 years (viewer-reported 40-48% win rate, average RR 1.64) is "not really my model". He
  takes more than 2R; teaches 2R on YouTube because people mismanage positions.
- Model premise: price only reverses when it has a swing point, so even when his bias is wrong
  a confirmation will still form — he does not treat confirmation as proof of bias.
- He prefers his model over footprint/time-and-sales (did TPO/volume profile/footprint for a
  year first) because footprint makes him decide non-mechanically; his model is "I get a close
  over this level ... I take it". TPO single prints can be read off a 30-minute chart.
- Overwhelmed with knowledge: stop learning new things, refine; he marks only highs, lows, FVGs.
