## New York Open Live Q&A  (GRc5FVB5tdg, https://youtu.be/GRc5FVB5tdg, 3822s)

### What is actually taught

**Day classification.** He arrives after the open; the prior days expanded into a daily low
that was a target, and that low is now taken. He labels the day a **reversal day** and states
the consequences: (a) no shorts at the low ("We don't want to short below this low"), he
prefers "shorting up in the range, not at the low"; (b) lower expectations — on a reversal day
targets are "back towards the daily open to the previous EQ", chosen by wick size; a small wick
leaves room for continuation; (c) his favourite reversal days open near the previous day low with
a small wick and then expand — this one is not that, so it is "not a clean day".

**Bias stack he states for the long.** Monthly: inside bar inside bar = consolidation, so he
favours "a reversion back into the range ... over breakouts". Daily: expansion into a target
then reversal. Daily profile: **open low first** into the target. Hourly: change in the state of
delivery; working assumption is the **7 a.m. low holds**. 4-hour: the candle containing the open
has already made a large range, so he waits for the **10:00 a.m. 4-hour candle**.

**Cross-asset rule (the core of the stream).** Relative strength on the hourly: YM strongest,
ES middle, NQ weakest. There is an hourly gap below; at the open **no asset had traded into it**,
so he says it is "really hard to frame" a long. Required sequence: the **weakest asset (NQ)**
trades into the gap / below the 9:00 low and reverses, while the stronger assets hold -> SMT ->
continuation expected on ES/YM. If NQ instead keeps going lower, he expects the others to reverse
down with it ("they are still correlated assets").

**10:00 a.m. mechanics.** Mark the 9:00 a.m. hourly low; the hourly must sweep and then continue
higher (form a reversal candle) — too large a wick toward the lows and it is "not bullish
anymore". Ideal: the 9:45 15-minute closes weak/lower, the next 15-minute opens, sweeps into
it, forms a 15-minute C2 reversal with a CISD inside the candle. His indicator marks **0.5 of
the 15-minute wick** as the expected swing area.

**Protected swing.** A wick into the T-spot does not make a protected swing; it becomes protected
only on a close over the series of down-close candles that made the low. You may anticipate it
and trade with a stop below (reversal style). He enters **early inside the 15-minute** (on the
1-minute) and requires that 15-minute candle to close over the level; if it had reversed and not
closed over, "you can scratch the trade".

**Timeframe choice.** Favourite is 4H/15m, but on a reversal day the 15-minute gives poor R
because little expansion is expected, so he refines to the **15m/1m fractal model**. The
3-minute would only have confirmed at the target, so the 1-minute was needed here; "generally
speaking, you don't".

**Break-even.** Conditioned on entry location: an entry down in the reversal (below the EQ of the
new expansion) can go to break-even, because price returning there means the expansion is over;
an entry right at the new protected swing should not, because a retest there is still valid.
"any sort of entry in here or below this EQ can be break even".

**Targets / management.** First mechanical target = the swing high that made the swing low being
traded away from ("short-term target for this 15-minute expansion"). Then the daily open (reached;
the reversal-day target). ES target: failure swings above; the higher high is "harder to get to"
because it sits in a large higher-timeframe order block.

**Swing trading (aside).** For a multi-day move after a daily reversal he enters on the
**hourly**; his Friday short was held into this low.

**Psychology / channel policy.** No signals, no call-outs; he trades off-stream; "never going to
grow as a trader if you go sit in a stream and copy someone".
