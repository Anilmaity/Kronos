## Let The Trade Play Out (6C5b8IOujiU, 42 s)
**Taught:** this answers whether a strict TP and stop loss is better than going break even. Early on, use a strict TP and SL and do not manage the position.
Management means extra decisions, thoughts and stress, and beginners "mess up the management". The evidence is one viewer:
holding every trade to only +2R or −1R would have left them "up an extra six R without any stress".
Management can come later.
**Library:** this repeats `mechanical-trade-management`, word for word from the 4rNC3QXxC20 variant ("extra 6R"). Nothing new.

## Let the Intra-Candle CISD Form the HTF Wick (A9QW9SuW0rs, 57 s)
**Taught (bearish):** a candle 2 closure with a CISD has displaced lower, and candle 3 opens. Ask "has this wick formed yet?"
and answer it mechanically by waiting for the intracandle CISD. Inside candle 3 price makes a new high, and that high "gives
us a valid point of interest". Then comes a close below the level marked under it. That continuation inside the HTF
candle "forms the wick", and the trade is taken lower. The previous day low as target gives "right around two R", which is hit.
**Hygiene:** the stop is never stated, and the ~2R implies it sits at the new high. No timeframe is named.
**Library:** this repeats `ic-cisd` (same example as Vo2n47RjjMo). PDL and 2R as targets are already in its execution block.

## When Is It Ideal to Trade Candle 4? (DMOVzdGZtsU, 59 s)
**Taught (bullish):** price makes a V-shape, then a consolidation, then runs the consolidation low. That candle is "not a candle two or a candle
three closure", so it is not traded away from. The next candle sweeps the range low, which is a candle 2 closure. It is still tradeable
("we have failure swings right here") but **not ideal**, because it has not closed over the series of down-close candles.
The next candle closes over that level, over candle 2's high **and** over the range high. Mark the EQ of that candle,
which "aligns pretty well with this opposing candle". Candle 4 is now ideal to trade. It prints a shallow wick and trades higher.
**Library:** this repeats `ideal-formation`. Its rule "a non-ideal candle 2 followed by a candle 3 that closes through the series is an ideal
formation for trading candle 4" comes from the same parent. It also repeats `fractal-model-c4`. The candle 3 closure passes three
tests at once (series, C2 high, range high), and the clip never says which one is required. That is recorded as an ambiguity.

## Candle 3 Ideal Formation → Candle 4 Expansion Higher (zIpL5N8AMig, 36 s)

**Library:** this repeats `ideal-formation` and `fractal-model-c4`, from the same SF61vCsBl1A passage as DMOVzdGZtsU.

## Using Previous Highs as Targets Within the Fractal Model (LlpzUXZStfk, 36 s)
**Taught:** price runs out the whole range, and then there is a candle 2 closure. The targets are the previous highs, and three are marked.
"Why am I not marking out this high here? Well, it's already been taken out", so only highs that have not been taken
are marked. All of them are hit. "Keep your targets very simple. Whatever the higher time frame reference is, those are
going to be your targets."
**Library:** this repeats `previous-period-high-low`, which already has "skip any extreme that has already been taken out" and
the same parent line.

## Stop Trying to Predict Your Bias (NXhJ9uvxHbc, 23 s)
**Taught:** you cannot know tomorrow's bias until today has closed. A close up at the high means probably bullish, and staying lower means bearish.
"Don't try to predict things until you have the daily closure."
**Library:** this repeats `daily-bias-c2-c3-h1-cisd`, which already quotes that line from uW55Tuk-ngY. The clip does not mention
the H1 CISD step. The close test is only pointed at on the chart ("up here"), so no level is named.

## Stop Messing With Your Trades, Wait for Candle Closures (qzfBzzVoecU, 34 s)
**Taught (psychology):** good results only in backtesting have two causes. A backtest skips ahead to closed candles
and never interferes with the trade. Live trading acts on active candles and manages trades by moving the stop. "Stop messing with your
trades, wait for your closures." He hedges it: "I could be wrong".
**Library:** this repeats `wait-for-closure-discipline` (the uW55Tuk-ngY variant, with both causes). It is untestable.
