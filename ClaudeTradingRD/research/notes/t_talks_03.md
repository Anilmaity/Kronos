## Trader Kane | The Mind Behind a Multi-Million Dollar Day Trader
(video_id: `1wKfc2gN4xg`, https://youtu.be/1wKfc2gN4xg, 1739s)

### What is actually taught (detailed)

An interview with a guest trader ("Trader Kane") conducted by TTrades. The guest
opens with a career timeline: roughly ten years around markets, entering through
advertising that promised outsized returns, and by his own account being scammed
early. He describes dipping in and out rather than trading continuously — heavier
involvement 2015–2016, a drop-out around 2018–2019, a return during COVID, with
2020–2021 dominated by crypto (he says he rode the market up and timed most of it
well). He then states he lost a million dollars when FTX collapsed at the end of
2022, pivoted from crypto to forex, used prop firms to rebuild capital, and later
moved from FX into futures, where he says he holds the record for the largest
payout at a futures prop firm.

The prop-firm section is the most concrete operational content. His stated plan
was deliberate rather than opportunistic: accumulate twenty accounts, then run
them all up inside a 70–80 day window because the payout structure at the time
required ten trading days plus six or seven payouts before the full balance could
be withdrawn. He traded aggressively at the start specifically to escape the
trailing drawdown (he says he cleared it in four days), then reverted to small,
conservative base hits — most trades around 100 ticks — relying on twenty accounts
for scale rather than on size or long holds in any one account. He states the goal
should always be to graduate to your own capital, and that prop capital is a means,
not an end; he calls the present moment the peak of prop-firm generosity.

His personal-capital behaviour is deliberately different: he lets positions
breathe, takes many small "paper cuts" trying to get positioned, and accepts those
because the winners he catches are large. His capital structure is explicit — he
funds a personal account with an amount he is willing to lose entirely (his example
is $50,000), treats that deposit as his capped downside, and once the account has
grown he withdraws the initial stake so that everything traded thereafter is, in
his framing, house money that he can risk more freely.

Risk sizing is given as a rule: a base of 1% per trade, scaling up half a percent
after a winner to a maximum of 2%, and scaling down after a loser to a minimum of
0.25%. The stated purpose is to minimise losing streaks and maximise winning
streaks, which he claims is what actually shapes an equity curve.

The methodology passage: he tries to be positioned in the 4-hour and hourly wick,
noting these often overlap a key market time of 10:00 a.m. He wants the retrace
into 50% of the prior move, and once price trades into and inverts something inside
that wick he treats the wick high as the reference and trades back toward the
close. He explicitly says a shallow wick means he cannot be positioned that way and
he switches to a different model. He avoids range-bound markets because his model
loses in them, and prefers "consolidating expansive" markets that retrace to 50% of
a leg and then expand again.

On partials he is strongly negative for intraday trading, arguing they steal from
the future trade and imply you do not actually have a draw; he concedes the logic
on swing trades to avoid round-tripping. On journaling he now records only losers
and emotionally-driven trades, having journaled everything early on to establish
consistency. On psychology he emphasises probability, acceptance of losses, having
a rule-based stop-trading trigger for tilt, and downsizing when a single loss would
be emotionally intolerable — which he says preceded his own six-trade losing streak
and preserved capital.

### Concepts introduced (speaker's own terminology)

- "Scaling matrix" for risk — dynamic percentage sizing keyed to the last outcome.
- "Base hits" — small conservative prop-account wins, ~100 ticks.
- Trailing drawdown escape as an explicit early-phase objective.
- "House money" / getting the initial out.
- Positioned "in the 4-hour wick" / hourly wick; trading the invalidation of a wick.
- "Shallow wick" as a condition that disqualifies his primary model.
- "Consolidating expansive market" vs "range-bound market".
- Market cycles: expansion → consolidation → retracement → consolidation → expansion.
- "Paper cuts" — repeated small losses while trying to get positioned.
- Partials framed as stealing from your future self.
- Tilt / revenge trading and the rule-based step-away.
- Break-even trader as a legitimate intermediate stage, not a failure.

### Rules / conditions stated (each decidable if possible)

1. Risk 1% as a base; after a winner increase by 0.5%; after a loser decrease by
   0.5%; cap at 2%; floor at 0.25%. (Decidable.)
2. Deposit only an amount you are willing to lose entirely into a personal account;
   withdraw the initial deposit once the account has grown; treat the remainder as
   house money. (Decidable given a cash-flow ledger.)
3. Do not take intraday partials; either you have a draw and hold to it, or you do
   not have a trade. Partials are permitted on swing trades. (Decidable per trade,
   though "swing" is not defined in minutes/days.)
4. Be positioned inside the 4-hour or hourly wick; take the retrace into 50% of the
   move; trade the wick's invalidation back toward the close. (Partly decidable —
   inverting something inside the wick is not precisely defined.)
5. If the wick is shallow, do not use this model; switch models. (Not decidable —
   no shallow-wick threshold is given.)
6. Do not trade range-bound markets; either trade the range if it is big enough or
   do not trade. (Not decidable — no range-size threshold.)
7. Have a rule-based number of losses (or losses-and-wins) after which you stop for
   the day; the exact number is explicitly left to the individual.
8. Journal losers and emotionally-driven trades; do not journal winners once the
   model's reps are done. Journal everything early in the journey.
9. Downsize when a single loss at current size would tilt you.
10. Always have a planned entry and a planned exit on both sides of the market.

### Examples walked through (instrument, timeframe, what happened)

- **Apex-style futures prop accounts, 20 accounts, ~70–80 day campaign.** Aggressive
  early trading to clear the trailing drawdown in four days, then ~100-tick base hits
  scaled across all accounts to reach the payout threshold.
- **NQ / ES futures, January vs the interview date.** He describes trading up to
  ~40–100 minis in January, then downsizing as volatility rose; cites NQ moving 50
  points in 5 minutes, and a two-hour range of ~400 points at the time of recording.
- **A partial taken "yesterday"** into new yearly lows on an index future: he sized
  a partial in because he could not guarantee the low, price pushed through his
  target level, and he closed the rest near the same area before the close.
- **A six-trade losing streak** roughly two weeks after he downsized following the
  January run — offered as evidence that the downsize preserved capital.
- **A Wednesday ~$6k loss against ~$50k of wins over Friday and Monday** on Ninja
  Trader, used to argue for judging results across a sample rather than per day.
