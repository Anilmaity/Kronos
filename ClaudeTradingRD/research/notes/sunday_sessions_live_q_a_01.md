## (CPI) New York Open Live Q&A  (`egV_gABTQks`, https://youtu.be/egV_gABTQks, 6558 s ≈ 1h49m)

### What is actually taught

A live, unedited walk-through of a single day's anticipation on index futures, plus a long
Q&A. The whole session hangs on one idea he states at the open and refuses to abandon: the
daily candle closed bearish, NQ is the strongest index and YM the weakest, therefore the one
and only thing he will anticipate that day is **YM trading into previous day low**. Everything
else — gold, oil, Bitcoin, EUR/USD — is examined and then explicitly dismissed as "consolidation"
or "not clean."

The mechanical spine he describes: (1) read the daily chart for direction and for whether the
daily candle is still forming its wick; (2) identify which correlated asset is weakest/strongest;
(3) wait for a lower-timeframe swing point / protected swing to trade away from; (4) trade
toward one relevant level and stop. He repeatedly refuses to predict beyond that single leg —
"you don't need to predict out 3 days when you're only trading the next day."

Three teaching moments stand out. First, **cross-asset confirmation as a live requirement**:
because his idea depends on NQ reversing off its high, he narrates in real time that NQ is *not*
reversing and that this means he is probably wrong — the weakest asset only falls once the
strongest one rolls over. Second, an explicit **edge case for CISD**: he declines to use a
change-in-state-of-delivery entry because the CISD level sits at the same price as his
short-term target, so waiting for the close would leave him entering at the target; instead he
leans on the V-shape reversal plus the gap plus SMT. Third, a **time-based exit** framework: an
expansion candle closes at its extreme, so a position taken inside a 30-minute expansion candle
can be held to the close of the period, and 10:00 a.m. is the meaningful boundary because new
higher-timeframe candles open there.

He also states his tool list exhaustively and narrowly: highs and lows, fair value gaps,
"opposing candles" (which he says is technically an order block but he defines it differently),
CISD/protected swings, and SMT. No break of structure, no change of character, no key opens
other than higher-timeframe candle opens, no midnight open, no indicators. He explains why 9:30
matters (NYSE cash equities open — SPY/QQQ/single names, so volume enters) and mocks the
"the algo injects volatility" explanation.

Risk and psychology are handled bluntly: one to two trades a week; 2R at above 50% win rate is
"doing good"; nobody has a 100% win rate ("you would bet all the money you have"); the psychology
fix is "don't do dumb stuff"; break-even is moved only once a *new* protected swing exists, and
he warns that moving to break-even too early gets you retested out of a valid trade.

### Q&A extractions

**Q:** What is the difference between inducement and a liquidity sweep?
**A:** Effectively the same thing — a lot of fancy words that don't mean much. Inducement is
simply price running out a low before trading higher. His preferred form is a sweep of a
short-term high/low followed by a fair value gap.

**Q:** Do you trade the Asian session?
**A:** Only in one specific case: a candle-2 closure on the *daily*, then inside that candle-2 a
reversal forms and a **protected swing** exists prior to the open of the next candle. Then he can
anticipate that low holding inside the next candle and trade higher, entering with the stop below
the protected swing. He does not otherwise drop to lower timeframes in Asia.

**Q:** What does your A+ setup look like?
**A:** Candle 3 on the daily — candle 3 on *every* timeframe — plus an open that reaches into a
point of interest, usually a fair value gap, then a closure through, and he enters there with the
stop beyond, everything aligned in one direction.

**Q:** How do you confirm your bias?
**A:** Have a direction on the daily, then wait for the daily to form the *wick* of its
higher-timeframe candle. Once you have a formed wick you have something to trade away from; then
find a swing point / fractal model on a lower timeframe in that direction. It is aligning multiple
timeframes plus a protected swing, nothing more.

**Q:** Do you break even at 1R or leave it alone?
**A:** It depends on the framework. He moves the stop once a *new* protected swing has formed —
he can move it to that new swing or to break-even, because price shouldn't come back there. Moving
to break-even too early gets people retested out right before the move.

**Q:** Which entry timeframe do you use?
**A:** Whatever matches the swing timeframe being traded. A 4-hour swing is usually entered on the
15-minute. 15-minute is the minimum for London; he only goes lower into the New York session when
volatility allows.

**Q:** Can you explain the conditions for a turtle soup?
**A:** All timeframes aligned in one direction, plus the phases of price (e.g. a consolidation),
so a sweep is anticipated; you enter *at* the sweep with a tight stop just beyond it. He says most
people should avoid it. He also calls out social-media traders who enter with a wide stop and then
trail it down to make the screenshot look like a perfect turtle soup.

**Q:** Is it wise to take a continuation trade after previous day low has been taken?
**A:** He prefers not to trade once his target is hit. The judgement is how much daily range was
consumed reaching the level — if reaching it used 500–600 points of a normal daily range, the
continuation below is much less interesting than if only 100 points were used.

**Q:** Why don't you take a retest entry at the point of interest?
**A:** He wants price to *show* him it will trade lower — a candle-2 closure / swing point forming
in the level — rather than blindly buying/selling the retest, so that he is protected if the level
fails.

**Q:** Do you use key opens (midnight, 8:30, etc.)?
**A:** Only the opens of higher-timeframe candles (daily open, 4-hour open) shown by his indicator.
He knows when 8:30 is by looking at the chart; he doesn't mark session opens because he wants a
clean chart.

**Q:** What is a good win rate?
**A:** At 2R, anything above a 50% win rate is good.

**Q:** How many trades do you take a week?
**A:** One or two — he prefers to trade less.

**Q:** What should I do about psychology to get payouts?
**A:** "Don't do dumb stuff." 95% of the time the error is an action you already know is wrong.

**Q:** Do you use RSI / OB-BOS-CHoCH indicators?
**A:** No. He does not use break of structure or change of character at all; only change in state
of delivery.

**Q:** Which asset should I focus on in FX?
**A:** Do all the analysis on the dollar, then pair it with the weakest (or strongest) counter
currency — that day, EUR/USD or USD/JPY, because EUR and JPY were weak while CAD and others were not.

**Q:** Does 9:30 affect currencies?
**A:** Not really. 9:30 matters for indices because the NYSE cash session opens — SPY, QQQ and the
single names start trading, which is where the volume comes from.

**Q:** Should I anticipate what happens *after* previous day low is hit?
**A:** No. Anticipate one leg, see what happens, then re-anticipate. Predicting several legs ahead
doesn't help you trade.

### Concepts introduced or clarified (speaker's own terminology)

- **Weakest / strongest asset** — among NQ, ES, YM; trade the weakest for shorts, the strongest for longs.
- **Protected swing** — a swing that reached into a point of interest; the invalidation anchor.
- **Change in state of delivery (CISD)** — the only structural break he uses.
- **Opposing candle** — his own term for what others call an order block ("I just define them differently").
- **V-shape reversal** — the shape a genuine reversal makes; contrasted with drifting/consolidating.
- **Time-based exit** — exiting at the close of the higher-timeframe expansion candle (e.g. 10:00 a.m.).
- **Daily wick formation** — the daily candle forming its wick via a 4-hour/15-minute swing point.
- **Fractal model** — his model name; daily/4H bias → LTF CISD → continuation.
- **Drivers** — referenced as an existing video topic, paired with open/high/low/close.

### Rules / conditions stated

- If the daily closed bearish and asset A is strongest, trade asset B (weakest) toward previous day low.
- Do not switch the daily bias on the basis of a single 15-minute candle; switch only if a *relevant level* has been hit.
- Skip the CISD entry when the CISD level coincides with the short-term target.
- After a large wick forms, use 0.5 of that wick as the level the next move must respect; price trading much above 0.5 of the wick puts the idea in danger.
- Expansion candles close at their extreme → a position can be held to the end of the period (10:00 a.m. for the 4-hour/30-minute boundary).
- Continuations must be quick: if price spends more than ~2 candles hanging around instead of expanding, treat it as consolidation and expect the prior high/low to be run again.
- Asia is only traded when there is a daily candle-2 closure plus a protected swing formed before the next candle's open.
- Move the stop only once a new protected swing exists; trail tightly as the target approaches rather than giving back a large move.
- Only trade Monday–Friday; no weekend/Sunday trading (stated in a later stream but consistent here).

### Quotes

> "you're aligning multiple time frames in one direction and then finding a protected swing"

> "I don't switch my bias because of 1 15-minute candle."

### Open questions / ambiguities

- "Relevant level" is used as the gate for changing bias but is never defined in this stream; he says a video on relevant highs and lows is scheduled.
- The 0.5-of-the-wick rule is applied to both the HTF candle wick and to the CISD leg; whether these are the same rule at different scales is not stated.
- "Candle 3 on every timeframe" as the A+ condition is not reconciled with his own examples where he trades candle 2.
- Whether "opposing candle" and an ICT order block are identical is deliberately left vague ("I just define them differently").

## Candle 2 Confirmations — Live Lesson, Q&A  (`uzPGXVYpVGc`, https://youtu.be/uzPGXVYpVGc, 6671 s ≈ 1h51m)

### What is actually taught

The most content-dense video in the unit, and the only one built around a prepared lesson (a PDF
he made ~20 minutes before going live). The thesis is a **sliding scale of required confirmation**:
how much proof you demand from the market before entering a candle-2 reversal should be inversely
proportional to how strong your higher-timeframe bias and structure are. At one end — clean daily
structure, expansion then consolidation then a sweep of the high, an ideal closure — he will take
the *reversal entry* itself (the first CISD that confirms the wick), stop beyond the extreme. In
the middle he demands a **continuation** entry or SMT. At the weak end — messy daily, no narrative
— he demands *both* a continuation and SMT, or he does not trade.

Underneath that sits the precondition: candle 2 is only tradeable **in that candle** if it has a
small wick. A large opposing run makes a large wick, which means the candle has already used its
range, so you wait for candle 3 instead. He gives the corollary target for large-wick days: look
back toward the candle's open / the liquidity around it, not for expansion.

The second half is dominated by what he calls the single takeaway: **"let the wick form, trade the
body."** He turns this into a decidable test he applies aloud on chart after chart — *do I trust
this high/low yet?* If price could plausibly return through the extreme and the idea would still
be valid, the wick is not formed and the stop cannot go there. If the extreme is early in the
candle, shallow, and confirmed by a CISD, the wick is trusted and becomes the invalidation. He is
explicit that his mechanical rules (specific closures, EQ usage, CISD) exist only because "let the
wick form, trade the body" cannot be taught as a course.

Timing rules recur: the reversal should form **early into the higher-timeframe candle**, not
midway — if it forms midway there is no point trading it and you wait for the next candle open.
Continuations should form in the *new* higher-timeframe candle so the HTF extreme is the stop,
which is his answer to "why do continuations always stop me out": people enter at the end of a
candle using an internal, insignificant high that the next candle takes out.

He walks live examples on NQ/ES/YM, gold, USD/CAD, EUR/USD, NZD futures vs NZD/USD, Bitcoin
(including an audience vote on reversal vs continuation entry, where both worked), Doge and a
monthly-timeframe Bitcoin example demonstrating fractality. He also explains the **first
short-term target** rule: when shorting into a target you want to be positioned in the upper 50%
of the swing-to-target distance; once price is inside the lower half there is nothing left to risk
against.

Notable dismissals: **macros are not real** — 10:00 a.m. "macro" behaviour is just the 4-hour/1-hour
candle opening, and he will never make a macro video except as a troll. He also says he does not
fear alpha decay from releasing his model, because a reversal must always form a swing point, and
that he did not invent swing points, hammers, or CRT-style ideas — he only defined how he wants them.

### Q&A extractions

**Q:** What does "demand more confirmation" actually mean?
**A:** With strong bias you can just take the entry. With less bias you require a continuation, or
SMT, or another confluence before entering.

**Q:** Do you use the new day opening gap, 8:30, or true day open?
**A:** Only the daily open. Not midnight, not anything else — just open, high, low, close.

**Q:** Why does my continuation entry always stop me out?
**A:** Because you are ignoring the higher-timeframe candles. You are entering at the *end* of a
candle against an internal high that has no significance; wait for the wick to form in the new
higher-timeframe candle and enter the continuation there, so the HTF extreme is your stop.

**Q:** How do you confirm the higher-timeframe wick won't come back and stop you out?
**A:** You don't know for sure. You ask whether you trust the high: if price reached it early,
shallowly, with a CISD, you trust it; if it's late/deep/consolidating, you don't, and either the
stop goes higher or you don't take it.

**Q:** How do you select timeframes?
**A:** Daily and hourly for bias. After that the exact pairing doesn't matter — H4/M15, H1/M5,
M30/M3 — the goal is only that they all point the same way.

**Q:** Do you fear alpha decay from publishing your model?
**A:** No. The model rests on price having to form a swing point to reverse, which will always be
true; if the market changes, you adjust how you enter the new swing points.

**Q:** What is the T-spot on your indicator?
**A:** It blends equilibrium with other elements of the model to mark the zone where he wants the
higher-timeframe wick to form. Its size accounts for average volatility.

**Q:** What if candle 3 opens *high* first when you expected an open-low?
**A:** Then usually you can't get the change in state of delivery, price comes back in, and it
tends to fail and make a new one. Often it is better to wait for the next candle and treat it as a
candle-3 setup instead.

**Q:** Why are macros not real but the open is?
**A:** Because a 10:00 a.m. "macro" is just the new higher-timeframe candle opening. It makes no
sense to enter in a magic window mid-candle when you should be letting the 10:00 candle form its
wick and trading it away.

**Q:** Why do I get chopped up trading a reversal?
**A:** Because you are entering before the session open on 1–3 minute charts where you can be
wicked out while the idea is still valid. Use 15/30-minute; your invalidation is the HTF extreme or
the continuation, not a 3-minute swing.

**Q:** How do you know which pairs to use for SMT?
**A:** Memorised groupings — GBP and EUR go together; JPY is alone; CAD is alone; CHF is alone;
NZD and AUD go together.

**Q:** Why don't you give high-R examples?
**A:** Because 17R screenshots are neither realistic nor sustainable. 2R is plenty.

**Q:** Does CISD validate a market structure shift?
**A:** No.

**Q:** Are you a scalper, day trader or swing trader?
**A:** He prefers higher timeframes — 15-minute or 5-minute execution is his ideal.

**Q:** Any tips for London traders?
**A:** Study the 4-hour chart. The 2 a.m. candle often has a large wick, so it is usually better
for the 6 a.m. candle; with a large opposing run, expect a move back to the candle's open rather
than expansion, then look at candle 3.

**Q:** What are the extra "advanced" concepts in the course?
**A:** Things like what to do when candle 3 consolidates, and which specific closures qualify.

### Concepts introduced or clarified

- **Candle 2 confirmation scaling** — bias/structure confidence vs confirmation demanded (the lesson's graphic).
- **Reversal entry vs continuation entry** — the two entry types inside a candle-2 reversal candle.
- **Let the wick form, trade the body** — the model in one sentence.
- **Trusting the wick / trusting the high** — the decidable test for whether the extreme can be a stop.
- **Small wick supports expansion; large wick → wait for candle 3.**
- **First short-term target / upper 50%** — position in the upper half of the swing-to-target range.
- **T-spot** — indicator zone where the HTF wick is expected to form, sized by average volatility.
- **Autobias 1 / Autobias 2** — indicator filter restricting setups to those aligned with one or two timeframes up.
- **Phases of price** — expansion, retracement, consolidation, reversal (referenced as his series).

### Rules / conditions stated

- Trade candle 2 within its own candle only if the wick is small; otherwise wait for candle 3.
- Strong bias + clean structure → reversal entry allowed (stop beyond the extreme). Weak bias → require continuation and/or SMT.
- The reversal must form early in the higher-timeframe candle; if it forms midway, do not trade it — wait for the next candle open and look for a continuation.
- Enter the continuation only after the new higher-timeframe candle has opened, so the stop sits at a higher-timeframe extreme.
- When shorting toward a target, enter in the upper 50% of the swing-to-target distance; below the midpoint there is nothing to risk against.
- Expansion → retracement should be shallow; a deep retracement into a fair value gap after expansion is not a valid continuation setup.
- If the high you would use as a stop is one you do not trust, either widen the stop to a level you do trust or skip the trade.
- Daily + hourly define bias; any of H4/M15, H1/M5, M30/M3 may be used for execution.

### Quotes

> "let the wick form trade the body"

> "if we have more bias and structure, we can demand less from the market"

### Open questions / ambiguities

- "Early into the candle" vs "midway through it" is never quantified (no fraction of the candle's duration or range is given).
- "Small wick" vs "large wick" has no numeric threshold in this stream; it is judged visually against the candle body / prior candles.
- The confidence scale (strong / medium / weak bias) is a hand-drawn continuum with no checklist for where a given day sits.
- SMT is listed as an alternative to a continuation in some sentences and as an addition in others.

## New York Open Live Q&A  (`WBwoiMDXT2c`, https://youtu.be/WBwoiMDXT2c, 5980 s ≈ 1h40m)

### What is actually taught

This is the "no-trade day" stream, and its value is almost entirely in the negative space: he
spends an hour and forty minutes explaining, live, why there is nothing to do. The daily bias is
bullish (daily candle 2 with an H1 CISD), but the **daily profile contradicts it** — price opened
and immediately expanded higher into objectives without first making a low, which he reads as
bearish intraday, back toward the daily open. That gives him a conflict he refuses to resolve by
forcing a trade.

The core structural rule he keeps returning to: **you must have something to trade away from.**
Because no low was swept all session, he cannot frame a long ("I need a sweep of a low"), and the
bearish idea kept failing its own conditions, so the correct answer is to do nothing. He turns this
into an explicit multiple-choice for chat — force trades, tilt, or don't place a trade.

The most transferable lesson is his **daily-bias recipe stated as a recipe**: go to the daily,
look for a candle 2 or candle 3 closure confirmed by an hourly CISD — that is your daily bias, "by
far the easiest way" — then layer on EQ, phases of price, relevant swings, and finally *confirm it
with the daily profile*. If the profile does not confirm (open, no low, immediate expansion), the
intraday direction can oppose the daily bias.

He also gives the sharpest statement in the unit on **SMT**: SMT is a *confirmation*, not a
framework. Price must first show it wants to go that way; an SMT with no displacement and no model
around it means nothing. He calls out traders who "use SMT to just frame things."

Two mechanical details appear here that don't appear elsewhere as clearly. First, the
**failure-to-close diagnostic**: when a reversal fails to close below the level quickly and instead
consolidates, it is building failure swings, and each minute it doesn't drop makes it less
interesting — "the longer it takes to reverse price, the less interested I become." Second, the
**low-disguised-as-a-fair-value-gap warning**: a fair value gap on a 3-minute chart may be nothing
but a 1-minute low; reacting off it is really a sweep of a low, which sends price the other way.

Psychology gets an unusually direct treatment. For an account-blower he refuses generic advice and
insists on **root-cause identification** — tilt/overtrading, no system, or oversizing — with a
specific fix per cause (max one trade a day; learn a model; size correctly), and frames it as a
binary: make a change or quit, because anything else wastes years. When a viewer says they felt
tilt coming, closed the trade, and moved to paper at −$100, he screenshots it and turns it into
the lesson that you cannot stop feeling emotions, only stop *acting* on them.

He repeats the anti-macro position and here goes further: he does not use kill zones either, only
higher-timeframe candles; and he says the DXY–indices correlation has not been reliable lately, so
he does not reference the dollar for index trades. He does not use order flow — he considers it
subjective versus a mechanical "did this close below this level."

### Q&A extractions

**Q:** I've been blowing accounts for four years. What do I do?
**A:** Identify the *root cause* first: tilt/overtrading, no system, or oversizing. Then apply the
matching fix — max one trade per day, learn a model (any model), or size correctly. You have two
choices: make a change or quit; everything else wastes your time.

**Q:** I trade multiple pairs — how do I choose which one to trade today?
**A:** Go to the daily. Is there a candle-2 or candle-3 closure with an H1 CISD? That is your daily
bias, by far the easiest way. Then add EQ, phases of price and relevant swings, and confirm with
the daily profile.

**Q:** What is the daily profile and which timeframe is it?
**A:** The structure of the daily candle — how it opened, where it made its low/high, how it
expanded. Any timeframe can be used to see it; he likes the hourly to visualise it, and 4-hour also
works.

**Q:** What is your entry confirmation?
**A:** Protected swings and continuation order blocks.

**Q:** Why are macros nonsense?
**A:** A "9:50–10:10 macro" is just the 4-hour/1-hour/30-minute candle opening at 10:00. It is only
new higher-timeframe candle opens.

**Q:** What is your takeaway on kill zones?
**A:** He doesn't care about them; he focuses on higher-timeframe candles.

**Q:** Would you enter on a 1-minute SMT?
**A:** He doesn't use the 1-minute like that. The SMT is irrelevant until price displaces away and
confirms it — SMT is confirmation, not the framework.

**Q:** Why are you ignoring the daily bearish fair value gap?
**A:** It isn't relevant within the daily profile; that is not how he trades.

**Q:** Do you consider trading this with order flow?
**A:** No — order flow is subjective for him (what counts as absorption, what delta means), whereas
"does this close below this level" is mechanical and that is all he needs.

**Q:** When I'm in a trade should I check every open/high/low/close on every timeframe?
**A:** No. Once you have defined where you are wrong, monitoring every timeframe just raises your
cortisol. Only the timeframe your swing is based on matters (e.g. how the 4-hour candle closes).

**Q:** What if the 4-hour bias is opposite the daily?
**A:** Then it is not confirming the daily bias.

**Q:** Which days do you trade?
**A:** Monday through Friday only; never weekends or Sunday.

**Q:** Does your fractal model work on crypto?
**A:** Yes, but use higher timeframes — crypto sweeps both sides before going and is messier than
indices, which tend to hold and trend.

**Q:** Any advice on 15-second / 30-second timeframes?
**A:** Very few people can do that sustainably; use higher timeframes.

**Q:** How do you train your reaction to market movement?
**A:** By being specific: have one idea and wait for it to occur or invalidate. People struggle
because they try to catch every move; choose the windows in price you actually care about.

**Q:** What do you think of CRT traders using your candle-2/candle-3 closures?
**A:** He thinks they lack the understanding because they take the *next candle* as the target,
which is pattern trading; the target should be the drawn liquidity, not the adjacent candle.

**Q:** How long did it take you to become profitable?
**A:** About two years.

**Q:** What is a broadening formation?
**A:** Mark a range's high and low; price makes a new range beyond it, falls back inside, then
broadens again — successive liquidity sweeps, generally occurring during consolidation, and that is
how expansion away from it starts.

**Q:** GBP/JPY — would you trade it?
**A:** No: you would be pairing a bullish currency with a consolidating one. Pair opposite
directions (e.g. GBP/USD when GBP is bullish and USD bearish) — that is when expansion occurs.

**Q:** Was it easy to skip trading days at the start?
**A:** No, because he didn't know exactly what he was looking for. A more defined model makes
skipping easier.

### Concepts introduced or clarified

- **Daily profile** — the shape of the daily candle (open → low/high → expansion) used to confirm or veto the daily bias.
- **Daily bias recipe** — daily C2/C3 closure + H1 CISD.
- **SMT as confirmation, not framework.**
- **Failure swings** — what a stalling reversal builds while it fails to close through.
- **Continuation order block** — his entry object (reversal → CISD → reach into FVG or sweep of an LTF low → closure over).
- **Root cause vs symptom** (psychology framework).
- **Feel the emotion, don't act on it.**
- **Relative strength video / comparing currencies** — pair strong against weak, never against consolidation.

### Rules / conditions stated

- Daily bias = daily candle-2 or candle-3 closure confirmed by an H1 CISD; then it must be confirmed by the daily profile.
- If price opens and expands immediately into objectives without first forming a low, the bullish daily bias is not confirmed intraday; expect a move back to the daily open.
- No swept low → no long framing. No relevant level hit → no trade.
- If you traded a reversal, price returning to the reversal point invalidates it (move to break-even), because it will then run the high.
- After a CISD you want the 50% of that leg to hold; failing to close through and consolidating instead means it will likely fail.
- Do not react to a fair value gap that is only a lower-timeframe low; that is a sweep, and price will go the other way.
- When confused: do not force a trade.
- When you can't tell which of NQ/YM is stronger, use ES (the middle asset).

### Quotes

> "The longer it takes to reverse price, the less interested I become."

> "You want to use SMT as a confirmation"

### Open questions / ambiguities

- "Daily profile confirms / does not confirm" is demonstrated by example only; no enumeration of valid vs invalid profiles is given here (he points to a separate daily-profile video).
- The threshold for "the longer it takes" is never given in candles or minutes.
- He says both that he is bullish on the daily and bearish on the daily profile, but does not state a general priority rule beyond "my model makes me look that way."

## New York Open Live Q&A  (`0AYGNc9czYc`, https://youtu.be/0AYGNc9czYc, 5853 s ≈ 1h38m)

### What is actually taught

The most quantitatively useful stream in the unit, because he improvises and then repeatedly
applies a **three-point trade-quality checklist** he says he wrote down during the stream:

1. Enter before the targets are hit.
2. Enter before the daily range has been created.
3. Enter around the daily open (or around the higher-timeframe candle opens).

He then scores days against it out loud — "three of three met, that's good; two of three, okay;
one of three, not good; zero of three, low probability" — and uses it to reject gold twice and to
explain why that day's index idea, while it worked, was low quality. He operationalises point 2 by
eyeballing recent daily ranges (NQ: 658, 740, 540 points, so roughly 500–600 expected) and asking
how much of that budget today has already spent; he explicitly says he does not use an ADR
indicator.

The day's single idea is again narrow: YM has already hit its targets and is consolidating; if YM
reverses off its range low, then **ES** — the middle asset — can trade to previous day high before
10:00 a.m., because the 6:00–10:00 4-hour candle is an expansion candle and he wants to see it
close into its high. He explains choosing ES precisely because he cannot tell whether NQ or YM will
be stronger; ES sits between them and is cleaner in that situation. He also states he will not
trade the *lagging* asset, and that after the 10:00 open, with targets hit and range spent, long
setups are off the table — the only frameable idea becomes a short back into the range, which he
notes is a reversal and therefore not his preference.

The second big theme, hammered three separate times, is that **SMT means nothing without a
framework**. Chat repeatedly points at SMTs at previous day high; he refuses each time on the
grounds that there is no V-shape reversal and no model saying to look lower — a consolidation at a
level tells him continuation, not reversal. He contrasts this with the EUR/GBP/DXY example where
the SMT *does* count because the dollar has a candle-2 framework around it.

Other durable material: he reframes the perennial "what timeframe should I enter on" question as
the wrong question — **ask where the invalidation is**, and that tells you the timeframe. He gives
the plain-English reduction of his whole model: waiting for a higher low or a lower high, which he
turns into a protected swing and trades away from. He explains why he prefers the 3-minute to the
1-minute (the 1-minute is too messy). He defines a **relevant swing** by *separation*: when two
highs sit close together with no separation, there is nothing relevant to trade away from, and he
loses interest.

Risk/psychology answers here are the most concrete in the unit: for a viewer who lost four trades,
set a max-loss or max-trade limit; treat paper trading as real or you build bad habits; if the
market is choppy, get off; if you keep failing payouts, identify the root cause before asking for
a fix. He also tells a profitable viewer to save for taxes and warns that keeping money is harder
than making it.

### Concepts introduced or clarified

- **Three entry-quality criteria** (targets / daily range / daily open) with an explicit 3-of-3 → 0-of-3 probability scale.
- **Daily range budget** — eyeballed from recent daily candles rather than an ADR indicator.
- **Middle asset (ES)** — the fallback when relative strength between NQ and YM is unclear.
- **Lagging asset** — the one that hasn't reached its level; explicitly not traded.
- **Relevant swing / separation** — highs or lows too close together are not relevant levels.
- **Invalidation-first timeframe selection.**
- **Higher low / lower high** — the plain-language reduction of the fractal model.
- **V-shape as the sweep-vs-consolidation discriminator.**

### Rules / conditions stated

- Score a prospective trade against the three criteria; 0-of-3 is a low-probability day.
- Do not enter after targets have been hit, after the bulk of the expected daily range is spent, or far from the daily / HTF candle open.
- Do not trade the lagging asset; trade the strongest (for longs) or weakest (for shorts).
- A consolidation at a relevant level implies continuation, not reversal; a reversal requires a V-shape.
- An SMT is only actionable when a framework (model + bias + level) already points that way.
- Two highs (or lows) with no separation between them are not a relevant swing to trade away from.
- Choose the entry timeframe by where the stop can be placed, not by preference.
- If a 4-hour candle is an expansion candle running 6:00–10:00, the move should complete before the 10:00 open.
- After the target is hit and the range is spent, the only frameable idea is back into the range — and that is a reversal, which he does not prefer.
