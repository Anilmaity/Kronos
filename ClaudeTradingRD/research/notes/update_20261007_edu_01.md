## 1. Let The Wick Form, Trade The Body (Stop Getting Stopped Out) (RX8vtP3PLYk, 1056 s)

### What is taught
- **CISD / protected swing recap:** price reaches an important level (a high, low or FVG), then
  closes through the series of opposing candles that went into that level. That shifts the trend.
- The video's subject is the confirmation **inside the continuation candle**. With a bullish HTF
  expectation he will not enter at the open, because "price could just open and drop right through
  the low". He waits for a higher low, i.e. a new protected swing. That means letting the bullish
  wick form: a short bearish move, then a shift back to bullish. This is the logic behind the
  **intra-candle CISD (IC-CISD)**. He says he created the concept "to try to mechanically define
  this logic".
- **The one question** he asks before any entry: *did this higher time frame candle form its wick
  yet?* Yes means there is a protected swing to trade away from. No means wait.
- **Failure mode named:** shorting before the wick forms means "we're going to be the liquidity for
  the wick to form".
- **Entry location:** "Let the wick form, enter around the opening price" gets you on side for the
  body/expansion. Early wick formation in the candle is wanted.
- **Exit:** with a true expansion candle you can hold toward its close, because "expansion candles
  close in its high or low".
- **Fractal:** the same logic is applied on every timeframe (daily/hourly, 4H/15m, 1H/5m).

### Worked examples (no prices are spoken. Levels are only described relative to the chart)
1. **Daily C2 closure with SMT vs ES, V-shape, bearish.** The current asset closes back aggressively,
   while ES shows the SMT and a retracement. He walks it candle by candle, asking "has the wick
   formed?" Answers: no (high not trusted), no, no (shallow sweep into a gap, can't close below),
   then "looks decent" but a little gap up. On the 30m it is only consolidating near equal highs,
   so still no. Next is a reach higher that disrespects the gap. He says he could give up and look
   at the next day, or allow **"one more high"** as "our last chance before I give up on the idea".
   It sweeps that high and gives a candle closure, then a CISD closure. When it looks unclean he
   drops to the 30m ("you can always adjust your time frames"), where a big up-close candle is
   closed below. Entry on the hourly with the stop at invalidation gives 2R into the gap, or a
   4H/15m or 1H/5m fractal model. The expansion lower follows.
2. **The same move at 5m under the hourly.** Pre-9:30 is "not the best formation": "generally prior
   to 9:30, I want to use the 15-minute candle". He anticipates the wick when price reaches the
   only gap before the previous protected swing. A confident trader can enter anywhere with the
   stop on the high. Otherwise wait for the close through the up-close series, which forms a new
   protected swing, then target 2R. The target is hit, and the next candle repeats the pattern
   (sweep, then closure, then continuation into target).
3. **Daily retracement into an FVG, candle closure into the New York session** (intraday-reversal
   context; targets are the daily open and the current or previous day's high, plus time-based
   exits). Hourly to 5m. He repeatedly says "no, the wick is not formed" because FVGs sit right
   below. Then a low is swept, price reaches into the FVG and closes over it: that is the IC-CISD.
   Targets are 3R short-term or ~7R to the HTF target, and both are hit.
4. **The "imperfect" range example (gold).** The range low is already taken and the range high has
   failure swings, so both a bearish and a bullish C2 closure exist. He zooms out: "Gold is bullish
   right now". A **bearish protected swing that forms first is ignored**. He wants it invalidated
   and a bullish protected swing to form, which it does (reach into an FVG, close over a single
   candle). He explains he used **a single candle rather than the whole range** because the lower
   timeframe already showed a swing-high/swing-low shift. Entry on the close-through, near the
   opening price, stop on the wick low, target 2R or the range high. Both are hit.

## 2. Relative Strength & Weakness + SMT Divergence (Full Guide) (yH4eYwUgdTY, 955 s)

### What is taught
- **Rule:** "generally I want to be longing the stronger asset and shorting the weaker asset".
- **Three inputs:**
  1. **SMT**, "between correlated assets a difference in structure". At the same low, the higher low
     is stronger and the lower low is weaker (mirrored at highs).
  2. **Structure / separation**, used "when SMT is not present". Both assets made higher lows, so
     compare each new low's distance from the extreme low. "The greater the separation, the
     stronger the asset". At highs, the asset closer to the shared high is stronger.
  3. **Candle closures**, called "more advanced". (a) **C2 test:** both sweep the prior low; the asset
     whose C2 closes back above C1's low is stronger, and the one that "can't close bullish or back
     above candle one's low" is weaker. (b) C4 bullish vs bearish at the same timestamp. (c) A
     close over the previous body vs a doji or an inside close.
- **Correlated assets only**, and "this shouldn't be used alone". A higher-timeframe framework and
  strategy come first.
- Particular emphasis: closures of "candles that create the change in the state of delivery or
  continuation" often show the strength difference.

### Worked examples
1. **ES vs NQ.** At the same high, NQ makes a lower high and ES a higher high, so NQ is weaker. Then
   the closures: ES has a strong bullish C2 while NQ has a doji. Next, an engulfing bearish candle
   on NQ vs a doji on ES. Separation: ES is a few points from the prior high while NQ is far away.
   NQ's drop is larger ("an anomaly of a day").
2. **Gold futures vs gold-GBP, bearish.** Gold-GBP took a high that gold did not (an SMT, so gold-GBP
   is weaker). At the lows, gold-GBP takes a low that gold does not. The key closure: gold-GBP closes
   below the level that validates the CISD and gold cannot. Both go lower anyway.
3. **An unnamed pair with multiple SMTs.** Two highs on different timeframes both show the same
   SMT, and one asset also takes a low the other did not. Later the closures show a **strength
   switch** (one asset consolidates flat while the other has more bullish closes). "strength and
   weakness is not going to remain consistent".
4. **Gold-euro vs gold futures, bullish fractal model.** No obvious SMT, so he uses separation:
   gold-euro is further from the low, so it is stronger. It also takes the high first and closes
   over the high, while gold futures sweeps and closes below.
5. **Final combined example.** SMT at two lows. Closure test: one asset's candle cannot close below
   the down-close candles, while the other closes below them. The next bullish candle closes back
   over the down-close candle on the strong asset only, which forms a continuation. The strong
   asset expands and the other catches up later.

### Transcript hygiene
"gold GBP" / "gold euro" are spoken pair names. Some assets are never named ("this asset"), so the
pairs in examples 3 and 5 are unidentified.

## 3. How to Trade With a 9-5 Job (TTrades Fractal Model Explained) (cWDnzI3lpUk, 743 s)

### What is taught
- For full-time workers he argues for **higher timeframes, not scalping**: "go to the higher time
  frame, not the lower time frame".
- **Weekly bias:** "is price going to reach for previous week's high or previous week's low?", or a
  weekly C2/C3 closure. A daily-chart bias is accepted as a shortcut.
- **Align daily swing points** with that bias (phases of price), then trade the **daily + hourly
  fractal model**: let the daily wick form, and the go-to entry is an hourly IC-CISD. Swing-point,
  candle-closure and positional entries are also allowed.
- **Chart-check cadence:** "1 minute taking a look at the top of every hour". In an 8-hour workday
  that is 8 minutes.
- **US time zones:** "focus on Asia session, see if I can position myself for the day", and if that
  fails check New York. He qualifies it: "this is really working on any session".
- Targets: often 2R within the daily range, or hold for the weekly range, even over weekends or
  for multiple weeks when aligned with the day of week.

### Worked examples (all named instruments, no prices)
1. **BTC.** A swing high is taken, then a daily C2 closure, an hourly CISD and an IC-CISD. Stop on
   the high or the body, 2R hit, with further continuations toward equal lows.
2. **Gold, weekly profile.** Mon/Tue expand up and Wednesday reverses, so Thu/Fri are expected
   lower. A protected swing at the new daily open is used to position, 2R hit.
3. **GBPUSD.** An aggressive C2 closure. A new protected swing forms off the open in a gap, which
   is anticipated as the low of day. 2R hit.
4. **Gold.** The range high is taken with a C2 closure. The first protected swing off the open
   **fails**, which he calls "a valid loss" and says he does not mind. A second protected swing
   forms after the retrace up, giving 2R to the daily FVG / range low. A new continuation in New
   York gives a further entry with better R. The target is hit.
5. **NASDAQ hourly.** A messy C2 closure with a V-shape and no continuation formed, so a trader
   waiting for the IC-CISD misses it. The **positional** alternative: "We expect the EQ to hold" and
   "We expect 50% of this change in the state of delivery to hold". The stop goes below both,
   "usually around the body of this opposing candle", for about 2R. It is hit.
6. **DXY daily into EURUSD.** The range high is taken. The relevant high has enough separation from
   the gap not to be a failure swing. C2 closure plus an hourly CISD. He wants a small wick and no
   trade above the high. "I would want to long a foreign currency": EURUSD mirrors the structure
   (swept the previous low), so he goes long with the stop on that low. After three expansion
   candles he considers a limit for a retracement but says not to be greedy and enters at market.
   Thu/Fri expansion, with a possible hold over the weekend.

### Transcript hygiene
"candle to closure" = candle 2 closure. "get to our or we can hold" is a dropped word (likely "2R").
"you you", "is is". No timestamps.

## Cross-video: new vs repeated

**Repeats the library (sources added only):** wick-trust-test (core rule), ic-cisd (definition),
htf-opening-price-entry, time-based-exit-htf-close (expansion candles close at their extreme),
smt-divergence (definition), relative-strength-weakness (the three inputs were already present for
ES/NQ/YM), ttfm-swing-trading-model (weekly to daily to hourly), midweek-reversal-week,
currency-pairing-opposition, gold-correlated-assets.

**Materially new rules or parameters:**
- wick-trust-test: the per-closure question loop. The **"one more high, then give up / look at the
  next day"** abandonment rule. **Ignore a protected swing that forms against the HTF trend** once
  that side's range extreme is taken. Single-candle vs full-series CISD selection when the LTF has
  already shifted. Drop to the 30m when the CISD is unclear.
- ic-cisd: **before 9:30, use the 15-minute candle** (in the hourly/5m example). The concept's own
  origin is stated: it was created to mechanise "let the wick form".
- relative-strength-weakness: an input **priority** (separation only when SMT is absent), the **C2
  close-back-inside test**, the **CISD-validating-closure test**, generalisation to gold vs
  gold-GBP/gold-euro, and "correlated assets only, never alone".
- smt-divergence: several SMTs on different timeframes can exist on the same leg.
- ttfm-swing-trading-model: **hourly-close check cadence** (1 minute at the top of each hour), the
  **Asia-first session preference** for US-timezone traders, and weekly bias from the previous
  week's high/low.
- positional-entry: a stop below **both the EQ and 50% of the CISD**, around the opposing candle's body.

No genuinely new concept ids. Everything maps to existing entries.
