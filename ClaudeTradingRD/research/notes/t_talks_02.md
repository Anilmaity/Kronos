## DexterLab | Putting All The ICT Pieces Together - Master PO3
(`wWIHS_dxbEY`, https://youtu.be/wWIHS_dxbEY, 3084s)

### What is actually taught
Dexter's thesis is that a single Power of Three (PO3) candle is a complete map of the
market, and that every ICT concept sits *inside* one candle if you decompose it on
lower timeframes. He first restates PO3 in his own terms: the **open** is both the
time element and the accumulation; the **manipulation** phase prints the wick (high or
low) of the candle in development; the **distribution** phase prints the *body*; and
the **close** prints the opposite wick. He then makes the claim that structures the
whole talk: both wicks of any candle terminate in a PD array of *the same timeframe or
higher — never lower*. If a higher-timeframe framework demands more precision you
refine downward, but the governing array is same-or-higher.

He restricts the working timeframe to the **4-hour candle** for intraday purposes, and
gives explicit opening times: for index futures, 4H candles open at 2am, 6am, 10am and
2pm New York time; for forex there are effectively only three usable candles (1am, 5am,
9am NY) because the PM period is the London close and moves little.

The mechanical core is a two-candle framework: you project standard deviations from the
*previous* 4H candle's manipulation leg, then classify the *current* candle by **where
it opened inside that projection**. If it opens at the 1 / 1.5 deviation, there is
"unfinished business" and continuation to the 2 / 2.5 deviation is high probability
(Framework 1 — continuation). If it opens at the 2 / 2.5 deviation, expect a
retracement to equilibrium/discount of the previous range, and if the higher timeframe
supports it, a reversal (Framework 2). He adds a further rule: a **body close beyond the
2.5 deviation** makes continuation to the 4th deviation high probability, and the
retracement inside that leg is the "second silver bullet zone".

Confirmation is always SMT: when the wick prints, a correlated market should fail to
make the matching extreme. He is explicit that SMT is a *confirmation* tool, not a
synchronisation tool, and that it matters precisely because the candle "looks" the
wrong colour at the moment the wick is being printed.

Finally he gives an ordered list of "internal dynamics" that fill a PO3 in sequence:
turtle soup (the sweep) → order block (the last opposing candle, confirmed by a close
through it) → **breaker, which forms at the candle's own opening price** → fair value
gap / OTE / silver bullet zone for the continuation leg. He stresses that the strongest
breaker is the one overlapping the time-opening of the candle in development, and that
which internal dynamic you get depends on where the candle opened relative to the
previous range.

### Concepts introduced (speaker's own terminology)
- "power of three universe", "crude representation" of PO3
- "unfinished business" (unfilled deviation / PD array objective)
- "framework number one" (continuation) / "framework number two" (reversal)
- "internal dynamics" (turtle soup → order block → breaker → FVG/OTE)
- "second silver bullet zone" (retracement after a body close beyond 2.5 dev)
- "the opening in function for the previous range"
- SMT as "confirmation tool… no synchronization tool"

### Rules / conditions stated
- Every candle's wick high and wick low terminate at a PD array of the same timeframe or higher, never lower.
- Index-futures 4H opens: 02:00, 06:00, 10:00, 14:00 NY. Forex: 01:00, 05:00, 09:00 NY (three candles only).
- Project standard deviations from the previous candle's manipulation leg (its high-to-low move).
- Open at 1 / 1.5 dev → high-probability continuation to 2 / 2.5 dev.
- Open at 2 / 2.5 dev → anticipate retracement to EQ/discount of the previous range, or reversal if HTF supports it.
- Body close beyond the 2.5 deviation → high probability of reaching the 4th deviation.
- In a retracement the draw is *internal* liquidity; the PD array is what continues the previous direction.
- A wick is only confirmed when a correlated market prints SMT at that extreme.
- Breaker zone is only "in development" once price closes back through the current candle's opening price.

### Examples walked through
- NASDAQ 15-minute chart, daily PO3 anchored on midnight open: previous day's projection range put the open between 2 and 2.5 dev → retracement, turtle soup below a short-term low into a 4H bullish order block at the 3.5 dev, then order block → breaker above midnight open.
- A second daily PO3 where the midnight open sat at 1 / 1.5 dev → continuation lower into the previous daily low + daily bullish order block, with turtle soup then order block then reversal (Framework 2).
- A 2am/6am pair where price closed a body below 2.5 dev and continued to the 4th deviation into the previous daily low, then reversed with SMT.
- 15m → 5m drill-down showing that when the 1/1.5-deviation zone has no visible FVG on 15m, the same zone contains one on 5m.

### Quotes
- "it's in one specific pdra the same time frame or higher"
- "the first internal Dynamic the turle soup the next the order block"

## Gene | Align Yourself For High Probability Trades
(`nncJGt6j19Q`, https://youtu.be/nncJGt6j19Q, 3013s)

### What is actually taught
This is part one of a two-part talk and is deliberately framework-level rather than
entry-level; Gene says outright that he is not giving an entry model, he is teaching
*when not to trade*. He opens with a probability argument: certainty language on social
media is the enemy, the only goal is money, and a model must be derived from statistics
so that neither a loss nor a missed trade is emotionally significant. He reports that
his own accuracy improved as his trade frequency fell from four-to-seven trades a week
to one-to-three, and is explicit that this is *his* pattern, not a prescription.

His central distinction is that **analysis and trading are different skills**. He
describes years of publishing correct directional calls (drawing arrows to levels that
price genuinely reached) while still being break-even, because the path into the level
cost him more than the move paid. From this he argues an "entry model" is not a model:
a model must be a step-by-step filter that separates analysis from execution.

The mechanical content is the **weekly candle read**. Order flow is framed as the
provision of liquidity by central banks to large funds; because of size, institutions
must transact at higher-timeframe reference points (order blocks, rejection blocks,
breakers, inefficiencies), which is why lower-timeframe trading must be framed from the
daily. He then decomposes the weekly OHLC: in a bullish week, institutions accumulate
**below the weekly opening price**, where the opening price is midnight New York (not
GMT). The early-week move against the intended direction he names **"valuation by
speculation"** — an aggressive counter-direction move, typically Monday into Tuesday,
not driven by an economic driver; he welcomes it because the weekly higher-low forms
out of it. Above the open comes the **"profit release phase"**: one-sided expansion
toward the weekly objective as the market seeks counterparty liquidity to square
positions. He pyramids into that expansion using the economic calendar as a timing
element and scales out into a lower-timeframe premium (a daily/H4/H1 level).

He then teaches **weak longs and weak shorts**: displacement equals attention, so a fast
move attracts late buyers chasing the breakout and early counter-trend sellers trying to
sell the top. The subsequent retracement into discount flushes both. The trader's job is
to be neither — to wait for price to trade below 50% of the range before buying (his
"optimal trade entry" argument, framed with an iPhone price analogy).

Finally he sets the timeframe hierarchy: daily/weekly for direction, **H4 and H1 as the
intermediate narrative timeframes**, and M15/M5 only for execution — with the M15 used to
confirm what was already seen on H1.

### Concepts introduced (speaker's own terminology)
- "valuation by speculation" (the early-week fake-out)
- "profit release phase"
- "weak longs" / "weak shorts", "displacement equals attention"
- "trading is about finding the highest probability analysis"
- "dealing range selection" on the lower timeframe
- the rabbit-and-eagle analogy for LTF vs HTF perspective

### Rules / conditions stated
- Weekly opening price is measured at 00:00 New York, not 00:00 GMT.
- If bullish, buy at or below the weekly opening price; if bearish, sell at or above it.
- If the daily draw on liquidity is neither clearly higher nor lower, the situation is 50/50 → do not trade, wait for more market information.
- Do not take reversal trades at the start of the week; trade with weekly order flow. Reversal trades are only considered when the weekly candle is likely being distributed.
- Before buying, price must trade below 50% of the range (discount); before selling, above 50% (premium).
- Add positions into the profit-release expansion using the economic calendar as the timing element; square into a lower-timeframe premium.
- Analysis must be done on H1/H4; M15/M5 are execution timeframes only.

### Examples walked through
- A randomly selected bullish weekly candle on a CHF pair, dropped to H1, showing accumulation below the midnight-NY weekly open then expansion above it.
- The same pair: a run lower into a *daily bullish order block* as the HTF discount, with accumulation below the weekly open forming there.
- Bitcoin's first break of 69k (2021) as the archetypal "weak longs" event — retail buying the breakout into the top.

### Quotes
- "trading is about finding the highest probability analysis"
- "buy at or below the opening price if I'm bullish"

### Open questions / ambiguities
- "Weekly opening price" — whether Sunday 18:00 NY or Monday 00:00 NY is meant is not stated; he only says midnight New York.
- No definition is given of how to decide the weekly draw on liquidity in the first place ("this is not the lesson for that").
- "Below equilibrium into discount" — the range whose 50% is measured is never specified (weekly? daily? the leg?).
- The promised part two (market profiles + explicit trade/no-trade rules) is not in this unit.

## GxTradez | Phases of Price & Profiles For Expansion
(`J_EeS2_2CAM`, https://youtu.be/J_EeS2_2CAM, 3839s)

### What is actually taught
Garrett (GxTradez) presents the most mechanically dense talk in the unit. His stated
goal is a filter for *when price action is tradeable at all*, and the model's single
objective is to **align expansion candles** across daily, 4H and 1H toward the same draw
on liquidity.

He begins with **phases of price delivery** (credited to the MMXM trader) as a closed
transition table: consolidation can only go to expansion; expansion can go to
retracement, reversal or consolidation; consolidation can *never* go directly to a
reversal or a retracement; every phase starts and ends with expansion. This makes the
next phase partly predictable — if price expanded and is now consolidating, it must
expand again.

He then gives **signatures** for each phase. Expansion: small wick, body made of
lower-timeframe expansion, lows rejected, highs displaced through, shallow retracements.
Continuation via consolidation: price engages the level, consolidates, and *fails to
manipulate* the consolidation high (engages it, prints opposing candles, closes back
through) — then continuation is trustworthy; the HTF signature of consolidation is an
inside bar. Continuation via retracement: deep pullbacks leaving **failure swings** with
no manipulation and no displacement back into the range; three consecutive candles or
one large candle into an objective is the HTF signature. Reversal: expansion into the
level met with expansion away (a V), confirmed by a CISD ("CSD" in the captions), and
always paired with an SMT.

The recurring doctrine is **trade away from manipulation, never away from failure
swings** — manipulation is the trigger for expansion. Where price has consolidated at a
level instead of reversing, he reverts to the consolidation high/low as the reversal
point (AMD reversal).

The second pillar is **wick size**. A small wick supports expansion; a large opposing
wick does not, and a candle with a large wick is not traded (except a daily reversal
candle, where he instead *adjusts targets* back inside the current day's range and does
not expect expansion past the open). He mechanises this with premium/discount: after a
reversal wick, the next candle's low should form in the **upper half** of the previous
candle's range for a bullish continuation; disrespecting that EQ would by definition
create a large wick.

Third: **candle profiles**. A bullish day opens low first, prints its low early and from
a key level, keeps a small wick and closes near its high. He will not accept a
low-of-day/high-of-day formed after roughly 10:30. Fourth: **4H session profiling** with
an if-then chain — if 2am manipulates then 6am continues; if 6am manipulates then 10am
continues; if 10am manipulates then 14:00 continues; and if the 4H candle you are inside
does not support expansion, you simply wait for the next candle open. Fifth: **driver
pairing** — a reversal put in *before* a news driver means the driver should expand away
(confirming narrative); no reversal before the driver means the driver should reverse
from the key level; a driver that fails to reverse from a key level invalidates the daily
profile.

He closes with SMT quality control: an SMT is **fake** when the asset that swept fails to
reverse, and he reads a **strength switch** between correlated assets (the weaker asset
momentarily out-delivers to catch up) as the tell.

### Concepts introduced (speaker's own terminology)
- "phases of price delivery", "signatures" (expansion / continuation / reversal)
- "reversal into expansion candle" vs "reversal candle"
- "failure swings" vs "manipulation" / "protected swing"
- "aligning expansion candles"
- "drivers" (8:30 news, 9:30 open, 10:00 news)
- "fake SMT" and "strength switch"
- "GXT" / "the lens" (his own indicator and community — not explained)

### Rules / conditions stated
- Consolidation → expansion only. Never consolidation → reversal, never consolidation → retracement.
- Continuation after consolidation is confirmed by a *failure* to manipulate the consolidation high (bullish case).
- Expansion is anticipated only after manipulation; never trade away from failure swings.
- A candle with a large opposing wick is not traded on the 4H; wait for the next candle open.
- On the daily, a large-wick reversal candle is traded only with targets adjusted back inside the day's range (do not expect expansion past the open).
- After a reversal wick, the next candle's low must form in the upper half of the prior candle's range (bullish).
- Bullish day must open low first; low of day must form at a key level and before ~10:30.
- If 2am manipulates → 6am continuation; 6am manipulates → 10am continuation; 10am manipulates → 14:00 continuation; 9:30 manipulates → 10am continuation.
- Reversal before a driver → driver continues. No reversal before driver → driver should reverse. Driver fails to reverse at a key level → daily profile invalidated.
- An SMT is fake if the sweeping asset does not itself reverse; expect the lagging asset to catch up.
- Every reversal is paired with an SMT.
- He does *not* require a candle-2 closure: either an LTF reversal inside C2, or C3 confirming, is enough. Applied only on daily and 4H.

### Examples walked through
- Gold: weekly opening low first inside a HTF consolidation, Tuesday reversal into expansion candle, weekly FVG target; 6am manipulation → 10am expansion; gold/silver SMT with a strength switch.
- Crude (CL/HO/RB): daily low formed from manipulation visible only on the correlated asset; 10am CISD entry; propulsion block noted.
- Silver vs gold: a *fake* SMT — silver sweeps the previous low, gold does not, strength switches, both continue; 6am manipulation → 10am continuation.
- YM/ES/NQ triad: strength ranked across the triad, 2am 4H reversal, 6am expansion, 8:30 driver expanding away from the low of day, entry paired with the 9:30 driver, target = daily swing point.
- ES: a New York reversal profile from a daily FVG (opened high, no established low of day), traded to break-even — presented as proof of concept, not a taken trade.

### Quotes
- "Price never goes from a consolidation to a reversal."
- "the whole goal of my model is to align expansion candles"

### Open questions / ambiguities
- "CSD" in the captions is almost certainly CISD (change in state of delivery); he never spells it out.
- The mechanical definition of the low he uses ("we can mechanically define with GXT") is deliberately withheld.
- The ~10:30 cutoff for a valid low/high of day is given as "probably around 10:30" — soft.
- "Small wick" is never quantified as a ratio; only the upper-half-EQ rule mechanises it partially.
- Which correlated asset defines the triad for a given instrument is shown by example only.

## Jokerszn | Daily Orderflow - ICT Daily Bias
(`JABOO4LYNjQ`, https://youtu.be/JABOO4LYNjQ, 2979s)

### What is actually taught
David (Jokerszn) walks a single multi-week EURUSD sequence in bar replay and derives his
whole daily-bias process from it. His bias has two inputs and only two: **liquidity
pools** — which for him are *only* old highs/lows (external) and HTF fair value gaps
(internal) — and **daily order flow confirming the draw**. If the draw is higher he
requires bullish daily order flow to take longs; when price reaches the HTF objective his
bias goes explicitly **neutral** and he stops trading until price shows its hand.

Order flow itself is defined by reaction to PD arrays: bullish order flow = bullish PD
arrays respected and bearish arrays to the left disrespected and inverted. He converts
this into a set of decidable tests. **Only daily closes count** — wicks exist only to take
liquidity from old highs and lows, never to define the narrative. A bullish PD array
should not see a *close below its mean threshold*, and ideally the reaction comes from the
upper half; the mirror applies to bearish arrays. A close below a bullish daily FVG turns
it into a bearish inversion level. Where an inversion FVG **overlaps** an opposing FVG the
overlap is a **balanced price range**, and he treats a large daily wick as a balanced price
range too (because on the LTF that is exactly what it is), using its EQ as a stop
reference.

He then adds **daily profiles**: expansion, retracement, consolidation. He anticipates
*which profile the next daily candle will have*, not just its direction, and he only really
wants to trade expansion profiles. Consolidation is expected when price has just traded
into a HTF level, when the previous few candles are overextended (he weights the last three
daily candles), or when price is at the **EQ of a dealing range**. A behavioural rule
follows: after three consecutive daily closes in the same direction he avoids trades in
that direction on the fourth day. He also refuses trades at range EQ entirely — only the
extremes.

When price is caught between two opposing PD arrays he calls it **stuck (trapped) order
flow** and waits for a daily close through one of them as the trigger.

Execution is fractal: take the daily PD array, refine it to an H1 POI, wait for a kill
zone (London or New York — he does not trade Asia), require a sweep of the opposing
liquidity, then a lower-timeframe change in order flow. He also marks the **Monday range**
every week and notes Mondays are typically consolidation or a retracement against daily
order flow. He walks a full H1 swing entry (stop above the EQ of the daily wick, ~25 pips,
2.8R first target) and an M15 entry (short at a balanced price range, 13-pip stop, first
target Monday lows ≈3.6R, add on the break of a bullish order block turned breaker, move
the first position to break-even).

### Concepts introduced (speaker's own terminology)
- "daily order flow confirming the draw"
- three "daily profiles": expansion / retracement / consolidation
- "stuck order flow" (order flow stuck between two PD arrays)
- "daily wick as a balanced price range"
- the three-consecutive-closes rule
- Monday range as a time reference

### Rules / conditions stated
- Bias inputs are only: old highs/lows (external liquidity) and HTF fair value gaps (internal liquidity).
- Bias requires daily order flow to confirm the draw; once the HTF objective is reached, bias becomes neutral.
- Use daily opens/closes only to determine bias; never wicks.
- A bullish PD array is weakened by a close below its mean threshold; a bearish array by a close above it.
- A close below a bullish daily FVG converts it to a bearish inversion level.
- The overlap of an inversion FVG and an opposing FVG is a balanced price range.
- Expect a consolidation profile after trading into a HTF level, after overextension in the last three candles, or at the EQ of a dealing range.
- After three consecutive same-direction daily closes, do not trade that direction on the fourth day.
- Never take trades at the EQ of a range; only at range extremes.
- Trade only during London or New York kill zones; Asia is skipped.
- Entry sequence: HTF POI → kill zone → sweep of opposing liquidity → LTF change in order flow → entry; add on the order-block-to-breaker close and move the first entry to break-even.

### Examples walked through
- EURUSD weekly: a weekly high resting inside a weekly volume imbalance aligned with a weekly order block (validated by a sweep + subsequent market structure break) as the bullish weekly objective.
- The transition to neutral once both weekly buy-side pools were taken, then the first daily close below a daily FVG turning bearish.
- Formation of a daily dealing range from swing low to swing high, the 0.5 as premium/discount, consolidation at the EQ.
- A daily balanced price range from an inversion FVG + bearish FVG overlap; run above the Monday high into it and rejection.
- H1 execution into PPI news: entry at the H1 balanced price range, stop at the EQ of the daily wick (~25 pips), targets at 2.8R and 3.5R.
- Following Tuesday: M15 entry after a London sweep of Asia + previous-week highs, 13-pip stop, targets Monday/Asia lows (~3.6R), scale-out to weekly lows.

### Quotes
- "I always only used the daily candle closes to determine my buyers"
- "order flow is stuck between two PD arays"

### Open questions / ambiguities
- "Overextension" of the last three daily candles is judged against an unspecified "average daily expansion".
- The dealing-range endpoints are chosen visually (last swing low to last swing high); no rule for which swings qualify.
- He says he prefers expansion profiles but never gives a positive test for anticipating one, only the conditions for consolidation.
- Kill-zone clock times are never given (only "London session"/"New York session").

## NickDoesFutures | Unicorn Model & Experiences With ICT
(`G44VpidBD_U`, https://youtu.be/G44VpidBD_U, 2052s)

### What is actually taught
Nick's talk is roughly half process/psychology and half a concrete weekly-to-execution
routine on ES/NQ futures. The autobiographical half argues that his strategy today is
essentially the strategy he had years ago; what changed was psychology, risk and
subtraction. He identifies over-learning (courses, rejection blocks, "noise") and social
media — Twitter especially — as the main causes of FOMO trades, and states repeatedly
that improvement came from *doing less*, not adding.

The process half is specific. On Sunday or Monday night he writes a **weekly game plan in
words, not just charts**: economic calendar first (what news is where), then what the
weekly and daily are reaching for, across ES, YM, NQ, DXY and bonds (bonds correlate with
indices, DXY inversely). Each day of the coming week is pre-labelled no-trade / low
probability / medium probability with the reason. His enumerated **low-probability
conditions** are: Monday with no news (the false move of the week), the day before CPI,
the day before NFP, the session before FOMC, a London session that aggressively takes both
sides of the Asia range, a Powell speech before/during, indecision on the daily bias
(trapped order flow — a daily FVG against a weekly FVG with no close through either), and a
HTF level already met before the session.

Execution is a New York AM kill-zone routine, 9:00–11:30, no new trades after 11:00. He
watches the 1H/4H and waits for price to tag one or two pre-marked points of interest
aligned with the daily bias — a high/low, an FVG, a volume imbalance, sometimes a new-week
opening gap. **If price does not tag the point of interest, he does not drop to the 5m or
15m at all** — explicitly as an anti-impulsiveness device. If it does tag, he drops to the
properly aligned timeframe: a 4H POI requires 15m alignment before a 1m execution; a 1H POI
maps to 5m execution.

The entry itself is the **Unicorn**: a short-term stop run (often the Asia range high/low)
followed by displacement leaving an FVG, with a breaker overlapping it. He sets limit
orders at the top/bottom of the breaker, stop at the top/bottom of the FVG candle or the
breaker itself — never at the swing low, because that ruins the R:R — and takes 80% at 2R,
the rest at 3R, sometimes leaving a runner at break-even. Target frequency is zero to three
trades a week; two winners ends the week.

Post-trade he journals a chart markup plus how he felt before, during and after — writing
the "after" section later in the day, on the theory that if a loss still stings twelve hours
later that is the thing to work on — followed by what he did well, what he did badly, and a
post-session note whether or not he traded.

### Concepts introduced (speaker's own terminology)
- "trapped order flow" (indecision on daily bias)
- "low probability day/session" checklist
- "the goal is not more trades it's more capital with less trades"
- "a model is more than a pattern on a chart" (risk protocol, conditions, times, journaling)
- Unicorn model / Unicorn entry (breaker + FVG overlap after a stop hunt)

### Rules / conditions stated
- Write the weekly plan in words before the week, including an if-this-then-that for each day.
- Trade only the New York AM kill zone, 9:00–11:30; no new entries after 11:00.
- Mark at most one or two HTF points of interest per session.
- If price does not tag a marked point of interest, do not drop to the 5m/15m chart.
- Timeframe pairing: 4H POI → 15m confirmation → 1m execution; 1H POI → 5m execution.
- Entry = stop run + displacement leaving an FVG + breaker; limit at the breaker edge.
- Stop at the FVG candle edge or the breaker; never at the swing low.
- Take 80% at 2R, remainder at 3R.
- Zero to three trades per week; stop after two winners.
- SMT with no displacement in the direction of expected order flow is meaningless.
- Journal every session, traded or not; write the emotional "after" entry later in the day.
- He will trade against his own weekly plan only after a clear daily shift or the invalidation of a HTF PD array.

### Examples walked through
- An April week: DXY with a weekly buy-side sweep and an indecisive candle between a weekly FVG pair → trapped order flow → no trade; bonds mirroring it; ES cleaner (low set, high run, displacement leaving a daily FVG plus a daily breaker) → the tradeable one.

## The MMXM Trader | Advanced ICT MMXM Lesson (Everything You Need To Know)
(`Ibw4saRtYMk`, https://youtu.be/Ibw4saRtYMk, 1090s)

### What is actually taught
The MMXM trader (introduced as one of TTrades' own mentors) presents the market maker
model in two layers: a macro *why* and a mechanical *how*.

The macro layer frames a market maker model as a **hedging program run by central banks**.
On the sell side of a bullish curve, as price declines, the central banks are building a
net long position — buying into sell-side liquidity — because their function is to *offer
liquidity* to large institutions and to retail, and offering liquidity requires hedging.
They break even on those positions; the profit comes from the extreme net-long position at
the very low (the smart money reversal). The buy side of the curve is where those hedges
are mitigated and sold on to the large funds — which is exactly why **mitigation blocks**
are the entry mechanism.

He then shows how to see this in **COT data**: use the individual currency futures contract
(e.g. Japanese yen futures), never the dollar index, take the last 12 months of COT data,
split that range in half, and check at a **new quarter** whether the commercials are above
or below the midpoint — net long or net short. The example shows commercials at their
highest net-long reading precisely at the daily chart's lowest low, at an old low in HTF
discount, at a quarterly boundary.

The mechanical layer defines an MMXM as **a lower-timeframe range paired with a
higher-timeframe key level**, where the range is the most obvious price swing whose high and
low connect. The phase sequence for a buy model is: original consolidation (range high) →
distribution 1 → distribution 2 → smart money reversal → low-risk buy (which only forms
after a short-term breaker) → accumulation 1 → accumulation 2. He states the second stage of
accumulation (or distribution) paired with SMT is the **silver bullet** — his highest
probability trade. He also states there are almost always **two stages of accumulation** on
the sell side of a buy model (and two stages on the buy side of a sell model), and that the
curve is defined by the lowest low in a buy model / highest high in a sell model.

Entries: extend the mitigation blocks (old up-close candles from the sell side of the curve)
across to the buy side of the curve, and require **a new PD array to form within the range of
the mitigation block** — an FVG or an opposing order block. That nested array is the entry,
not the block itself.

Finally he gives a fixed **timeframe alignment table** for internal↔external range plays:
monthly PD array with a daily MMXM, weekly with 4H, daily with 1H, 4H with 15m, H1 with 5m,
M15 with M1 — and stresses this table is for the internal/external pairing, not for the
ranges themselves.

### Concepts introduced (speaker's own terminology)
- market maker model as a central-bank "hedging program"
- "sell side of the curve" / "buy side of the curve"
- original consolidation → distribution 1/2 → smart money reversal → low-risk buy → accumulation 1/2
- "the silver bullet" = second stage of accumulation/distribution + SMT
- mitigation block paired with a nested PD array

### Rules / conditions stated
- Read COT on individual currency futures, never on the dollar index.
- Take the last 12 months of COT data, split the range in half, and read the commercials' position at a new quarter.
- An MMXM is always a LTF range paired with a HTF key level; a level is only a key level if it is in discount (for a buy model).
- The curve is split at the lowest low (buy model) / highest high (sell model).
- There are almost always two stages of accumulation on the sell side of a buy model.
- The low-risk buy only forms after a short-term breaker.
- Extend mitigation blocks from the sell side of the curve to the buy side; only take the entry if a PD array formed within the mitigation block's range.
- Timeframe alignment: monthly↔daily, weekly↔4H, daily↔1H, 4H↔15m, H1↔5m, M15↔M1.

### Open questions / ambiguities
- "Most obvious price swing where the high and low connect to each other" is the entire range-selection rule — it is visual, not mechanical.
- COT: whether "at a new quarter" means the first COT report of the quarter or the quarterly candle open is not stated.
