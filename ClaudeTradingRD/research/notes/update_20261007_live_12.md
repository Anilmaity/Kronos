### What is actually taught

**1. Pre-open daily read (stated levels are descriptive only; no prices are read out except BTC).**
- NASDAQ: into all-time highs after multiple days of expansion; a bullish day, but "not super
  interesting to be longing intraday over the previous highs". On the hourly he sees "kind of
  just failure swings up", which he calls "not quite bullish in terms of daily profile".
- ES took out equal highs, "still has a little ways to go".
- Gold: clean bearish day — took a low, reversed back into the range high. Still bearish,
  looking for the previous lows; views the whole thing as a consolidation that gets swept out
  to go lower. Later refines: the daily candle is "by definition ... a dogey candle", so it does
  not support expansion lower; he wants it back to the open and would rather wait for the
  daily close and trade it tomorrow.
- Oil: running out a low; if it closes bearish or fails to manipulate the low, target the next
  low down; if it reverses, failure swings higher — "more of a range trading".
- Dollar: back into a daily gap after a large run-up through previous highs; later notes a
  decent daily range already printed.
- BTC: bullish, continuation-expansion-retracement, looking for another move through the high
  into **91.2 / 91225** (the only explicit price level in the stream).

**2. Relative strength at the open (ES / NQ / YM) — "which one's lying".**
- Pre-open closures: NQ strongest, YM weakest. Framing rule stated as a question to chat: if
  indices are going higher, he wants to see **the weakest asset (YM) reverse off its low and
  frame something**; if they are going lower, it is most likely NQ (the strongest) forming a
  reversal off its high. Because of the daily profile (failure swings, already expanded over
  highs) he favours the downside.
- YM hits the low while NQ goes into the high: "Now you have to decide which one's lying."
- How to decide: "You don't know. You just kind of wait for closures to tell you" — wait for
  the first 5, maybe 15 minutes. "They could both be lying and then you end up with a
  consolidation."
- Live, the strength **switched three times** (ES held up when it should be lowest; then NQ
  weakest/YM strongest; then YM weak/NQ strong again). He reads the repeated switching as a
  chop session.
- Outcome ~10:00: "YM reversed, which is that first option" — the weakest asset reversed, so
  ES can continue higher, "I would just be bullish for the remainder of the day". He did **not**
  trade it, and earlier said he sticks with his bias (downside) regardless of chat.

**3. Stop / gap and timeframe stepping (live, 5m -> 3m).** On the 5-minute there was no fair
value gap except one far above; "I wouldn't want to put a stop all the way up there. So you go
to the 3 minute, which is your next lower time frame." He half-names it ("Immediate rebalance or
something") — where there is no gap you go down one more timeframe and then you have a gap.
Rule implied: if no gap on the execution timeframe, the trend continuing means "that high should
remain" (the only gap's high is the protected reference).

**4. Open-uncertainty waiting ladder (explicitly labelled "a little lesson").**
- "If 9:30 move doesn't get a continuation, it's chopped. Wait for 10:00 a.m."
- "Entry prior to 9:30. Use M15 or greater after 9:30 ... any time frame works." (He says he
  typed this in chat with a winky face; the caption run-on makes the split ambiguous — most
  plausible reading: pre-9:30 entries need M15+; after 9:30 any timeframe.)
- "If you don't know what is going on ... Just wait five minutes. If you still don't, wait for
  15. If you still don't, wait for 10:00 a.m. ... just wait longer."
- He would rather wait for "a new 4-hour open" (i.e. 10:00) given the forming structure.

**5. Why the 4-hour/15-minute model is his favourite.** Most people "get chopped up" on lower
timeframes despite having the right overall direction; 4H/15m helps them not get chopped.

**6. EQ as the wick-size test (Q&A).** "The EQ is what we use to mechanically determine what is a
small enough wick for expansion." If the wick stays above EQ it can still expand; if price goes
all the way down below it, "it's not that it can't be bullish, but it's not going to expand" —
likely to return to the daily open or intraday highs.

**7. Candle numbering (Q&A).** C2 is always the swing high/low; C1 is arbitrary, just prior;
C3 follows C2; C4 follows C3. Every C2 is a swing point but he is only interested in "a candle 2
closure or a candle 3 closure at a point of interest". Worked chart: one C2 that formed a high
but was not a closure at a POI (ignored) vs one that "sweeps out a high in a gap and closes back
below" (interested).

**8. Gold correlation / FX selection.** For gold he uses gold-euro and gold-pound for divergences,
"but you can also compare it with gold and silver. But I prefer gold and silver on the higher time
frame"; his indicator auto-checks SMT on the two he configured. For EU vs GU with a bearish dollar:
pound has more strength off the lows than euro, judged on the higher-timeframe candles, so focus
pound. Gold scalp back into the range would need refining in the "T-spot"; target is the hourly gap
(where candles do not overlap).

**9. All-time highs.** "How can we trade all-time highs? It's the exact same if you were trading
two weeks ago. You guys overthink alltime highs so much."

**10. Misc.** Strategy building: use an existing strategy (his free fractal-model course) or blend
concepts yourself; ask specific questions. Bias discipline: "I stick with my bias", won't flip on a
chop. He is "still in some swing positions" (no detail).
