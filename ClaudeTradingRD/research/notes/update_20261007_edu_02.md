## How to Trade London Using TTrades Fractal Model  (tUbrCewFdCU, 1038s)

### What is actually taught
A London-session application of the daily / 4-hour / 15-minute fractal model.
1. **Daily bias first.** Simplest route: a daily candle 2 or candle 3 closure (PDF: expansion,
   retracement, internal-to-external, C2 closure at an FVG; C3 closing over the series of
   down-close candles = new protected swing, so C4 also expected). Other routes he mentions:
   phases of price, daily closures, EQ.
2. **Let the daily wick form, trade the body** — using the 4-hour candle / 4-hour profile.
   For London specifically he wants the reversal in Asia or early London: "usually the 2200
   candle or the 2:00 a.m. candle" (4-hour candles, New York time, futures grid). Forex is
   "off by an hour due to their higher time frame opens".
3. **15-minute execution.** Confirm the swing with a CISD (find the low, the series that made
   it, did price close over it). Then wait for the **intra-candle CISD** that shows the 4-hour
   wick has formed, and get onside; stop on the 15m protected swing.
4. **Holding.** Expansion candles have small wicks, so hold toward the high/close of the
   4-hour candle, "and potentially even the next" — he explicitly calls this a time-based exit.
   Or take 2R.
5. **Kill zones irrelevant.** 19:00, 22:00, 01:00, 02:00 are all fine as long as the
   higher-timeframe wicks have formed. Same model works in Asia and New York; this is "my
   preferred method for trading London".

### Worked examples
- **ES (daily → 4H → 15m), bearish.** Daily: range high taken, trading to range low; the candle
  is read as consolidation/retracement with SMT vs YM off the previous high → continuation,
  draw = previous day low (NQ also to its range low). 4H: an early internal swing he does not
  trust; then an aggressive move lower that swept its previous candle and closed below but
  **failed to take out the earlier high** — "a valid candle to closure ... but it is a failure
  swing". Tradeable, but he would prefer SMT and "must demand more confirmation on the lower
  time frame"; failure swings close to the reversal are "pretty concerning", further away
  acceptable. 15m: end-of-London / early-NY aggressive reversal forms a protected swing; entry on
  the open of candle 3, stop on the protected swing; target previous day low — hit.
- **Gold (daily/monthly → 4H → 15m), bullish.** Daily: expansion, retracement into an FVG →
  target previous high. 4H: candle 2 closure with "a little bit of a wick", but daily expansion
  candles run "around 100 points" and the day is halfway through, so range remains. 15m: C2
  reversal with a very deep run — "I'll only ever look to trade away from something like this
  if we have SMT on the low". Continuation with SMT formed before the new 4H open → positional
  entry, or wait for the next continuation (taken). 2R ≈ back to the daily open / daily EQ; hit;
  then held as the 4H candles expanded. Day was a reversal day out of the FVG ("more advanced
  trading candle two"), ~100-point range.
- **GBPUSD (daily → 4H → 15m), bullish.** Daily sweep of previous low with C2 closure. 4H: price
  sweeps into an FVG and forms a candle 2 closure respecting the upper half of the daily range.
  15m: CISD confirming the low of the day plus a continuation; "ideal candle to closure" when the
  closure and protected swing coincide; target previous day high at 2R. A second entry: London
  opening within a continuation after a deep opposing run, stop at the low, 2R hit almost
  immediately ("early London, late Asia type of move").
- **Oil futures, bullish London reversal.** Daily C2 (V-shape) with hourly CISD. 4H: 22:00 did not
  form the swing; London forms the low of the day. On the 15m there is no series of candles to
  close through, so he uses the V-shape and seeks a continuation / positional entry. 2R reached
  only after consolidation and a run of previous highs; he admits it is not a great example. A
  10:00 a.m. continuation into an expansion candle is mentioned as a further trade.

## Understanding Dealing Ranges in Trading (Get Better Risk Reward)  (yb3Te7tiGRk, 915s)

### What is actually taught
- **Setup:** Gann box ("gone box") or 0.5 Fibonacci; levels 0.5 and 1, backgrounds lowered.
- **Definition (swing-anchored, model-first):** only with a valid fractal model. Take the C2
  low being traded away from; on the **higher-timeframe** candles walk back to "the swing high
  that made this swing low" — "this is not always going to be candle one". Range = that high
  to the C2 low. The far end is the overall HTF target, the near end the invalidation.
- **Use:** longs in discount (lower 50%), shorts in premium (upper 50%), so 2R is available
  before the dealing-range extreme is taken; entering near the target risks a new phase of
  price. "It's okay if I'm entering right around that EQ point." Hard floor: "As I get to 25% of
  the range or lower, that is where I'm not really looking for shorts".
- **Not the same as his EQ concept:** candle 3 still has to respect the EQ of candle 2 for
  expansion; the dealing range is the larger frame for entry location.
- **Nesting:** multiple dealing ranges inside each other; best R = entering in the premium of
  both the lower-timeframe and the higher-timeframe range; take partials at the inner target
  (≥2R) and hold runners to the outer target.
- Without the indicator: draw it on the daily, then view on the hourly.
- Side benefit: staying out of the near-target half "keeps you out of consolidation moves"
  (consolidation = low of the range back to premium).

### Worked examples (no prices stated)
1. Daily/hourly short: C2 closure + SMT; range from C2 high to the higher-timeframe swing low that
   made it; shorts in premium/EQ give room to target; move lower follows.
2. 15m/1m long: from the low back to the swing high that made it; target ends are only failure
   swings ("ideally you want to see price reach out the failure swings" but swings define it
   mechanically); highs run. Then, at the top after the target is taken, longs "don't make
   sense"; a new bearish fractal setup gets its own range; shorts in premium work, one at the
   range low gets chopped before the target.
3. Bearish range: setup around EQ works; a short in discount would not have offered 2R.
4. Long range: longs around EQ / discount targeting failure-swing highs; a dip back into discount
   sets up again.
5. Hourly + 5-minute nested ranges: short in premium of the 5m range and of the daily/hourly
   range, stop on the protected high, 2R at the first target, runner to the outer target; a
   continuation short deep in the range gives less than 1:1 to the first target.

### Transcript hygiene
"gone box" = Gann box; "shorten the premium" = short in the premium; "to R" / "too R" = 2R;
"riskto-reward" run-together.

## How to Trade Breakouts (Failure to Manipulate)  (8yt3jn8L6O4, 686s)

### What is actually taught
- Default at a range extreme: expect a **reversal** (sweep, aggressive candle back in, trade to
  the other side). He does not trust a CISD/breakout at the extreme.
- **Failure to manipulate** = price "takes out a high or a low and fails to form a reversal";
  operationally "price failing to form a change in the state of delivery in the other
  direction". Then expect continuation through the level.
- Entry: never the breakout candle itself — "first wait for a continuation to form" (retest of
  the old range, higher low / lower high, new protected swing, candle 2 closure after a retest of
  a gap). Stop on that swing; 2R.
- Must be paired with a higher-timeframe bias / reason ("It's not used alone as a model"); his
  preference is hourly or 30-minute paired with the daily.
- Wait for closes ("Don't try to make decisions prior to the candle closure").
- Study by hindsight on normal charts, not replay.

### Worked examples
1. Range with equal highs and equal lows; aggressive move lower, retracement wicks reject the old
   range → read as expansion + retracement → continuation lower.
2. 15m low as level, 1m execution: reversal would need a close over the down-close series into
   the low; instead a bearish continuation / new bearish protected swing forms → short, 2R hit.
3. Hourly/30m: after running a previous high, consolidation; each new high fails to produce a
   bearish CISD ("can't close below here" three times) and closes aggressively bullish → breakout,
   retest into a gap, candle 2 closure, long with stop on the low.
4. Last example: after a large expansion up and consolidation, a sweep of the range high prints a
   **mechanical bearish CISD**, but the higher-timeframe candle is a candle 2 closure with
   **bullish SMT**, so he reads it as a bullish failure to manipulate; bullish continuation with a
   new protected swing formed **before** the HTF open → positional entry around the opening price,
   stop on the protected low, candle 3 expansion.

### Transcript hygiene
"candle to closure" = candle 2 closure; otherwise clean.
