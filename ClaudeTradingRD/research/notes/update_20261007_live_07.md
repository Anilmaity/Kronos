## New York Open Live Q&A  (9YVBe-20Hdg, https://youtu.be/9YVBe-20Hdg, 4765s)

### Context of the session
- FOMC rate decision later that day ("70% chance they raise rates"). He expects
  range-bound trade before it and says he does not trade through CPI/FOMC/NFP.
- Indices: daily is an "inside day inside day" → consolidation; monthly the same. NQ the
  strongest, YM the weakest; SMT noted. Daily profile shows an open-low going higher but
  "doesn't go into anything relevant" (no low swept), so not trusted.
- Dollar bullish on the daily; oil cooling off; gold choppy at first.

### What is actually taught (mechanical)
1. **Weakest-asset reversal gate.** For the strong asset (NQ) to go higher, the weakest (YM)
   must reverse at its low, giving SMT. He frames it as "is YM lying or is NQ lying". YM went
   well below the low without reacting → called a **failure to manipulate**, expected draw =
   YM previous day low, with the other indices expected to be dragged lower. In the end he
   said it "either chops around or I'm just wrong" and declined the downside for himself.
2. **No-bias day = reaction game.** With no bias, wait until **9:45** so the first 15-minute
   candle has formed, then react; anything traded is a scalp (≈2R on 1m), not held for
   expansion. Only two scalps on the day: ES short (around the 9:30-9:45 1-minute) and NQ
   long. He took neither ("not really that interested in scalping"). In tension with his
   restated rule "have a bias before you look to trade" (otherwise pattern trading).
3. **CISD filters.** A visible 15m NQ CISD lower was ignored because (a) not paired with a
   swing point and (b) against the asset's bias. Volatility is *not* part of the CISD
   definition; the exclusion is CISDs formed inside consolidation. Protection against bad
   CISDs = wait for continuation: retrace into the FVG and close through; on the example
   price "reaches up into the gap, fails to close, never closes" → no continuation.
4. **30-second entry procedure.** Swing point → CISD → do not trade the reversal → wait for
   continuation → enter → stop on protected swing (indicator marks it) → 2R.
5. **Liquidity = swing points; relevant levels = last three days.** Go back three days, mark
   the daily highs/lows (indicator shows four candles incl. today); ask which side is the draw.
   Rationale for PDH: shorts from yesterday have stops at PDH or a swing high; once above PDH,
   "you cannot short yesterday and be in profit".
6. **Kill zones not used.** The model is built on HTF candles; a 6:45 entry is not skipped
   for being outside 7:00. Favourite trades 6:00-8:00 a.m. (15m positioning before the open).
   For limited time: 9-11 a.m. Also: no BOS/MSS/CHoCH (CISD only, after MSS in 2022), no
   quarterly theory, no volume profile.
7. **Top-down.** Daily bias → 4H/1H structure (FVG, EQ of the daily wick, swing points) → let
   the daily wick form → align a fractal model.
8. **"Let the wick form" is not mystical time.** Time only gives the HTF candles (9:30, 9:45,
   10:00). Wick formed = sweep of the previous candle, then expansion and close back.
9. **MMXM mapped onto the fractal model** (YM): smart money reversal = swing point; original
   consolidation = the swing low that made the swing high (target); distribution legs =
   continuations; target 4H swing low / PDL. "Same thing without understanding MMXM."
10. **Reverse engineering vs backtesting** — defined procedurally for the first time: not
    candle-by-candle replay guessing, but looking at completed expansions in hindsight and
    asking how you could have got on side, which TF gave reversal + continuation, was the R:R
    enough, where was the draw. He says he built his model this way over ~2 years.
11. YM is used mostly as a relative-strength indicator, not traded. He takes one trade and
    logs off.
