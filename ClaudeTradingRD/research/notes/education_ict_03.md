## What is Liquidity? - ICT Concepts (`Njs-eRpeP9Q`, https://youtu.be/Njs-eRpeP9Q, 287s)

### What is actually taught

The most elementary liquidity lesson in the corpus, and deliberately so — he opens by saying
the concept is best taught through a pile of examples rather than a definition, then gives
the definition in one line anyway: all liquidity is is where people's stops are sitting, and
that is found at the most obvious highs and lows. Everything else follows from asking, at
each level, *who is trapped and where did they put their stop*. Above an obvious high sit the
stops of everyone who got short — buy-side liquidity. Below an obvious low sit the stops of
everyone who got long — sell-side liquidity.

Two refinements make the lesson more than a naming exercise. The first is a **granularity
rule**: you do not always have to run the whole range of stops. Inside a range he asks
whether someone who got long mid-range would really put a stop at the far low, answers
"probably not", and marks the internal obvious highs and lows as pools in their own right.
The second is a **sequencing rule** that survives into the current method: taking the pool is
not the trade. Once those are taken, that is when you look for your setup — the sweep is a
precondition for the entry, not the entry.

The video then walks the timeframe ladder he was using at the time. Liquidity is mapped on
the 15-minute chart (he says explicitly he finds it easier there than on the 5-minute), and
execution drops to the 1-minute, where he names the entry model: a **2022 mentorship setup**.
Its four parts are stated compactly — a short-term high is put in, price runs it, displacement
follows over it, a fair value gap is left behind — and then a fifth condition is added that is
easy to miss: a fib is drawn across the displacement range and the fair-value-gap entry must
be in **discount** of that range. He offers the cruder alternative honestly ("Could you just
buy this right here? Yes, but that's also more risky"), which is the clearest statement in the
unit of *why* the extra structure exists.

The closing example is about unfinished business: two clusters of relatively equal highs and
lows, neither of which has been hit. His answer to "what does that mean" is that they become
targets for later. One is run; the other is carried forward and used as the target of a
subsequent short.

### Concepts introduced

- **Liquidity = resting stops at the most obvious highs and lows.**
- **Buy-side / sell-side liquidity** by trapped-participant reasoning.
- **Internal range liquidity** (the mid-range stop), reasoned from trader behaviour rather
  than named as a term.
- **Sweep-then-setup sequencing** — the pool being taken is the cue, not the trade.
- **Relatively equal highs / lows as carried-forward targets.**
- **The 2022 mentorship setup** — short-term high → run → displacement → FVG → fib discount.
- **Displacement** and **structure shifting** (both used, neither defined).
- **15-minute for mapping, 1-minute for execution.**

### Rules / conditions stated

- Mark the most obvious highs and lows; stops rest beyond them.
- Also mark internal obvious highs and lows inside a range — you do not have to run the whole
  range of stops.
- A pool that has been taken stops being a target; look left for the next untaken one.
- Only after the pool is taken do you look for the setup.
- Map liquidity on the 15-minute (he states a personal preference over the 5-minute).
- Drop to the 1-minute for the entry sequence.
- The entry sequence: short-term high formed → run it → displacement over it → fair value gap.
- Draw a fib on the displacement range; the entry must sit in discount.
- Buying the sweep directly is permitted but explicitly more risky.
- Untaken relatively equal highs/lows are targets for later.

### Examples walked through

Instrument is never named; charts only. (1) A 5-minute chart where the obvious highs and lows
are marked, the buy-side above is taken, then attention moves left to a cluster of equal
highs that becomes the next target. (2) The same market on the 15-minute to show it is
"easier to see", with a range whose internal highs and lows are marked from the mid-range-stop
argument. (3) A 1-minute drill-down after both sets of stops are run: short-term high, run,
displacement, fair value gap outlined, fib drawn from the displacement range, entry confirmed
to be in discount. (4) Two untaken clusters, one run, the other carried forward as the target
for a short.

### Quotes

- "All liquidity is is where people stops are sitting"
- "Once those are taken, that's when you look for your setup"
- "Could you just buy this right here? Yes, but that's also more risky"

### Open questions / ambiguities

- "Most obvious" is the entire selection rule for a level and is never bounded — no swing
  strength, no separation, no lookback.
- Whether a wick through a pool counts as taken, or a close beyond is required, is not raised.
- "Displacement" carries no threshold, so the 2022-mentorship sequence is not decidable
  without importing a definition from outside — which is why that concept is filed
  `underspecified`.
- The fib anchors on the "displacement range" are not specified (swing-to-swing? the
  displacement candle's own range?), nor is the level that divides discount from premium.
- No stop placement and no target rule is given for the entry model itself.
- The claim that stops rest at these prices is asserted from trader psychology, never
  evidenced — as everywhere in the corpus.

## What is Rebalance? - ICT Concepts (`opYUBJ8IFgg`, https://youtu.be/opYUBJ8IFgg, 137s)

### What is actually taught

The shortest video in the unit and the most mechanically clean thing in it. Rebalancing is
defined exhaustively as **two** cases, and he says so up front: there are two ways a fair
value gap is rebalanced, one before it is created and one after it is created.

The **after** case is the familiar one and is taught first because it makes more sense: a fair
value gap exists, price comes back down and closes it, and it is now rebalanced. He then adds
the consequence that makes the concept do work rather than just name a state — after you
rebalance something there is not an imbalance there, so price tends to reverse, or has the
possibility of reversing. The hedge is his, not mine.

The **before** case is the genuinely useful half and is, he says, what the majority of candles
do. Walking three candles at 10:40, 10:45 and 10:50 he shows price moving quickly, the third
candle opening away from the first candle's range such that a fair value gap *would* exist if
price simply continued — and then price first comes down, balances that area out, ensures no
fair value gap is created, and only then moves higher. This is the mechanism that explains why
so many strong moves have a small pullback at the start of each candle rather than being
uninterrupted. He states that this pre-creation form is what he focuses on **on the daily
chart**, and shows the daily equivalent: the candle opens, comes down, rebalances the prior
day, then moves higher.

### Concepts introduced

- **Rebalance**, defined as exactly two cases.
- **Post-creation rebalance** — price returns and closes the gap; no imbalance left; reversal
  becomes possible.
- **Pre-creation rebalance** — the would-be gap is balanced before it can form.
- **The claim that pre-creation rebalancing is what "the majority of candles" do.**
- **Daily-chart application** of the pre-creation form.

### Rules / conditions stated

- Two cases only: rebalanced before creation, or rebalanced after creation.
- Post-creation: price trades back and closes the fair value gap → rebalanced.
- After a post-creation rebalance there is no imbalance in that area, so price tends to
  reverse or has the possibility of reversing.
- Pre-creation: candle 3 opens leaving a would-be gap; price trades back through that area
  before continuing, so no fair value gap is created.
- He focuses the pre-creation form on the daily chart.

### Examples walked through

(1) An intraday chart with two or three stacked fair value gaps, all of which price returns
to and closes — the post-creation case. (2) A three-candle zoom at 10:40 / 10:45 / 10:50
showing the third candle opening with a prospective gap, then coming back to balance it before
continuing higher. (3) The same mechanic on two daily candles: open, come down, rebalance,
move higher.

### Quotes

- "there are two ways a fair value gap is rebalanced"
- "after you rebalance something there's not an imbalance there so price tends to reverse"

### Open questions / ambiguities

- "Closes the fair value gap" is never resolved to a full fill versus consequent
  encroachment; the examples all show a full close but no rule is stated.
- Whether a wick or a body must do the rebalancing is not addressed.
- "Tends to reverse **or has the possibility of reversing**" is hedged in the source itself —
  no probability, no follow-through condition, nothing that makes it a signal.
- "The majority of candles" is a frequency claim with no measurement behind it. It is
  directly testable and worth testing.
- Vocabulary drift: the updated playlist re-records the fair-value-gap material and puts most
  of the post-fill logic under **inversion** rather than rebalance. No contradiction is
  stated, but the word largely disappears.

## Why You Should Use 8:30 & Midnight Open (EST) - ICT Concepts (`b6yvRKf8haE`, https://youtu.be/b6yvRKf8haE, 469s)

### What is actually taught

The densest of the three and the one most sharply superseded. Two levels are marked — the
**midnight open** and the **08:30 open** — and the entire video is about the three jobs they
do. He is explicit that the source of the idea is ICT's 2022 mentorship and core content.

**Job one: a directional filter, expressed as premium and discount.** Below either level is a
discount and is where longs are looked for; above either level is a premium and is where
shorts are looked for. Then the refinement that is the most decidable statement in the whole
unit: below **both** levels is a **deep discount**, above **both** is a **deep premium**. That
is a two-line boolean, and it is the location he wants the entry to occur in.

**Job two: pivots.** On days that are not clean he shows price bouncing around the midnight
open repeatedly, and gives a close-based read: failing to close back above the level after
trading below it signals more downside, and vice versa. He strings four of these together on
one chart ("fail to close below this 830 open signaling more upside… aggressive displacement
down, fail to close here, can't displace back up, close below here, more downside").

**Job three: targets.** After an aggressive sell-off or breakout away from the levels, the
same two opens become the objective for the return move. In the final two examples he uses
exactly that: the low of the day is put in, and the question "where can you likely target"
is answered with the midnight open.

Underneath all three sits the daily-profile frame: open-high-low-close is a sell day,
open-low-high-close is a buy day, and the leg to be captured is the **Judas swing** that forms
the day's opposite extreme. The video's real contribution is that it anchors that leg **in
price** rather than only in time — on a sell day the Judas swing is normally above the 08:30
and midnight open, on a buy day below them. That pairing is what makes the whole thing
operational: you know both roughly when the manipulation happens and roughly where it has to
be.

Two smaller but valuable things. First, the word **"aggressive"** — which blocks several
concepts elsewhere in the library — is used here in apposition with displacement: "had an
aggressive move or displacement above hives". Across the rest of the video "aggressive" only
ever attaches to displacement, sell-offs and directional moves as an intensifier, never as a
test with its own criteria. That is not a definition, but it is the corpus's clearest evidence
of what the word is doing. Second, he shows a degraded day honestly: a gap down followed by
chop, no clean shape, "although it isn't super clean some days you can still use it to build
a framework".

### Concepts introduced

- **Midnight open and 08:30 open** as the two marked levels.
- **Discount / premium measured against those levels** (orthodox usage — buy the discount).
- **Deep discount / deep premium** = below both / above both.
- **Line in the sand / point of interest / target** — his three names for the same levels.
- **Judas swing** anchored above/below the two opens.
- **Daily profile OHLC (sell day) vs OLHC (buy day).**
- **Levels as pivots**, with the fail-to-close continuation read.
- **Levels as targets** after an aggressive move.
- **"Aggressive move or displacement"** used as one term.
- **New York kill zone** and **London open / Asia low** named in passing, with no hours.
- **Order block / mitigation block** forming where price fails to close back over a level.

### Rules / conditions stated

- Mark the midnight open and the 08:30 open.
- Longs below either level; shorts above either level.
- Below both = deep discount; above both = deep premium; that is where the entry is sought.
- On a sell day expect the day's high (the Judas swing) above both levels; on a buy day expect
  the day's low below both.
- Look for the lower-timeframe setup, and for PD arrays being reached into, in that area.
- Fail-to-close back through a level after trading beyond it → continuation in the direction
  of travel.
- After an aggressive move away, target the two opens on the way back.
- On unclean days use the levels as pivots and build a framework rather than expecting the
  daily shape.

### Examples walked through

Index futures throughout (never named on the transcript). (1) London takes the Asia low, then
an aggressive move/displacement above the highs → look for a move below the opens; price goes
below the midnight open, then below the 08:30 into the New York kill zone (his deep discount)
and reaches the fair-value-gap draw. (2) A textbook sell day: open, high, low, close; the only
short entry is above the opens, either the London deviation and midnight retest or the later
deviation of the range. (3) An unclean gap-down chop day used to demonstrate the pivot and
fail-to-close readings, and where a mitigation block forms below the midnight open before new
lows. (4) A pivot-heavy choppy range into the New York AM: retest of midnight open, aggressive
move down, deviation above the 08:30 that does not reach the midnight open, then the sell-off.
(5) A classic buy day where the manipulation sits below both opens. (6) A news-driven day
where 08:30 drops price into a fair value gap, the manipulation is below the midnight open,
the 08:30 is retested repeatedly, and after the low of day the midnight open is the upside
target — reached, rejected, then a deep-premium deviation before an aggressive drop.
(7) "Last Friday": failure to close below the midnight open in Asia/London, the 08:30 news
drop, and the two opens used as the return targets.

### Quotes

- "if you're looking for Longs you want below the a30 or midnight open"
- "if you're below both of those it is a deep discount"
- "had an aggressive move or displacement above hives"

### Open questions / ambiguities

- **No timezone is spoken anywhere in the transcript body** — only the video title carries
  "(EST)". The corpus elsewhere fixes bare clock times to New York.
- No rule for when the midnight open governs and when the 08:30 does. "Either" defines the
  plain case and "both" the deep case, and that is the whole of it.
- No depth requirement: a tick below both levels counts the same as a large excursion.
- "Fail to close" is applied to intraday candles whose timeframe is never named.
- "Aggressive" is never given a test of its own; it reads as apposition with displacement, but
  whether it is a strength grade or a synonym cannot be settled from this video.
- No entry, stop or target *sizing* is given — the levels locate the trade, they do not size it.
- The 08:30 level is a US-data-release open; nothing is offered for other asset classes.
- **The supersession is the main caveat.** The later canon (`ttfm-minimal-toolkit`,
  `entry-time-window`) lists the midnight open among the tools he no longer uses, and the
  updated premium/discount video reverses the orthodox usage taught here. Both readings are
  preserved in the concept files with this one flagged as the earlier.

## Cross-video observations

- **This unit is a snapshot of the pre-fractal-model method.** Not one of the three videos
  mentions candle 2 / candle 3, CISD, protected swings, expansion candles or the weekly
  profile. The scaffolding is instead ICT-standard: liquidity → displacement → fair value gap
  → discount entry, timed off session opens. Anyone diffing the method over time should treat
  these three as the "before" column.
- **Two of the three are directly contested by the newer canon**, and the concept files say so
  in `ambiguities` rather than smoothing it: the midnight/8:30 framework (later dropped from
  the toolkit) and orthodox premium/discount (later explicitly inverted). The liquidity and
  rebalance material is *not* contradicted — it is simply more elementary than its
  re-recording.
- **The one thing this unit adds that the newer material does not have** is a price anchor for
  the manipulation leg. Elsewhere the Judas swing / opposing run is anchored only in time
  (midnight, 08:30, 09:30); here it is also anchored in price (beyond both opens), and the
  deep-discount / deep-premium boolean makes that anchor mechanical.
- **Nothing in this unit touches the library's top gaps.** No wick-size ratio, no kill-zone
  hours (the New York kill zone is named once, with no times), no CISD closing-price rule. The
  word "aggressive" is the only blocked term this unit moves at all, and it moves it by
  showing the word is an intensifier rather than a concept.
- **Terms used but never defined here**: displacement, structure shift, PD array, order block,
  mitigation block, kill zone, fair value gap (used from the first video onward but only
  defined in the updated playlist), point of interest, draw on liquidity.
