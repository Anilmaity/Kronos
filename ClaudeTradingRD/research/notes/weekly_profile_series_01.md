## Weekly Profile for Daily Bias - Classic Expansion (`Qt7Ek4keMzs`, https://youtu.be/Qt7Ek4keMzs, 751s)

### What is actually taught

This is the anchor video of the series and it establishes the machinery every later video
reuses. A "classic expansion" week is a week whose weekly candle is a directional /
expansion candle. The workflow starts one timeframe up: first form a bias — this is said
to generally arise after a reversal candle on the weekly timeframe, or from an expectation
that price should expand in one direction. With bias in hand you then read the daily
candles as they print, looking for Monday **or** Tuesday to form the low of the week
(bullish) or the high of the week (bearish), followed by multiple days of expansion into a
key level or until the average range is covered, with Friday "capping off" the weekly range.

The mechanical trigger is his fractal-model vocabulary applied to the daily chart: the
Monday-or-Tuesday extreme should print as a **candle 2 closure**. That alone is not enough
— the crucial second step, repeated in every example, is to drop to the **hourly** chart
and check that a change in the state of delivery exists *inside that daily candle's wick*.
The CISD is what "confirms this wick has formed" so that the rest of the week can be traded
away from it. Once confirmed, Wednesday is treated as candle 3 and is tradeable as a
continuation; and if candle 3 closes well, Thursday (candle 4) is tradeable too — giving
the two-to-three days of expansion the profile is named for.

He is explicit that this is not a prediction exercise: "the goal with weekly profile is not
to predict is this going to be a Monday low of the week, a Tuesday low of the week" — you
let the low form, then trade. If a day closes as a strong opposing expansion candle, you
simply let another day print rather than declaring the extreme in.

The video also teaches when to stand aside, which is treated as part of the profile rather
than an afterthought: after three days of expansion, or after two very large expansion
days, or once the higher-timeframe objectives are already hit, he will not look for a
further continuation ("this is something that I would avoid trading", "that is a great time
to avoid price"), because a new phase of price is likely. The final example deliberately
shows an imperfect week (Monday actually made the low but Tuesday also reversed) to make
the closing point: the profiles are not templates to be matched, they are a way of locating
the weekly extreme and then trading continuations away from it.

### Concepts introduced

- **Classic expansion (weekly profile)** — the profile name.
- **Candle 2 closure** ("candle to closure") on the daily as the weekly-extreme signature.
- **Hourly change in the state of delivery** as the confirmation of a daily wick.
- **Candle 3 / candle 4** = Wednesday / Thursday continuation days ("my fractal model").
- **Higher timeframe key level / objective**, **average range**.
- **Protected swing** (used, not defined here).
- **V-shape reversal**, **retracement vs reversal** on the higher timeframe.
- **"Avoid price"** — his term for standing aside after exhaustion.
- **TGIF** — named here as the Friday cap of the weekly range, deferred to its own video.

### Rules / conditions stated

- Establish a directional bias first; the profile is applied underneath an existing bias.
- Bullish: the low of the week forms on Monday or Tuesday. Bearish: the high of the week
  forms on Monday or Tuesday.
- That extreme day must print a candle 2 closure on the daily.
- Go to the hourly and require a CISD inside that daily candle's wick before trading away
  from it. If the closure through has not happened yet, it is "not confirmed" — wait.
- With the extreme confirmed, trade Wednesday (candle 3) as a continuation.
- If candle 3 closes well / closes strong, also trade Thursday (candle 4).
- Expansion is expected for 2–3 days, generally Tue–Wed–Thu or Wed–Thu–Fri.
- Do not seek a further continuation once three days have expanded, or once the HTF
  objective has been hit, or after two very large expansion days.
- Ideal but not required: Monday makes a shallow opposing run of the weekly range.
- If the current day's closure does not let you call the weekly extreme, let another day print.

### Examples walked through

Instrument is never named on screen in this transcript (charts are described, not labelled);
timeframes are daily for the profile and hourly for the CISD check.
1. A HTF retracement (prior highs swept, then a V-shape reversal off an order block back
   with trend). Monday prints a bearish expansion candle → wait. Tuesday prints an
   aggressive candle 2 closure plus a close through a series of down-close candles. Hourly
   CISD present. Wednesday continues, Thursday continues, HTF objectives reached → Friday
   is declared un-tradeable.
2. Equal highs left as target. Monday opposes the bias (desired). Tuesday gives a candle 2
   closure inside a fair value gap / opposing candle; hourly CISD present. Wednesday sweeps
   a small consolidation then expands; Thursday expands. After two large days a third is
   not anticipated.
3. A **Monday** low-of-week variant: Friday expanded into an objective, Monday opens, makes
   the low, moves higher (weekly wick formed), hourly CISD present → Tuesday and Wednesday
   both continue; after Mon/Tue/Wed expansion he avoids price.
4. A deliberately imperfect week: Tuesday's candle 2 closure has **no** hourly CISD yet, so
   it is not confirmed until the following day's closure through. Wednesday closes below
   Tuesday's low → Thursday continuation; no objective yet hit, so Friday is either avoided
   or taken as a further continuation. He notes Monday actually formed the low here.

### Quotes

- "We want to see the weekly candle open low then reach for its high"
- "not trying to match templates together, but instead finding the high or the low"

### Open questions / ambiguities

- "Closes well" / "closes strong" (the gate for taking candle 4) is never quantified — no
  close-within-x%-of-range rule is given.
- "Average range" / "average daily range" is named as an expansion target but no lookback
  period or calculation is given.
- The bias that must precede the profile is sourced from the weekly timeframe reversal
  candle, but the bias procedure itself is out of scope of this video.
- "Candle 2 closure" is assumed knowledge from the fractal model; the sweep-and-close-back
  definition is never restated here.
- Whether the hourly CISD must be inside the *wick* specifically or anywhere inside the
  daily candle's range is stated both ways across the series ("inside this wick" here,
  "inside that candle two" / "inside Thursday's range" later).

## Weekly Profile for Daily Bias - Midweek Reversal (`-gyDd_E_qD4`, https://youtu.be/-gyDd_E_qD4, 778s)

### What is actually taught

The second profile. A midweek reversal is a week where the weekly extreme is set by
**Wednesday** rather than Monday/Tuesday. The signature is: Monday and Tuesday both trade
*against* the intended bias — in a bullish week those are the down days, and the point of
them is that they build the lower wick of the weekly candle — then Wednesday prints the
candle 2 closure / reversal candle, and Thursday and Friday are the expansion days. In
fractal-model terms Wednesday is candle 2 and Thursday/Friday are candle 3 and candle 4, so
the focus days for trading are Thursday and Friday, though he says you may also trade
Wednesday itself if you want to trade candle 2.

The confirmation step is identical to classic expansion and is stated with the clearest
wording in the series: after Wednesday's candle 2 closure you look inside that candle on the
hourly for a change in the state of delivery, and that CISD confirms the daily high, which
in turn confirms the *weekly* wick high you are trading away from. The stack is explicitly
three-deep — weekly candle direction, anticipated daily candle, and an aligned hourly.
Targets are framed the same way: run the expansion until the average daily range is covered
or until the higher-timeframe objective is met. Friday is only sought as a second
continuation day when Thursday closed strong **and** an objective is still open.

Most of the video is spent on degraded cases, which is where the real teaching is. The
second example has no clean Wednesday candle 2 closure at all — only a "reversal looking
candle". His stated preference is to have the closure agree with the setup, so rather than
dropping to a lower timeframe for a substitute he lets **Thursday** print; Thursday's
candle 3 closure plus the hourly CISD is what retroactively establishes that Wednesday made
the high of the week, and the trade is then taken on Friday only. He concedes this yields
"one day of clean daily bias this week". The fourth example shows a Wednesday candle 2
closure that is also "somewhat of a bearish expansion candle", where the hourly CISD has
**not** yet occurred — the series of candles making the low has not been closed over — so
the setup is not live; he then waits intraday for the open-low-then-close-over sequence,
which confirms the daily profile and the weekly profile simultaneously before entry.

### Concepts introduced

- **Midweek reversal (weekly profile)** — Wednesday sets the weekly extreme.
- **Small opposing run** — Monday+Tuesday trading against bias to form the weekly wick.
- **Failure swings** (used as targets; never defined).
- **High resistance liquidity** (named once, in passing).
- **SMT off the low** (named as extra confluence at the extreme).
- **Inside bar** used to declare a consolidation range.
- **Average daily range** as an expansion stop-point.
- **Open objective** as the precondition for a Friday second continuation.

### Rules / conditions stated

- Monday and Tuesday must oppose the anticipated bias (they form the weekly wick).
- Wednesday must produce a candle 2 closure / reversal candle.
- Require an hourly CISD inside that Wednesday candle before trading.
- Trade Thursday (candle 3); trade Friday (candle 4) only if Thursday closed strong and an
  objective is still open.
- Trade the expansion until average daily range is reached or the HTF objective is met.
- If Wednesday's closure is not clean, do not force it — let Thursday close, and use
  Thursday's closure + hourly CISD as confirmation that Wednesday was the extreme; then
  trade Friday.
- Hourly CISD test, stated procedurally: find the low, find the series of candles that made
  it, and require price to close over that level. Not confirmed until that close happens.
- Intraday sequencing for a bullish day: price should open, form its low, then close over
  the level (open-low-high-close).

### Examples walked through

1. Prior Friday reversal → Monday continuation expected. Monday and Tuesday are merged into
   one candle due to a bank holiday; that combined candle runs out a low, opposing the
   bullish bias. Wednesday gives a candle 2 closure with failure swings above and high
   resistance liquidity below; hourly CISD present plus an SMT off the low. Thursday
   continues, first target gives no reaction, later target is hit; Friday continues into the
   final objective because Thursday closed strong with an objective still open.
2. Range high swept, then trade through the range low. Wednesday gives only a
   "reversal looking candle", no clean candle 2 closure → wait. Thursday's candle 3 closure
   plus hourly CISD confirms Wednesday as high of week; Friday trades down into the lows
   past failure swings.
3. Last swing point stale, so a new swing point is required first. Monday expands higher,
   Tuesday prints a reversal candle, Wednesday reverses out of a point of interest with a
   candle 2 closure and hourly CISD → Thursday expands, Thursday closes strong → Friday
   continuation reaches the previous day high and beyond.
4. V-shape then a consolidation flagged by an inside bar. Monday and Tuesday stay internal
   to the range; Wednesday sweeps the range low with a candle 2 closure that is also
   somewhat a bearish expansion candle. Hourly CISD is **absent** at first — must wait for
   the close over the series of candles that made the low. Once price opens, makes its low
   and closes over, both daily and weekly profile are confirmed → Thursday expands into the
   failure swings above; a protected swing going into Friday gives a further expansion.

### Quotes

- "Monday and Tuesday oppose our bias. Candle two closure created on Wednesday"
- "This confirms this daily high which helps us to confirm the wick high"

### Open questions / ambiguities

- "Reversal looking candle" vs "candle 2 closure" is a distinction he acts on but never
  defines — the first is explicitly not tradeable on its own, the second is.
- "Closes strong" is again unquantified.
- Whether Monday *and* Tuesday must both oppose, or whether one is sufficient, is not
  stated categorically; the diagrams show both opposing.
- "High resistance liquidity" is name-dropped with no definition in this video.
- Which correlated asset supplies the SMT is not named.

## Weekly Profile for Daily Bias - Consolidation Reversal (`McJflkKuvrI`, https://youtu.be/McJflkKuvrI, 533s)

### What is actually taught

The third profile is defined *residually*: it is what you have when neither classic
expansion nor midweek reversal happened. The opening frames it exactly that way — you check
for a classic expansion week, you see no expansion through Wednesday, and because the week
has consolidated there is no way to have a midweek reversal either, so you are in a
consolidation reversal.

Structurally: Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday consolidate; Thursday manipulates that
consolidation range and reverses; Friday is the expansion day. The manipulation is stated
with unusual precision for this channel — Thursday takes out **Monday's** high (bearish
case) *and* the previous day's high, and closes back below both. That double take-out plus
close-back-inside is what he reads as a candle 2 closure on Thursday. In the bullish case it
is Monday's low plus the previous day's low, closing back above. He notes you may trade the
Thursday reversal itself as a small wick, but states a preference for waiting: the
continuation day is Friday, and that is the day he wants.

The confirmation gate is unchanged and is stated as a conditional every time: Friday can be
anticipated to continue **if** there is a change in the state of delivery inside that
Thursday candle 2 / inside Thursday's range, checked on the hourly by locating the low, the
series of down-close candles that made it, and a close through. Targets for the Friday
expansion are the opposite side of the consolidation range, previous highs/lows outside it,
a higher-timeframe key level, or the average daily range.

Two secondary points get real airtime. First, he refuses to call a low on a day whose candle
is a bearish expansion candle even when it sits inside his bullish zone — "I would not trust
myself to say that Wednesday has formed a low" — and lets another day play out. Second, the
third example widens the entry criteria for the consolidation leg: Monday small range,
Tuesday a doji that takes both sides of the range ("that's not giving us any information"),
Wednesday also consolidating — all of these count as the consolidation, as does a
retracement, described as "a large range to a small range". The same example also introduces
the **T-spot** as the zone his indicator paints where he wants price to react on Friday, and
shows the invalidation concretely: after the new continuation forms he does not want to see
the low taken out; price wicks slightly below it with an SMT on the low but then expands
through the other side of the range.

### Concepts introduced

- **Consolidation reversal (weekly profile)** — residual profile.
- **Manipulation of the range** as the Thursday trigger (Monday's extreme + previous day's
  extreme, close back inside).
- **Consolidation range** drawn across Mon/Tue/Wed.
- **Continuation day** (Friday) vs **reversal day** (Thursday), with a stated preference.
- **T-spot** — indicator-marked reaction area on the intraday chart.
- **Large range to small range** as a retracement/consolidation signature.
- **Doji taking both sides of the range** = no information.
- **Protected swing** as a quality marker on an "ideal" candle 2 closure.
- **SMT on the low** (observed at the invalidation edge, not used as a trigger here).

### Rules / conditions stated

- Precondition: no expansion through Wednesday, so neither classic expansion nor midweek
  reversal applies.
- Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday form a consolidation (a retracement or a small opposing run
  also counts).
- Thursday takes out Monday's high/low **and** the previous day's high/low and closes back
  inside → candle 2 closure on Thursday.
- Require an hourly CISD inside Thursday's range: find the low, find the series of candles
  that made it, require a close through.
- Trade Friday as the continuation/expansion day; the Thursday reversal day is tradeable but
  he prefers to wait.
- Targets: the other side of the range / range high, previous highs, a higher-timeframe key
  level, or the average daily range.
- Do not call a reversal on a day that closes as an opposing expansion candle — let another
  day play out.
- After the new continuation forms, the prior low must not be taken out; the daily wick
  should form in the marked reaction area.

### Examples walked through

1. Price closes through a series of up-close candles into a fair value gap → bearish
   expectation. Monday respects the area but Tuesday does not expand — consolidation.
   Mon/Tue/Wed marked as a range. Thursday takes out the consolidation low and a swing low
   and closes back inside the previous candle's range (candle 2 closure), with failure
   swings above. Hourly CISD confirmed by the indicator. Friday expands higher into the
   previous highs and the range high; the T-spot is where the reaction is expected.
2. Previous week expanded into an objective → a new phase of price is due. Mon/Tue drift,
   Wednesday is a retracement/consolidation and closes as a bearish expansion candle, so no
   low is called. Thursday prints an ideal candle 2 closure that also forms a protected
   swing; hourly CISD present (close through the series of down-close candles) with a new
   continuation formed → Friday delivers a large-range expansion higher.
3. A high on the left taken out and expanded into. Monday small range, Tuesday a doji taking
   both sides, Wednesday consolidation. Thursday reaches into a fair value gap with a
   candle 2 closure; read as large-range-to-small-range. Hourly CISD present. Green box
   marks where price must respect; price dips slightly lower with an SMT on the low, then
   expands through the other side of the range. Mon–Thu formed the weekly wick, Friday the
   expansion.

### Quotes

- "consolidation Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday and then we're looking for a manipulation"
- "I prefer to wait for the continuation day on the following day"

### Open questions / ambiguities

- How much movement disqualifies Mon/Tue/Wed from being a "consolidation" is never bounded;
  the examples span a tight range, a doji day, and a retracement.
- "T-spot" is shown but never defined — it comes from his indicator and no derivation rule
  is given.
- Whether Thursday must take out Monday's extreme specifically, or just the consolidation
  range extreme, is stated both ways in the same video ("Monday's high" in the PDF section,
  "the consolidation low" in the first example).
- The choice between trading Thursday and waiting for Friday is stated as a personal
  preference, not a rule.

## Weekly Profile for Daily Bias - Intraweek Reversal (`FvOUvQ7odiw`, https://youtu.be/FvOUvQ7odiw, 726s)

### What is actually taught

The fourth profile is the deliberately untidy one, and the video says so: this is what you
use when the week does not produce a picture-perfect classic expansion, midweek reversal or
Thursday counter. Its defining property is negative — an intraweek reversal is a reversal
that does **not** form the high or low of the week. The canonical shape given is: Monday
expands away from the weekly open (which is itself the information — it tells you the weekly
candle wants to go that way), Tuesday consolidates, Wednesday forms a reversal, and Thursday
and Friday expand. The opportunity is the swing point formed *inside* the week, after price
has already moved away from the weekly open, and the trigger can be a candle 2 closure or
even a candle 3 closure on the daily.

The confirmation gate is the same hourly CISD inside the reversal candle. What this video
adds is a set of substitutions and quality tests around the daily candle itself. The
sharpest is the shape test: he compares two candidate reversal days and rejects the first
because it is an open-high-low-close candle (an expansion candle) while accepting the second
because it is open-low-high-close (an actual reversal candle) — "you see how much better this
candle two closure is versus this previous". Where a day is simultaneously a bullish and a
bearish candle 2 closure, the hourly is not enough on its own and he demands additional
intraday confirmation: let price open, make a low, form a new continuation, then trade it.
In the example that follows, price fails to respect that area and the setup is abandoned.

The second addition is **SMT as a swing-point substitute**. In the third example a day takes
out the previous day's low and closes above, and an SMT against a correlated instrument
(transcribed as "RB") lets him "treat this like a candle 2 closure or treat it like a swing
point", explicitly relaxing the usual requirement that the candle 2 sit at a point of
interest. The third addition is the **Monday rule**: he only trades a Monday when the prior
Friday formed a reversal or candle 2 closure, which makes Monday the candle 3 of that
sequence. The final example runs the same logic from a monthly candle 2 closure downward,
and shows him refusing to trade after Monday's expansion (that is already candle 4 — a new
phase of price is due) and instead waiting for a fresh swing point, which arrives Wednesday.

### Concepts introduced

- **Intraweek reversal (weekly profile)** — reversal that is not the weekly extreme.
- **Monday expanding away from the weekly open** as the directional tell.
- **Candle 2 closure quality**: OLHC (reversal) preferred over OHLC (expansion).
- **Two-sided candle 2** (bullish and bearish simultaneously) as a low-quality signal.
- **Intraday confirmation** — open, make a low, form a new continuation, then trade.
- **SMT as a substitute for a clean candle 2 / for being at a point of interest.**
- **The Monday rule** — Monday is tradeable only as candle 3 of a Friday reversal.
- **Protected swing** used as an explicit invalidation level.
- **50% of the candle / of its wick** as the level to be respected.
- Fractal application upward to the **monthly** candle 2 closure.

### Rules / conditions stated

- The reversal does not form the weekly extreme; it forms inside the week after price has
  expanded away from the weekly open.
- Trigger: a candle 2 closure (or candle 3 closure) on the daily inside that week.
- Require an hourly CISD inside the reversal candle before trading the following days.
- Prefer an open-low-high-close reversal candle; an open-high-low-close expansion candle is
  not a proper reversal candle.
- If the day is a candle 2 on both sides, require additional intraday confirmation: open,
  make the low, form a new continuation, then trade — and abandon it if the area is not
  respected.
- SMT against a correlated instrument permits treating a day as a candle 2 / swing point
  even when it is not at a point of interest.
- Only trade a Monday when the prior Friday formed a reversal or candle 2 closure (Monday is
  then candle 3).
- After a large-range day, be careful seeking the next continuation — a new phase of price
  may start; wait for a new swing point.
- Where a protected swing exists, price must not trade below it (bullish case).
- On a monthly candle 2, the continuation holds as long as 50% of that candle is respected.

### Examples walked through

1. Price inside a fair value gap; Friday leaves failure swings on the low. Monday expands
   away (bullish tell). Tuesday gives a small opposing candle 2 closure → expect the previous
   day low to be taken. Wednesday prints a candle 2 closure manipulating the consolidation
   low; hourly CISD present with a new continuation → Thursday continues; the following day
   is large and gets a move up that comes back into the range.
2. Price reaches a fair value gap, no aggressive reaction, Monday expands straight through
   the highs → no reversal, continuation instead. Later a day prints as both a bullish and a
   bearish candle 2 closure; hourly CISD exists but the model is two-sided, so intraday
   confirmation is demanded — price does not respect the area and the idea fails. The next
   daily close is a proper OLHC reversal candle with a protected swing → Friday continuation,
   strong close, new protected swing, Monday continuation into the daily highs.
3. Prior Friday candle 2 closure at a point of interest → Monday tradeable as candle 3;
   Monday expands away. Later a day takes out the previous day's low and closes above with an
   SMT against "RB" → treated as a candle 2 / swing point. Mon expansion, Tue/Wed
   consolidation, Thursday treated as candle 2 → Friday continuation, then a further Monday
   continuation as candle 4.
4. Monthly candle 2 closure with price trading lower and the month closing below. Weekly:
   range high taken out, candle 2 closure, candle 3 closes low → Monday can expand lower
   while 50% of the candle is respected. Monday expands (candle 4) → he stands aside and
   waits for a new swing point; equal lows form, then a swing point on Wednesday whose wick
   50% level is used, with equal lows and previous months' lows below as targets. Thursday
   and Friday continue and take those lows.

### Quotes

- "where we don't have the reversal forming the high of the week"
- "only time I will trade a Monday is when I have a Friday reversal"

### Open questions / ambiguities

- "RB" is never expanded. It is the correlated instrument supplying the SMT in example 3;
  the transcript gives no ticker definition and the video is not visible, so the instrument
  is left unidentified rather than guessed.
- What counts as "expanding away from the weekly open" is not quantified (no ATR or
  percentage-of-range threshold).
- The candle-2-on-both-sides case is resolved by "more intraday confirmation", but how much
  and on which timeframe is not specified.
- Whether a candle 3 closure is fully equivalent to a candle 2 closure as an intraweek
  trigger, or a fallback, is not made explicit.

## Weekly Profile for Daily Bias - Thursday Counter (`uPUNOi_R9fA`, https://youtu.be/uPUNOi_R9fA, 676s)

### What is actually taught

The fifth profile, and the only one in the series he explicitly attributes to someone else —
he credits **AM Trades** with teaching it to him. A Thursday counter is a week in which
Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday all expand away from the weekly open *without creating a
significant low* (bullish-expansion case) or high, so the week has no meaningful internal
swing point, and then Thursday prints a candle 2 closure that counters the range. Because
Thursday counters an unanchored three-day run, its wick tends to be large, which is what
makes it a reversal candle. Friday is then expected to trade back toward a specific and
unusually well-enumerated target set: the **weekly open**, the **current week's low (or
high)**, and any **resting failure swings**.

The confirmation gate is the same — an hourly CISD inside Thursday's range confirms the
daily wick, after which Friday's continuation can be sought. But this profile carries a
stated exception to his usual preference: Thursday counter is "one of those scenarios where
I will actually look to trade the reversal day", conditional on the Thursday candle having a
*small* wick and usually being paired with an SMT off the previous day's high or low, because
it can set up a large one-sided move.

Two pieces of risk realism sit inside this video. First, a target-feasibility check: in the
second example the weekly open is simply too far away, and he says it does not make sense to
expect the instrument (Nasdaq, named here) to cover that distance in one day given its
average daily range — so the nominal target is discarded on ADR grounds. That same example
then fails outright: the fair value gap he wanted respected is traded straight through, an
inversion is respected instead, and price trades through the high. He uses it to state
plainly that the appearance of a profile does not guarantee it works. Second, the last two
examples deliberately break the pattern's own preconditions: one where Monday does not expand
at all (Monday reverses, Tuesday and Wednesday expand, Thursday still counters), and one
where Thursday does not even take out Wednesday's high yet is still counted as countering the
range. In the final example he also cross-checks ES/NQ and finds the reversal there formed on
Wednesday, making it a Thursday continuation of a midweek reversal on those assets.

### Concepts introduced

- **Thursday counter (weekly profile)**, credited to AM Trades.
- **Countering the range** — Thursday reversing an unanchored Mon–Wed run.
- **"Without creating a significant low"** as the defining precondition.
- **Weekly open** as an explicit Friday target.
- **Resting failure swings** as targets.
- **Trading the reversal day**, gated on a small wick + SMT off the previous day's extreme.
- **Average daily range feasibility check** on a target.
- **T-spot** (named again, as the lower-half area where the daily wick should form).
- **Intermarket / correlated-asset cross-check** (ES, NQ) to reinterpret the profile.
- **Inversion** (of a fair value gap) observed at the failure.

### Rules / conditions stated

- Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday all expand away from the weekly open without creating a
  significant low (bullish-expansion case) or high.
- Thursday prints a candle 2 closure countering that range; a large wick marks it as a
  reversal candle.
- Require an hourly CISD inside Thursday's range to confirm the daily wick.
- Trade Friday as the continuation day, targeting the weekly open, the current week's
  extreme, and resting failure swings.
- Exception: trade the Thursday reversal day itself when its wick is small and it is paired
  with an SMT off the previous day's high/low.
- After a close on the high, the expected Thursday signature is a candle 2 closure off that
  high, meaning a sweep of the previous day high or an SMT on it.
- Discard a target that exceeds what the instrument's average daily range can plausibly cover
  in one day.
- Monday need not expand — a Tuesday/Wednesday expansion is enough for Thursday to counter.
- Thursday need not take out Wednesday's extreme to count as countering the range.
- On the intraday chart, the daily wick should form in the marked area (the T-spot); price
  trading through the fair value gap instead of respecting it invalidates the continuation.

### Examples walked through

1. Prior Friday candle 2 closure with SMT → Monday tradeable as candle 3. Monday, Tuesday and
   Wednesday all expand higher into a high on the left; no significant low made inside the
   week (the low belongs to Friday). Thursday takes out the previous day high with an SMT and
   an hourly CISD confirming the wick → Friday continues lower into the previous day's low and
   reaches the weekly open.
2. **Nasdaq** (named). Mon/Tue/Wed expand away, no relevant low, failure swings present. The
   weekly-open target is rejected as beyond a plausible one-day range. Friday grinds up, then
   moves lower into a fair value gap — but expands *through* it rather than respecting it,
   respects an inversion instead, and trades through the high. The profile fails.
3. Prior Friday reversal/expansion candle → Monday and Tuesday move higher; a fair value gap
   is ignored because daily closures do not respect it; a second fair value gap with failure
   swings is reached on Wednesday. Thursday prints a candle 2 closure on both bullish and
   bearish sides, but only the bearish side has an hourly CISD, so the low is not trusted →
   Friday moves lower toward, but does not reach, the weekly open.
4. A quick counter-example on preconditions: Monday does not expand (it reverses), Tuesday and
   Wednesday expand, Thursday still counters the range and Friday still continues.
5. Final example: Thursday does not take out Wednesday's high but is still counted as
   countering the range; Friday trades toward the weekly open and the current week's low.
   Cross-checking ES/NQ shows the reversal there formed on Wednesday — a Thursday continuation
   of a midweek reversal on those assets.

### Quotes

- "Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday expand away without creating a significant low"
- "It is not 100%. Things do fail."

### Open questions / ambiguities

- "Significant low" is the load-bearing precondition and is never quantified; example 2 uses
  "not really… quite close to this low enough to consider a failure swing" as the disqualifier.
- "Small wick" (the gate for trading the reversal day) vs "large wick" (what marks Thursday as
  a reversal candle) are used in the same video without thresholds, and appear to point in
  opposite directions.
- Which correlated instrument supplies the SMT is unnamed in examples 1 and 3; ES/NQ are named
  only in the final example.
- The T-spot is again used without derivation.
- No rule is given for choosing among the three Friday targets (weekly open, week's extreme,
  failure swings) when they conflict.

## Weekly Profile - TGIF Setup (`_CSO5Mf7CM8`, https://youtu.be/_CSO5Mf7CM8, 743s)

### What is actually taught

The closing video, and the only one that is explicitly a *dependent* profile: TGIF is
defined as revolving around a classic expansion week, and the video opens by telling the
viewer to go back and watch that first. TGIF is the trade of the weekly candle's wick —
in a bullish week, trading from the wick high back down to the close; in a bearish week,
from the wick low back up to the close. It is a Friday-only setup.

Three preconditions are given and each is enforced in the examples. First, the weekly
extreme (low in a bullish scenario) must have formed on Monday or Tuesday, exactly as in
classic expansion. Second, there must be expansion through Thursday, ideally three days
(Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday). Third — and this is the condition the video spends most of
its time on — a **key level or the draw on liquidity must have been hit**. His reasoning is
explicit: "if our key level has not been hit, it doesn't make sense to look back into the
range", you would expect continuation into that level instead. Two of the five examples are
included solely to show weeks that satisfy the day-structure but fail this level condition,
and therefore are *not* TGIF.

The target is the most numerically specific thing in the whole series: mark the weekly range
from the week's high to the week's low and expect Friday to retrace **20–30%** of it (0.2–0.3
on a fib), ideally coinciding with nearby PD arrays. Execution is layered: if Thursday
already formed a reversal, Friday simply continues off it; if Thursday did not, Friday must
form the reversal itself. On the hourly you wait for a sweep of a previous high and then
either a change in the state of delivery or a candle closure — he prefers the CISD earlier in
the day because volatility is lower, and allows candle closures once New York is in. On a
candle 2 closure with a large wick he marks 0.5 of the wick and wants an intra-candle CISD
there. One example carries a full trade: a 4-hour bullish candle closure, then his 4-hour and
15-minute model showing a reversal CISD followed by continuations, entry on the open of a new
continuation, stop on a low chosen because it sits below the EQ of the previous candle, and a
2R target that is reached.

The video also refines what "relevant level" means: fair value gaps that price has already
traded through are no longer relevant, and a level that is taken out without a V-shape
reversal — only a small retracement then continuation — does not trigger TGIF either. He
closes with a study-method aside: learn concepts in hindsight on formed examples first, then
test yourself.

### Concepts introduced

- **TGIF** — Friday trade of the weekly candle's wick, built on classic expansion.
- **The 20–30% weekly-range retracement target** (0.2–0.3 fib).
- **Relevant level** — and its decay: levels already traded through stop counting.
- **Phases of price around a level** (reversal vs retracement/consolidation) as the test for
  whether the level actually produced a turn.
- **Thursday-formed-reversal vs Friday-formed-reversal** branch.
- **CISD earlier in the day vs candle closures in New York** (volatility-based choice).
- **0.5 of the wick** of a large-wick candle 2 as the reaction level.
- **Intra-candle change in the state of delivery.**
- **EQ of the previous candle** as the stop-placement reference.
- Hindsight study method.

### Rules / conditions stated

- Precondition: a classic expansion week. Bullish: low formed Monday or Tuesday.
- Expansion through Thursday, ideally three days (Tue/Wed/Thu).
- A key level or the drawn-on liquidity must have been hit; if not, expect continuation into
  it instead and do not look for TGIF.
- Friday returns into the range, forming the wick of the weekly candle.
- Target: 20–30% of the weekly range measured high-to-low, preferably aligning with nearby
  PD arrays.
- If Thursday formed a reversal → Friday continues off it. If not → Friday must form the
  reversal.
- Entry on the hourly: wait for the previous high to be swept, then take either a CISD or a
  candle closure; prefer CISD earlier in the day (lower volatility), candle closures once in
  New York.
- On a candle 2 closure with a large wick, mark 0.5 of the wick, expect price to reach it,
  form an intra-candle CISD, then trade away.
- A fair value gap already traded through is no longer a relevant level.
- A level taken out that produces only a small retracement and continuation (no V-shape
  reversal) does not qualify — trade toward the higher-timeframe objective instead.
- Stop below the low that sits below the EQ of the previous candle; the shown target was 2R.

### Examples walked through

1. Uptrend, equal lows taken plus a fair value gap reached, V-shape reversal with two fair
   value gaps forming a CISD. Monday moves lower, Tuesday gives a candle 2 closure forming a
   protected swing; Tue/Wed/Thu expand and a high on the left is taken → all TGIF conditions
   met. On the hourly, Thursday did not reverse, so Friday must. A previous high is swept; he
   takes the candle 2 closure into New York, marks 0.5 of the large wick, wants an
   intra-candle CISD, and targets the 0.2–0.3 zone of the weekly range, which lines up with a
   low. It is reached.
2. **Counter-example.** Tuesday forms a high, Wednesday and Thursday expand — but no relevant
   level has been hit, so Friday is not expected back into the range; continuation follows
   instead. A second, near-identical week is shown stopping just shy of the level with the
   same conclusion.
3. Monday higher, Tuesday candle 2 closure, Wednesday and Thursday continue lower. The fair
   value gaps above have already been traded through, so they are not relevant; a fair value
   gap and a candle with no wick remain. The relevant level is only hit **on Friday itself** —
   after which the return into the range is valid. On the hourly he waits for the daily wick
   to form, uses a bullish 4-hour candle closure as counter-trend confirmation, then his
   4H/15m model shows a reversal CISD and continuations; entry on the open of the new
   continuation, stop on the low below the previous candle's EQ, 2R reached.
4. **Counter-example.** Tuesday low, Wednesday and Thursday expand into previous highs. Those
   highs are a relevant level, but the phases of price around them show no V-shape reversal —
   only a small retracement then continuation — so TGIF does not apply and the HTF objective
   is the right trade.
5. Monday consolidation; Tuesday sweeps Monday and Friday to form a new swing with a candle 2
   closure; Wednesday expands into an objective; **Thursday** hits the objective and forms the
   reversal candle. This is TGIF with Thursday as the reversal and Friday as the continuation
   day — an hourly CISD is required, then Friday trades back into the weekly range, choppily.

### Quotes

- "TGIF is going to be looking to trade that wick low to the close"
- "we have traded through both of those. So, I wouldn't consider them relevant anymore"

### Open questions / ambiguities

- The 20–30% retracement is stated as an area to look for, not as an order-placement rule; it
  is not said whether to enter at 0.2, at 0.3, or on a reaction anywhere inside.
- "Key level" / "relevant level" / "drawn liquidity" are used interchangeably; the video
  enumerates instances (prior swing highs/lows, equal highs/lows, fair value gaps, failure
  swings) but gives no ranking or selection rule when several exist.
- The "V-shape reversal" test for whether a level produced a turn is visual and unquantified.
- The 2R target in example 3 is what that particular trade offered; it is not stated as a
  general TGIF rule.
- The instrument is never named in any TGIF example.
- Whether TGIF can occur in a midweek-reversal or consolidation-reversal week is not
  addressed — it is defined only on top of classic expansion.

## Cross-video observations

- **One confirmation gate runs through all six videos**: a daily candle 2 closure is never
  tradeable on its own; an hourly change in the state of delivery inside that daily candle
  must exist first. This is the single most repeated, most decidable rule in the unit.
- **The profiles are mutually exclusive and are checked in order.** Consolidation reversal is
  literally defined as "neither classic expansion nor midweek reversal", and intraweek
  reversal is presented as the residual for weeks that match none of the named shapes.
- **The weekly extreme's day-of-week is the classifier**: Mon/Tue → classic expansion;
  Wed → midweek reversal; Thu after a Mon–Wed consolidation → consolidation reversal;
  Thu after a Mon–Wed unanchored expansion → Thursday counter; a reversal that is not the
  weekly extreme at all → intraweek reversal; the Friday wick trade on a classic expansion
  week → TGIF.
- **Anti-template stance is stated in four of the six videos**, most strongly in Classic
  Expansion and Midweek Reversal: do not predict which day makes the extreme, do not
  pattern-match; let the extreme form, confirm it, then trade continuations away from it.
- **Terms used but never defined anywhere in this unit** (assumed from the fractal-model
  playlists): candle 2 / candle 3 / candle 4 closure, protected swing, failure swing, fair
  value gap, order block, PD array, SMT, high resistance liquidity, T-spot, EQ. Only the
  hourly CISD procedure is spelled out step-by-step here.
