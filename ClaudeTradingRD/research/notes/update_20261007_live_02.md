## TTrades MNQ Cup - Afternoon Session  (h1ZQWWQDhKA, https://youtu.be/h1ZQWWQDhKA, 7545s)

### Context
The final, PM session of a one-day MNQ trading competition he ran (sponsored by NinjaTrader and
Lucid Trading; only MNQ allowed; ends 5 p.m.; ~200 entrants disqualified for back-end abuse such
as hedging multiple accounts). Price was a chop day, which he says repeatedly, and he says he
has not watched a PM session in years. The first ~third is Q&A; the rest is an entertainment
segment where Nick (and TTrades) take 5-minute-structure / 15-second (Nick: 5-second) executions
and give away an indicator subscription each time a trade hits 2R. He says explicitly that this
is not how he trades.

### What is actually taught (TTrades)
- **Consolidation / range deviation.** The day is a "double inside day": neither previous-day
  extreme taken, so "by definition consolidation", and price is expected to sit around the EQ
  and the opening price. A *range deviation* = price leaves a range and comes back in; while
  consolidating you expect a deviation, not a breakout. He says range deviation, inducement and
  AMD are the same idea under different names. After a target is hit on a consolidation day,
  expect a return into the range. On such a day the only way to make money is to catch a move
  and take profit, repeatedly - a long off the open never closed made nothing. With no expansion
  expected there is no defined target or invalidation, so he stays away.
- **Breakout acceptance test** (said to Nick during his short): if price breaks out of a range,
  you do not want it to close back inside; you want a retest or a continuation. A close back
  inside = AMD the other way.
- **Correlated triad.** "Which one is lying? They can't all go different directions." He explicitly
  rejects "one has to come back into alignment" - one asset reverses and that releases the other.
  Morning example: NQ up, YM down, ES between; YM made a small reversal so NQ finished its highs;
  NQ then reversed off the highs, which let YM trend lower. He looks for the **weakest** asset to
  run into its failure swings on a range day (YM, reaching for previous-day low).
- **Near-target exit.** A viewer held a long to within one point of take-profit and gave it all
  back. Once that close you are risking one point for the whole open profit; if price is
  reversing, exit ("to each their own").
- **Break even / trail.** Reviewing a viewer's 8:30 gold short off a protected swing for 2R: go
  break even once it runs into the intermediate lows, or at least trail; do not let it reverse
  after sweeping a low.
- **News.** "I do not trade through NFP, CPI or FOMC." This clears up the library's
  "API, FOMC, NFP" caption problem: the third release is CPI.
- **Session.** Does not trade or watch the PM: the volatility is usually gone and the intraday move
  is already done.
- **Top-down.** The daily chart starts all his bias, always, even when working a 30-minute chart.
  Which intraday profiling timeframe you use (15m/30m/1H/4H) does not matter - it is the same read.
  Never 7h/8h. A viewer who gets "influenced by price" and forgets the bias: stop going to the
  lower timeframes before looking at the higher ones.
- **Timeframe pairing.** The 15-second model = 5-minute swing point / 15-second execution. "There's
  always a fractal model" - only the timeframe changes.
- **Pattern trading** (definition on request): following a pattern and trading it every time with
  no higher-timeframe analysis. You need discretion and higher-timeframe analysis, "otherwise
  people would just trade bots".
- **Reversals.** About a live reversal short: he does not normally trade reversals like that
  because the win rate is "20, 30, 40% less". This is the first number the corpus gives for his
  continuation-only doctrine. Attribution is by turn order: the next speaker is the one with
  "seven trades" (Nick).
- **Reversal speed / candle timing** (5m/15s game): after a 5-minute reversal candle, expansion
  normally waits for the open of the next 5-minute candle (he counts down the seconds). A
  reversal entry that hangs around the entry area is more likely to be stopped out - you want
  expansion to continue quickly.
- **Closure discipline.** He tells Nick to wait for the 5-minute closure before a 15-second
  entry; "now you actually have a valid model" once it closes.
- **Indicator.** Bias selector: neutral prints both sides; bullish or bearish restricts to that
  side; autobias uses the higher timeframe (4H bullish → longs only). **He personally leaves it on
  neutral.** No NinjaTrader version yet. He executes on NinjaTrader and uses no Bookmap or level 2.
- **XAUUSD remark.** Gold reached down into a daily gap/imbalance. If the hourly shows good
  displacement up out of it (tonight in Asia or tomorrow), he would target the high above. "Gold
  has been very very good about pulling back to daily gaps and moving away from them."
- **Psychology.** Blowing five accounts is a management problem (tilt, sizing), not a strategy
  problem. When you are about to revenge trade or size up, don't. Unprofitable traders first
  have to work out whether they lack a strategy or lack discipline. Video-game analogy: after a
  loss you respawn; you do not rebuild your whole loadout. Reading books is no use without acting
  on it. Signal trading is "dumb".
