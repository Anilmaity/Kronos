## ICT Daily Profile - London Reversal / New York Continuation
(`y6SnEdASVLY`, https://youtu.be/y6SnEdASVLY, 684s)

### What is actually taught

The video opens by insisting that daily profiles are unintelligible without first fixing the
session windows. He shows a kill-zone PDF (Asia, London, New York a.m., New York p.m.) and then
deliberately discards most of it: for this series he works with **London = 02:00–05:00** and
**New York a.m. = 08:30–12:00**, noting the 12:00 end is his personal extension ("I kind of
extend this out to 12 personally").

A London Reversal is then defined at the *daily candle* level: it is "basically a classic
expansion candle". Bearish version — the day opens, London prints the **high** of the day, then
price expands lower into New York and closes near the low, producing an Open-High-Low-Close
daily candle. Bullish version — open, London prints the **low** (this is the daily candle's
wick), then expansion higher through New York, producing Open-Low-High-Close.

The mechanic on the lower timeframe is a three-step chain: (1) during 02:00–05:00 price runs in
the direction *opposite* to the day's intended expansion, (2) a **change in the state of
delivery / market structure shift / inversion** ("however you define it") occurs on that London
extreme, (3) **New York continues** in the new direction. The slogan is "London reverses, New
York continuation."

Execution is not on the London CISD itself — he repeatedly says "if I was trading London I could
look to get on side here, however I focus on New York session." Instead he waits for New York to
produce an **opposing run** into London's order block: at 08:30, if he is bearish, he *wants* to
see a move up; that manipulation up creates the order block he sells. He explicitly treats an
08:30 move in the *expected* direction (i.e. down when bearish) as a signal that a deeper
retracement into London's range is coming first, and he then trades that retracement instead.
Targets shown are a flat **2R**, and he calls out that lows/highs of day often land on 10:00 or
09:30 because of the 4-hour OHLC. Order block, propulsion block, mean threshold and midnight
open are all used as refinement layers but assumed known from earlier videos.

### Concepts introduced (speaker's terminology)
- "London reversal" / "London reverses, New York continuation"
- "classic expansion candle"
- "change in the state of delivery" (CISD) — used interchangeably with "market structure shift"
  and "inversion"
- kill zones / sessions; London 2–5; New York a.m. 8:30–12
- "opposing run" (the New York manipulation leg into an order block)
- propulsion block, mean threshold, midnight open, internal liquidity, equilibrium of a range,
  low resistance liquidity

### Rules / conditions stated
- London window is 02:00–05:00; New York a.m. window is 08:30–12:00 (12:00 is his extension).
- Bullish London Reversal: daily candle = Open, Low, High, Close; the London leg forms the wick.
- Bearish London Reversal: daily candle = Open, High, Low, Close.
- Between 02:00 and 05:00 price must run counter to the intended daily direction, then close
  back through the opposing series of candles (the CISD).
- The New York continuation is entered on an **opposing run** into London's order block, not on
  the London CISD.
- If at 08:30 price runs in the *same* direction as the anticipated expansion, expect a
  retracement back into London's range first and enter there.
- Stop goes beyond the opposing run's extreme; target 2R (his stated default).
- 09:30 and 10:00 are called out as levels where the day's extreme tends to complete.

### Examples walked through
- **Oil, daily → 15m → 5m.** Bullish daily reversal candle with internal liquidity above as
  first target and range equilibrium beyond. London 2–5 drops, closes over a series of
  down-close candles = CISD. 5m at 08:30 shows accumulation → manipulation → distribution; entry
  on the order block, stop at the low, 2R hit; a propulsion block forms and is retested.
- **Bearish example, 15m → 30m.** London sweeps a high; 15m too messy so he steps *up* to 30m
  for a clean series of up-close candles; New York moves higher into the order block / 30m
  propulsion block, retest at 08:30 with a small volume imbalance, then continuation lower.
- **Third example.** London consolidates but reaches a high right at the end of the session;
  CISD there; 08:30 makes the opposing run higher = manipulation; entry on the order block below
  it, 2R, low of day near 10:00.
- **Fourth example.** London runs low-resistance liquidity higher; at 08:30 price manipulates
  *down* instead of up, so he waits for the retracement back into the previous range, shorts
  there, continuation lower at 10:00 (tied to the 4-hour OHLC).
- **ES 15m.** London sweeps one side of a range; CISD on the swept high; 08:30 news manipulates
  up into London's order block; continuation lower into 09:30/10:00.
- **Final example, 30m.** Previous day swept below its low and closed back inside = bullish
  reversal candle → expect the next day higher. London 2–5 moves lower and closes over
  down-close candles; New York retests the level, fails to displace over a high, holds mean
  threshold and midnight open, and the *following* day London again forms the low and expands.

### Quotes
- "a London reversal is basically a classic expansion candle"
- "London reverses New York continuation"

### Open questions / ambiguities
- The 12:00 end of the New York a.m. window is explicitly his personal preference, not a rule.
- "Change in the state of delivery", "market structure shift" and "inversion" are said to be
  interchangeable here — the video does not give a decidable definition for any of them.
- No timezone is ever named (EST/EDT is implied by ICT convention but never stated).
- Whether the London extreme must remain the extreme of the whole day (i.e. what invalidates the
  profile intraday) is never stated for this profile.
- Target is "2R" in every worked example, but he also targets internal liquidity and range
  equilibrium — no rule for choosing between them.
- Stepping up a timeframe (15m → 30m) when the CISD is "messy" is done twice but never defined.

## ICT Daily Profile - New York Reversal
(`xdzyejskSKE`, https://youtu.be/xdzyejskSKE, 783s)

### What is actually taught

Part 2 defines the New York Reversal by **contrast with the London Reversal**, and the whole
video hangs on a single discriminator. Both profiles produce the same daily candle shapes
(Open-High-Low-Close or Open-Low-High-Close). The difference is *where the reversal is allowed
to happen*, and that is governed by whether a **relevant higher-timeframe PD array** has been
reached: "How this differs from a London reversal is that London never reached a relevant PD
array." If London runs in the counter direction but does not engage an HTF level, price is
expected to keep retracing until New York, where the HTF level is finally hit, the CISD occurs,
and only then does the day's expansion begin.

The strongest teaching point is the **fake CISD**. In example two, London makes what looks
exactly like a London-Reversal low with a clean structure shift, but looking left there is no
HTF PD array there — only more equal lows, i.e. freshly manufactured liquidity. He labels the
structure shift "a fake change in the state of delivery" and predicts price will run those lows
again, ideally around the New York opening times. That is precisely what happens; the real entry
comes after the New York sweep, with New York open itself doubling as the order block.

Execution pattern: identify the HTF level that must be reached (previous day low / high, a wick's
consequent encroachment, a gap, external liquidity); wait for New York to reach it; mark the
opposing series of candles at that extreme; enter on the close back through them (order block),
stop beyond the extreme or beyond the order block; target the accumulated **failure swings**,
session highs/lows (Asia and London), midnight open, and previous day levels. He notes that a
dense field of failure swings / low-resistance liquidity above is what produces the aggressive
post-CISD move, and that risk-reward should decide where the stop sits (he moves it from the
swing low to the bottom of the order block because targeting the failure swings from the low
"doesn't really make sense in terms of risk to reward").

The final example deliberately blurs the boundary: a day that is a London Reversal *and* then a
New York Reversal, because London's continuation reaches the HTF objective inside the New York
session, and reaching that objective is what licenses the New York reversal back the other way.

### Concepts introduced
- "New York reversal"
- "relevant PD array" / "higher timeframe key level" as the gating condition
- "fake change in the state of delivery"
- "failure swing" (repeated highs/lows left behind, used as targets)
- consequent encroachment of a wick; midnight open; New York open as an order block
- 8:30 manipulation → 9:30 continuation ("8:30 offers an entry, 8:30 manipulation, 9:30
  continuation, similar to London and New York")

### Rules / conditions stated
- New York Reversal requires that **London did not reach a relevant HTF PD array**.
- If London produces a structure shift without an HTF PD array behind it, treat it as a fake
  CISD and expect the same liquidity to be run again, ideally around New York open times.
- The reversal is framed at the HTF level once New York reaches it: mark the opposing series of
  candles, wait for the close through them, enter the resulting order block.
- Stop may be placed either at the swing extreme or at the far side of the order block, chosen
  by risk-to-reward; his floor is "over two R".
- Targets: failure swings, Asia and London session highs/lows, midnight open, previous day
  high/low.
- A day can be both profiles: once the HTF objective is reached intraday, a reversal from it is
  valid even if London already produced a reversal.

### Examples walked through
- **Example 1 (15m).** Previous day low marked as sell-side liquidity. London moves lower and
  higher but reaches nothing relevant; New York reaches the HTF level; first attempted CISD
  fails → new low → new series of down-close candles lining up with New York open → close over =
  entry, stop at low, target the failure swings above. Runs.
- **Example 2 (15m → LTF).** Looks like a London Reversal; nothing to the left → fake CISD; lows
  purged again; at New York open price sweeps a low, cannot close below, then closes over = entry;
  targets Asia and London highs, partials taken at midnight open.
- **Example 3 (daily → 30m/15m).** Previous day closed back inside the prior candle's high =
  bearish reversal candle → anticipate Open-High-Low-Close. London only trades higher (no London
  Reversal); New York displaces down off a 30m series of up-close candles; targets = Asia/London
  failure swings, 2.65R; midnight and 08:30 provide a second order block.
- **Example 4.** Framing off external liquidity: London sweeps a low, New York open makes an
  opposing run higher and takes previous day's high, aggressive move out and back into the range
  = CISD for the reversal day; his 2R entry is missed (never tagged in) — he shows the miss.
- **Example 5 (ES hourly → 15m).** SMT at the highs, low-resistance liquidity, an order block,
  and a measured gap between a high and a low = HTF imbalance objective. London Reversal
  continues lower into that objective inside New York; once the objective is hit, the New York
  Reversal higher is framed off the order block, targeting equal highs.

### Quotes
- "London never reached a relevant PD aray"
- "this is going to be a fake change in the state of delivery"

### Open questions / ambiguities
- "Relevant" PD array is never defined. Examples use previous day high/low, a wick's consequent
  encroachment, a measured gap, and external liquidity — but no test for relevance is given.
- The fake-CISD rule is diagnostic in hindsight; no stated way to know in real time that nothing
  relevant sits to the left other than "look left".
- Stop placement (swing vs order block) is chosen by R, with no rule for the choice.
- Since a day can be both a London Reversal and a New York Reversal, the profiles are not
  mutually exclusive — the video does not say how to label such a day for statistics.

## ICT Daily Profile - New York Manipulation
(`IeXtmeKqjnQ`, https://youtu.be/IeXtmeKqjnQ, 547s)

### What is actually taught

Part 3 covers the profile where **London does nothing**. Precondition: London either consolidates
outright or fails to reach a higher-timeframe PD array, leaving a London range with a defined
high and low. New York then **manipulates that range** — sweeps one side of it — has a change in
the state of delivery, and expands the other way. He describes this as "a great depiction of a
power 3", i.e. accumulation (London) → manipulation (New York sweep) → distribution (the
expansion), and the resulting daily candle is again OHLC or OLHC.

Timing is sharper than in the other profiles: if a manipulation is coming, it is expected at
**08:30 or 09:30**. He reads an 08:30 move *up* as evidence to look lower and vice versa — the
same opposing-run logic as part 1, but here the level being swept is explicitly London's range
edge (or the current dealing range edge) rather than an HTF array.

He adds an entry-refinement rule that is not in the other videos: after the CISD, rather than
entering at the opening price / at the CISD level, he prefers to enter at a **discount** (for
longs) of the manipulated range, choosing the **mean threshold of the order block** where it
coincides with that discount. He also re-draws the premium/discount range as the swing extends.

Directional bias is set from the daily chart before any of this: consecutive closes over the
previous day's high, a fair value gap holding as support, or a close over a wick's consequent
encroachment, all give "previous day high" as the target. The last example ties the profile to
the **4-hour Power of Three** — the whole London-consolidation-then-NY-sweep sequence fits inside
one 4H candle as its accumulation/manipulation/distribution — and adds **standard deviation
projections** as confluence, noting 2–2.5 lined up with previous day high and 4–4.5 with the max
expansion.

### Concepts introduced
- "New York manipulation" daily profile
- London consolidation / London failing to reach an HTF PD array as the precondition
- Power of Three / AMD mapping onto the profile; 4-hour Power of Three
- entering at discount / mean threshold of the order block instead of at the opening price
- standard deviation projections as a target confluence
- "failure swing", "opposite order flow" sweep, external liquidity

### Rules / conditions stated
- Precondition: London consolidates **or** fails to reach a relevant HTF PD array — it must not
  form a significant high or low that New York can trade off.
- The manipulation is expected at **08:30 or 09:30**.
- New York must sweep one side of the London / current range, then close back through the
  opposing series of candles (CISD) to confirm.
- Prefer an entry at a discount (long) / premium (short) of the manipulated range; use the order
  block's mean threshold where it overlaps that half.
- Stop beyond the manipulation extreme; targets are previous day high/low or the opposite side
  of the range.
- Daily-chart bias inputs stated: closing over previous day high repeatedly, a fair value gap
  holding, a close over a wick's consequent encroachment.

### Examples walked through
- **Index/NQ-style, daily → 15m → 5m.** Three days of expansion, swing low, FVG holds and price
  closes over previous day high → target previous day high. London consolidates; 08:30 moves up
  → look lower; low taken; first CISD attempt fails; new low; at New York open price closes over
  a down-close candle → long, stop at low, previous day high hit.
- **Oil 15m → 5m.** Bullish but London only makes a high, so he switches to the current dealing
  range's high/low. New York reaches below the previous low; the down-close series coincides with
  **midnight opening price**; first CISD fails, second succeeds; he takes the entry at the order
  block's mean threshold at a discount of the range rather than at the opening price; the range
  is re-extended as price moves; target reached.
- **Third example.** Whole day trades inside a prior high and low; London takes neither side and
  prints a failure swing; at 08:30 price sweeps one side of the range and a high; 15m series is
  ambiguous so he drops to 5m for the up-close series; entry with stop over the high, target the
  other side of the range = 1.75R; he then re-checks that the 15m framing would also have worked.
- **Fourth example.** Daily order flow: close over a wick's consequent encroachment then a
  continuation, closing over previous day high each day. London takes one side and reverses,
  builds equal highs with previous day high. Near New York, price sweeps external liquidity
  "opposite order flow"; on the 5m the CISD is paired with a fair value gap, giving two valid
  entries (order block or FVG); both fill; previous day high hit. He then overlays the 4H candle
  to show the AMD, and adds standard deviation projections (2–2.5 at previous day high, 4–4.5 at
  max expansion).

### Quotes
- "London is consolidating or failing to reach a higher time frame key level"
- "London did not reach an important level it just Consolidated"

### Open questions / ambiguities
- "Consolidating" is never quantified — in example 2 London made a high but he still treated the
  day as a manipulation profile by substituting the dealing range for the London range.
- Which range is manipulated (London's range vs the current dealing range vs the whole prior-day
  range) shifts between examples with no stated selection rule.
- 08:30 *or* 09:30 — no rule for which, and no stated cut-off after which the manipulation is
  considered to have not occurred.
- The discount-entry preference is stated as "normally a discount", not as a hard rule.
- Standard deviation projections are used as confluence but their anchoring is not explained here.

## ICT Daily Profile - Seek & Destroy
(`4WCiIyCiBrQ`, https://youtu.be/4WCiIyCiBrQ, 531s)

### What is actually taught

Part 4 is the anti-profile: the day to **not** trade. On the daily chart Seek & Destroy is an
**indecision candle** — open and close at roughly the same point, no direction all day. On the
lower timeframe it looks like repeated failures to hold outside a range: each excursion beyond
the range is immediately reclaimed. Bullish and bearish versions differ only in where the daily
candle happens to close.

Two causes are given: the market is waiting on high-impact news (the day *before* CPI, FOMC or
NFP), or there is no direction on the higher timeframe charts.

The **early recognition tell** is the one genuinely mechanical rule in this video and it requires
looking at Asia, which he otherwise excludes: with a Seek & Destroy, "London will take both sides
of the Asia range." London sweeps Asia's low and Asia's high without holding beyond either, and
that sets up consolidation conditions for New York. A **second, separate** condition is given:
if London did *not* sweep both sides of Asia but instead expanded and created most of the daily
range already, then New York is unlikely to produce a large move — also low-probability
conditions, also a day to sit out.

If he had to trade it, the approach inverts: trade **outside-in** rather than inside-out. Mark
the London range's equilibrium (premium/discount 0.5); when price deviates outside the range,
target equilibrium first and then the opposite extreme, expecting oscillation. Any interior
reference — the Asia range, the daily open — acts as "a magnet" that price returns to rebalance.
As price makes new lows he re-draws the premium/discount on each new leg and keeps targeting
equilibrium and the initial range.

Tooling shown: a Power of Three indicator plus session boxes ("the indicator called underscore
boxes"), with London drawn as 02:00–08:30 purely so there is no blank gap on the chart between
his real 02:00–05:00 window and the a.m. session start.

### Concepts introduced
- "Seek and Destroy" daily profile
- "indecision candle" (deferred to his Open-High-Low-Close video)
- London sweeping **both sides of the Asia range** as the recognition condition
- "most of the daily range already created in London" as a second low-probability condition
- trading "from the outside of the range back in"; equilibrium / premium-discount as the target
- the daily open and the Asia range as magnets for rebalance
- session boxes indicator; London drawn 02:00–08:30 for display continuity only

### Rules / conditions stated
- Daily candle must be an indecision candle: open and close at relatively the same point, no
  directional expansion.
- Recognition A: London takes out **both** the Asia high and the Asia low without displacing
  beyond either.
- Recognition B: London expanded and produced most of the daily range → expect New York
  consolidation.
- Context: a very large range day makes consolidation the following day more probable; the day
  before CPI/FOMC/NFP is a typical setting.
- If traded: enter only from outside the range, first target = equilibrium (0.5) of the range,
  second target = the opposite extreme.
- Re-mark premium/discount on each new leg as the range extends.
- Deviations outside the Asia range / daily open are expected to be rebalanced.
- Stated preference: avoid the profile entirely; its value is knowing when to stay out.

### Examples walked through
- **NQ-style 15m** with daily OHLC marked, Power of Three indicator and session boxes. Asia
  ranges and sets its high/low. London takes the Asia low with no displacement, comes back in,
  then takes the Asia high with no displacement either. New York moves out of the range and
  returns to equilibrium; a new premium/discount range is drawn from the new leg and price
  returns to its equilibrium, the daily open, and the initial range. The day closes as a small
  up-close candle only ~80 NQ points from the open — described as whipsaw, not range.
- **EURUSD 15m.** Asia range marked. London moves outside it and is aggressively rejected back
  in; the opposite side also cannot break out; price returns to equilibrium, the initial range
  and the daily open. Later a new low is made and he tracks premium/discount down with it,
  again returning to equilibrium and the initial range. Daily candle finishes as an indecision
  candle.

### Quotes
- "London will take both sides of the Asia range"
- "this is a profile I wish to avoid"

### Open questions / ambiguities
- "Relatively the same point" for the indecision candle is not quantified (the 80-point NQ
  example is illustrative, not a threshold).
- "Most of the daily range" created in London is not quantified (no percentage given).
- No stated rule for *when* the both-sides-of-Asia sweep must complete (by 05:00? by 08:30?).
- What would invalidate Seek & Destroy mid-session — i.e. what displacement counts as a genuine
  breakout rather than another failed excursion — is not defined.
- Asia's session window is never given numerically anywhere in this unit, yet the Asia range is
  the load-bearing input for recognition A.
- The relationship between recognition A and recognition B is not stated (they are alternatives,
  but he does not say whether either alone is sufficient).

## Cross-video observations

- **A decision tree is implicit across parts 1–3** and is the most valuable structure in the
  unit: (a) did London reach a relevant HTF PD array and reverse? → London Reversal, trade the
  New York continuation. (b) Did London run counter but reach nothing relevant? → expect the run
  to continue; New York Reversal at the HTF level. (c) Did London consolidate / fail to make a
  usable extreme? → New York Manipulation of London's range. (d) Did London take both sides of
  Asia, or already spend the day's range? → Seek & Destroy, stand aside.
- The **08:30 opposing run** is the common execution trigger in parts 1–3: a move counter to the
  intended daily direction at 08:30 is treated as the manipulation that builds the order block.
- **Nothing in this unit contradicts mainstream ICT**, but two emphases are non-standard: the
  "relevant PD array" test as the *formal discriminator* between London and New York reversals,
  and the explicit "fake CISD" label.
- No timestamps exist in any of the four transcripts, so no `approx_time` is recorded in the
  concept files for this unit.
