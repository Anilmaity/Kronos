# Fitting the `session` gate — XAUUSD, New York clock

Source: `m3_scalper/xau_m1_3y.parquet` — 1,074,472 M1 bars, 2023-07-02 to 2026-07-23, tz-aware UTC. Generated by `python/fit_session_window.py`. Offline, no network.

`session` gates **68 concepts**, more than any other undefined term in `meta/automation_gaps.md`. The corpus answers the timezone (New York); the hours are the open question. This document turns the gate into a fitted default plus a sweep range, by measuring what gold actually does by New York hour.

**A correction to the project's own record, up front.** `RESUME.md` finding #8 reads *"session / kill-zone HOURS ARE NEVER STATED. Settled negative"*, with the 08:30-12:00 quote as the widest usable claim. That is too strong. `concepts/time/killzones.yaml` records two **complete** kill-zone grids, and they were verified for this document against `raw/transcripts/MPeeE55rNOw.txt` rather than taken on trust:

| grid | Asia | London | New York AM | fourth window |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| forex | 20:00-00:00 | 02:00-05:00 | 07:00-10:00 | London Close 10:00-12:00 |
| indices | 20:00-00:00 | 02:00-05:00 | 08:30-11:00 | New York PM 13:30-16:00 |

All eight windows are present verbatim in that transcript, and the same video states the timezone (*"all the time shown here are in New York time so that is EST or Eastern Standard Time"*) and, importantly, addresses daylight saving directly — resolving it by setting TradingView's timezone selector to New York, which is a DST-aware selector. Section 5 confirms that operational reading empirically.

**Provenance, graded — because this is weaker than it first looks.** MPeeE55rNOw is a solo instructional video walking through a PDF, so the windows are in TTrades' own voice within it; the concept file's `voice: mixed` tag comes from merging that unit with two guest-heavy units (Trader T, Ben, $niper), not from the windows themselves. But this is **taught convention presented in a lesson, not a rule he is narrated applying to his own entries**, it appears in essentially one video, and the concept is filed `contested`. That places it below a rule he repeats as his own gate and well above convention imported from outside the channel. The **Asian window is the one externally contested** — `meta/external_crossref.md` records outside sources disagreeing on it, and it is the only one of the eight where this data also finds nothing (section 4). The other three sessions match public ICT convention, with only minor variation on the forex New York AM bound — see the provenance grades at the end of this document for the per-window detail.

So the corpus does supply candidate windows. It does not supply evidence that they work, and *"volatility is higher"* is asserted in that video and never measured. That is what the rest of this document does.

## Headline

**Recommended default: 10:00-12:00 New York on `America/New_York` — the corpus's own forex *London Close* kill zone. Conservative superset: the attested 08:30-12:00.** Sweep `start` over 08:30-10:30 and `end` over 11:00-13:00.

10:00-12:00 is recommended because it is **pre-specified rather than fitted** — the corpus states it (`concepts/time/killzones.yaml`, video MPeeE55rNOw, verified against the transcript), outside convention agrees on it without dissent, it sits inside the attested 08:30-12:00 quote, and it contains the Silver Bullet sub-window — *and* it happens to score best here: -0.004R with 1.49x range density and 1.71x daily-extreme density on 8.7% of the traded clock. Its edge over the rest of the day is **not** statistically significant (t = +1.46); the four independent reasons to have looked there before seeing the number are what make it a defensible default rather than the best cell in a grid search.

**Evidence strength, graded honestly — the two halves of the question give opposite answers.** *Strong* for opportunity: the 08:00-11:59 block carries **29.9% of all daily-extreme observations** on 17.4% of the traded clock — a **1.72x** lift that survives re-anchoring the day, survives dropping the weekly open and close, and survives excluding the market-reopen window. Minute-bar range there peaks at 2.9x the day's trough. *Absent, and where measurable actually negative*, for edge: the attested window returns -0.036R (+/-0.011) against an all-hours null of -0.026R.

**So be blunt: for gold, time-of-day buys opportunity, not accuracy — and the New York AM kill zone actively costs accuracy.** Across 12 windows the only result that survives correcting for multiple testing is **negative**: the corpus's forex New York AM kill zone (07:00-10:00) returns -0.057R against the rest of the day, t = -3.11 on 7,835 events. Hour 08 is the day's worst at -0.109R while 08:30 is simultaneously the half hour where the day's extreme most often prints (5.1% of all extremes, 2.3x its clock share). **The best half hour to have a level is not the best half hour to take an entry.** A session gate is defensible as a liquidity and range filter — trade when there is something to trade, size against a known range — and is not defensible as an edge. Anything in the corpus leaning on 'this must all be done within a kill zone' to explain why a setup works is unsupported here. The sign is, however, exactly what the corpus's own 'manipulation first' framing predicts.

**A trap in the hour table, flagged because it is the most likely way to misread this document.** The single densest hour for daily-extreme formation is **not** in the New York morning — it is hour 18 NY at 10.9%, ahead of hour 10 at 8.9%. That is the market-reopen hour and it is an **artefact**: the first 15 minutes after a trading halt are 6.0x over-represented among daily extremes relative to the time they occupy, and excluding the first 30 minutes after each halt collapses hour 18 from 10.9% to 6.7% while leaving the 08:00-11:59 block untouched (29.9% to 30.2%). **Do not build an 18:00 NY kill zone out of that row** — the level is already made by the time the window is observable. Section 1 separates it from the day-boundary (arcsine) artefact that also inflates hours 18 and 16.

**DST: settled — use `America/New_York`, do not sweep it.** The venue's own daily break resumes at 18:00 NY on 99.8% of occasions under DST-aware Eastern, but splits across two hours (67.8% / rest) under fixed UTC-5. The venue clock is DST-aware and the question is closed. It is also **material**, not a technicality: outside the break hour the choice moves up to 4.0pp of extreme-formation share and 23.3% of minute range between hours.

**4H grid: the forex grid (17/21/01/09 NY) for gold, weakly.** The two grids are statistically indistinguishable on candle behaviour (follow-through 59.6% vs 59.4%, z = 0.08). The tiebreak is structural: the forex grid opens a candle at 17:00 NY, exactly on the venue's daily break, so 0% of its candles straddle it against 17% for the futures grid. Confidence: **low-to-moderate** — sweep both.

## 0. The data, and where its holes are

XAUUSD is not a 24h instrument. In this dataset the week runs **Sunday 18:00 NY to Friday 17:00 NY**, with a **daily break from 17:00 to ~18:04 NY**. New York hour **17 contains zero bars** across the whole three years; every other hour is essentially complete.

| fact | value |
|---|---:|
| M1 bars | 1,074,472 |
| consecutive-minute steps | 99.90% |
| daily-break gaps (60-180 min) | 605 |
| weekend gaps (>16h) | 165 |
| other intra-week gaps (2-59 min) | 248 |
| NY hours with no bars at all | [17] |
| session days (18:00-anchored) | 788 |
| window membership agrees with `bars.session` | yes, all 12 windows |

**How gaps are handled.** Nothing is forward-filled — `bars.resample` drops empty buckets rather than inventing flat candles, so an hourly or 4H bucket spanning the break is built from the minutes that actually traded. Forward-return scoring walks bar positions, not wall clock, so an event late in the session is scored against the next bars that exist, which for a 16:5x event means bars on the far side of the break. That is a real limitation of the 16:00 row and it is why the recommendation below does not lean on the last hour of the session.

Two day framings are reported because the corpus is contested on the daily open: **18:00-anchored** (the corpus's canonical daily candle, and the primary framing here) and **midnight NY**. All hours below are New York local unless stated.

## 1. Where the day's high and low actually form

18:00-anchored day, M1 resolution, 788 session days = **1,576 extreme observations** (one high and one low per day).

**Units, stated once and used throughout.** `day high %` and `day low %` are shares of *days* and each column sums to 100. **`share of extremes`** is the share of all 1,576 extreme *observations* printing in that hour — it is `(high% + low%) / 2`, sums to 100, and is the only one of the three directly comparable with a share of the clock. Adding the high and low columns without halving gives extremes-per-day on a 0-200 scale; that number looks like a percentage, is not one, and doubles the apparent concentration when set against a clock share. ### Control 2: the market reopen (tests the gap artefact)

XAUUSD halts 17:00-18:04 NY daily and over the weekend. The first bars after a halt absorb whatever repriced while the market was shut, so they can set a session extreme mechanically rather than informatively. Counting every halt of 60 minutes or more — daily breaks, weekends and the handful of holiday closes — there are **788 reopens**, with a median repricing across the halt of $1.09 and a 90th percentile of $8.65 (the tail is the weekend gaps).

The test compares the share of extreme bars falling within k minutes of a reopen against the share of all bars that do — if the two match, the reopen window is unremarkable:

## 3. Where the corpus's own primitives fire, and where they pay

Detectors run on **15min** bars (71,873 bars): `c2_events` (sweep of the prior candle's extreme with a close back inside), `cisd_events` with `level_rule="series_open"`, and `displacement` (body > 2x the trailing 20-bar mean body, direction taken from the body's sign).

**Follow-through measure.** Each event is entered at its own close and scored on a symmetric +/-1R barrier over the next 8 bars (= 2h), with R = trailing ATR(20) shifted one bar so a bar never sizes itself. Target-first scores +1, stop-first -1, neither the mark-to-market close in R. Both barriers inside one bar is scored as the loss. Costs are not modelled: a gold spread is roughly hour-invariant except in the thin hours, so the Asia rows below are flattered by an amount this table cannot see.

## 4. Candidate windows

`time %` is the share of traded 15m bars inside the window — the cost of the gate. `path %` is the share of total minute-bar range travelled, and `range dens` is that divided by `time %`. `extr dens` does the same for daily extremes, `opp dens` for detector events. Above 1.00 means the window is denser than the clock alone would give it. `vs null` is the window's expectancy minus the all-hours expectancy, and `+/-se` is the standard error of the window's own expectancy.

A raw "total R captured" column is deliberately **not** reported: every window's expectancy is negative, so such a column would rank a window highly for capturing more of the *losses*.

### What the table actually says

**1. Density is real; edge is almost entirely noise.** The density columns move by factors — `extr dens` runs from 0.58 (London) to 2.19 (09:30-11:00) — while the expectancy column barely moves at all. With 12 windows tested, a Bonferroni-style threshold is about |t| = 2.9. **Exactly one window clears it, and it clears it in the wrong direction.**

**2. The one significant session effect on gold is negative, and it lands on the corpus's own New York AM kill zone.** `KZ forex NY AM 07:00-10:00` returns -0.057R against the rest of the day, t = -3.11 on 7,835 events — the only result in the table that survives correcting for how many windows were tried. `KZ indices NY AM 08:30-11:00` points the same way (-0.050R, t = -2.17), as does `08:00-12:00` (t = -2.25). Both of the corpus's New York AM windows are, on XAUUSD, **worse than average places to act on one of these primitives** — while being better than average places for the day's extreme to form. Those two facts are not in tension; they are the same fact seen from two sides.

**3. The best-scoring window is also corpus-anchored: London Close, 10:00-12:00.** -0.004R (t = +1.46), 1.49x range density and 1.71x extreme density on only 8.7% of the clock. It is **not statistically significant** — t = 1.46 is nowhere near the 2.9 threshold, and it would not be worth mentioning if it had been found by scanning the grid. It is worth mentioning because it was **pre-specified**: it is a window the corpus states, that outside convention agrees on without dissent, that sits inside the attested 08:30-12:00 quote, and that contains the Silver Bullet sub-window. Four independent reasons to have looked there before seeing the number.

**4. And yet 08:30 is where the day's extreme most often prints** — 5.1% of all extreme observations in that single half hour against 2.2% of the clock (2.3x), the joint highest of the morning (section 1) — inside the hour with the day's worst expectancy (-0.109R). The release spike makes the day's high or low and simultaneously runs anything that follows a primitive into it. That pairing is the corpus's own 'manipulation first' claim showing up as a number, and it is the most decision-relevant line in this document: **the best hour to have a level and the best hour to take an entry are not the same hour.**

**5. The Asian window — the externally contested one — is the emptiest.** `KZ Asia 20:00-00:00` sits at density ~1.0 on every measure (0.99 range, 0.95 extremes) and t = +0.31. On gold it is indistinguishable from picking hours at random. The outside disagreement about its bounds (20:00-00:00 vs 19:00-22:00 vs 20:00-23:00, per `meta/external_crossref.md`) is therefore not worth resolving for this instrument — there is nothing there to get right.

## 6. The 4H opening grid for gold

The corpus gives two grids and says the 09:30 open "doesn't really apply" to forex. Gold is neither. Both grids are built on NY-local time and compared on the behaviour the methodology cares about: a one-sided sweep of the prior 4H candle that closes back inside (`C2 %`), and whether the **next** 4H candle then closes beyond that candle's open (`follow-through %`, the corpus's own delivery test). `inside %` and `sweep-both %` are range-splitting symptoms — a grid that dices a move produces more of both.

| grid | open hours | candles | C2 % | follow-through % | ft n | sweep-both % | expansion % | inside % | mean body/range | straddles the daily break % |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| futures 02/06/10/14 NY | 02/06/10/14/18/22 | 4,703 | 35.3 | 59.4 | 1,661 | 5.8 | 48.6 | 18.7 | 0.452 | 16.5 |
| forex 17/21/01/09 NY | 01/05/09/13/17/21 | 4,707 | 36.2 | 59.6 | 1,706 | 5.6 | 48.2 | 18.0 | 0.443 | 0.0 |

**Verdict for gold: the forex 17/21/01/09 NY grid, weakly.** It shows a 0.1pp higher sweep-then-reverse follow-through (59.6% vs 59.4%) and a 0.9pp higher C2 rate. That difference is **not statistically meaningful**: a two-proportion z on the follow-through rates gives z = 0.08 on 1,706 and 1,661 events. On candle statistics alone the two grids are indistinguishable.

**The tiebreak is structural, and it is one-sided.** The venue's daily break runs 17:00-18:04 NY. The forex grid opens a candle at 17:00, so **0.0%** of its candles are built across the break; the futures grid's 14:00-18:00 bucket straddles it, so **16.5%** of its candles — one in six — mix flow from either side of a one-hour halt. For XAUUSD specifically that argues for the **forex grid**, and it is consistent with the corpus's own reason for offsetting the forex grid in the first place: the 09:30 cash open is not gold's event, the 17:00/18:00 rollover is.

Confidence: **low-to-moderate**. The behavioural metrics do not separate the grids, so the recommendation rests on the break alignment, which is an argument about data hygiene rather than about market structure. Sweep both offsets in any backtest that uses a 4H candle.

### Default

```yaml

  start: "10:00"                 # KZ forex London Close
```

### What the evidence does and does not support

A session gate on gold is justified by **where the tradeable range is**, not by a demonstrated per-trade edge. The strong claims this data will carry: the daily extreme is ~1.7x more likely to print in the New York morning than the clock alone implies, and that result is invariant to the day anchor and to dropping the weekly open and close; minute-bar range in that block is nearly 3x the day's trough. The claim it will **not** carry is that any of this improves a signal's hit rate. Every candidate window's follow-through expectancy sits within a few hundredths of an R of the all-hours null, and the two windows the methodology most favours — both New York AM readings — sit measurably on the wrong side of it.

Read alongside `meta/primitive_base_rates.md`, where 40-44% of candles sweep the prior candle's range on every timeframe, the combined message is unglamorous and worth stating plainly: **the primitives are close to coin flips and the session gate does not fix that.** A strategy that only works inside 08:30-12:00 is not being validated by these numbers; it is being told it has fewer, larger opportunities there. That is still a reason to use the gate — position sizing and cost per unit of range both improve — but it is a different reason from the one the corpus implies.

Limitations, stated rather than buried. This is in-sample across three years of one instrument on one execution timeframe, with no cost model (which flatters the thin hours where the best expectancies happen to sit) and one fixed +/-1R, 2-hour scoring rule — a different horizon or an asymmetric target could reorder the hour table. The event set is three primitives fired unconditionally, not a strategy: a real setup adds HTF context, and it is entirely possible that context interacts with hour in a way an unconditional pass cannot see. The window should be re-fitted, not inherited, if the instrument, the timeframe or the setup changes.

### What this unblocks

`session` is now a parameter with a default, a provenance grade and a stated sweep range rather than an undefined gate, which unblocks the **68 concepts** that cite it in `meta/automation_gaps.md` — including `average-daily-range`, `chart-timezone-est`, `cisd`, `irl-erl-flowchart-model`, `killzones`, `new-york-am-session-only`, `session-cascade`, `daily-profile-session-windows` and the rest of the `time` category's session-boundary problem. It also settles DST for the neighbouring `timezone` term (34 concepts) and answers `po3-four-hour-opening-times` for gold specifically. None of these become *correct* — they become *decidable*, with the fitted value and its evidence grade carried explicitly rather than assumed.
